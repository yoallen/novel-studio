# Welcome to Story Canon

This chapter is the front door. It tells you what Story Canon is, the one idea the whole program rests on, what each main screen is for, how to find your way around the window, and how the rest of this guide is ordered. Read it first, before you open anything else. It takes about ten minutes, and everything after it builds on it.

## What Story Canon is

Story Canon is a program for planning and writing a novel. It holds three things in one place:

- **Your manuscript**, the pages of the book itself.
- **Everything you know about your story**: the people, the places, the clues, the questions you haven't answered yet, and the plan for each scene.
- **How sure you are about each of those things**: what you have settled, what you are still working out, and what is only an idea someone else offered.

Think of it as a notebook that never loses a page and always knows which pages are written in pen and which are still in pencil.

You do not need to have written a novel before. You do not need to plot in advance, either. Some writers plan every scene first; others find the story as they write. Story Canon works for both. Every part of it beyond the page is optional.

## The one big idea: Story Canon remembers, you decide

When you open Story Canon, the first page shows its name, a short line under it (*Where your story takes shape, and nothing gets lost.*), a small flower ornament, and a motto in slanted type:

*Story Canon remembers. You decide.*

That motto is the whole program in two sentences. It says how the work is split between you and Story Canon.

### Story Canon remembers

Every idea you write down, every chat you bring in from planning, every change you make is kept, along with who said it and when. Nothing is quietly thrown away. Even what you delete waits in a place where you can bring it back. Months from now you can ask "where did this come from?" and get an answer.

### You decide

Only you settle anything about your book. The program never turns a maybe into a yes for you. An idea from a friend, from an AI chat such as ChatGPT, or from the writing partner built into Story Canon stays an idea until you choose it, on purpose, with a click.

### Why this matters to your book

Most notebooks treat every line the same. Over months of planning, a line you once wrote as "maybe" starts to feel settled, simply because it has been sitting there so long. Then you build three chapters on something you never actually chose. Story Canon keeps the difference visible, so a maybe always looks like a maybe, and a decision always looks like a decision.

**The common mistake** is treating the program like an ordinary word processor and typing everything as if it were final. You don't have to decide anything to write it down. Writing it in pencil is free, and you can ink it later.

**Example.** You are writing *The Keeper of Gull Point*, a mystery about Marisol Vega, the keeper of a lighthouse on a small island. In March you jot down, "Maybe her brother Eli drowned off the north rocks?" By June you have forgotten that was only a guess. In Story Canon, that line still looks like pencil in June, with its March date beside it. You see at a glance that you never decided it.

The name of the program comes from this idea. **Canon** is a word readers use for what is officially true in a story: if it's canon, it happened. Story Canon keeps track of what is canon in your book, what might become canon, and what never will. The next chapter teaches this word, and the other words the program uses, properly.

## The window: a book you can open

Story Canon is drawn to look like an open book.

- **The purple around the edges** is the book's cloth cover.
- **The cream area in the middle** is the page. Whatever you open appears there.
- **The words *Story Canon*** in slanted type at the top right of the cover, above the list of names, are just the program's name. They are not a button.

Around the page are three things you will use all the time:

- **The list of names** down the right edge of the window, explained next.
- **The header**: the row of buttons across the top of the page, with a thin line under it. It is described in [The header across the top of the page](#the-header-across-the-top-of-the-page).
- **The story strip**: once you have a novel open, a thin map of your whole book drawn on the purple cover above the page, across the top of the window. It uses words you will learn later, so it is explained in [Narrative Zoom](#narrative-zoom).

The first screen you see is different from the rest: it is the **Library**, your shelf of novels. Nothing else appears until you open a novel from that shelf. The chapter [Your first hour: the Library and a new novel](#your-first-hour-the-library-and-a-new-novel) walks you through it.

## The list on the right: every main screen

Down the right edge of the window is a column of names, like the cut-out tabs on the side of a dictionary. Each name opens one **view**: one of the program's main screens. The view you are on turns cream and bold, so you always know where you are.

The first five names are large. These are the main rooms, where you will spend most of your time. Under the slanted word *and* are five smaller names, for things you need now and then.

### The five main views

| View | What it is for |
| --- | --- |
| **Where was I?** | Your novel's home page. Under its name in the list are the words "your novel's home page". It shows the next thing to write (with a **Write** button), then three numbered pages: **Today** (how your writing is going and notes you left for later), **Still open** (questions you have not answered yet and what you decided most recently) and **This week**. Your novel opens here every time you come back to it. |
| **The Writing Room** | Where you write the book. Your chapters and scenes are listed on the left, the page you are writing is in the middle, and the plan for that scene sits on the right. Clicking this name always lands you on a page you can write on: the scene you had open; or, if none is open, the scene Where was I? suggests next; or a new page for the next scene you planned but haven't written; or the scene you worked on most recently; or, in an empty book, a first empty scene. |
| **Story Brain** | Everything you know about your story, with each person, place, clue or question on its own page. This is where you keep track of what is decided and what is still open. |
| **Narrative Zoom** | The shape of your plot, from the whole book down to one scene, like zooming a map. It also holds the checks that catch problems, such as a character who knows something before they could have learned it. |
| **Conversations** | Your talks with the collaborator (the optional AI writing partner built into Story Canon), and any chats you bring in from ChatGPT or Claude. |

**Still open, the page, and Still open, the status.** The next chapter teaches six words for how sure you are about something, and one of them is also *Still open*. The page **Still open** on Where was I? is a place: it gathers what you are carrying in your head, such as your open questions and your latest decisions. The status **Still open** is a label on one thing you wrote, saying "this matters, and I haven't answered it yet".

### The five smaller views

| View | What it is for |
| --- | --- |
| **Search** | Look through everything at once: your pages, everything in the Story Brain, every conversation and every document you brought in. |
| **Story Trail** | The history of every change to your story, newest first, with **Undo** beside each one. From here, **Deleted things** opens the place where anything you deleted waits to be restored. |
| **Import and export** | Bringing files in (a manuscript, a planning document, a spreadsheet, an editor's changes) and sending your book out (as a Word file, a PDF, an ebook and more). |
| **Settings** | Choices for this novel and for all your novels, including **Novel setup** (what kind of book this is and which methods you use), **About you**, the collaborator, goals, your other computers and backups. |
| **Help** | This guide, inside the program. |

### Pages that light up another name

A few pages are opened from inside another view. While you are on one of them, the name it belongs to stays lit in the list, so you still know where you are:

| You are on | What it is | The name that lights up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Novel setup** | Where you say what kind of book this is | **Settings** |
| **My methods** | Where you keep your own ways of building a story, so any of your novels can use them | **Settings** |
| **Deleted** (opened with **Deleted things**) | Everything you deleted, waiting to be restored | **Story Trail** |
| **Drafts** | Whole copies of the book, kept each time you finish a draft | **The Writing Room** |
| **Writing Trail** | The list of times you left the page while writing | **The Writing Room** |

Each is taught in its own chapter.

## Getting around Story Canon

### The header across the top of the page

Once a novel is open, a row of buttons runs across the top of the page, with a thin line under it. This guide calls it the **header**. From left to right:

1. **Return to Writing**, a pink bookmark ribbon. It appears once you have been writing, and takes you straight back to the scene you were in. See [The Return to Writing ribbon](#the-return-to-writing-ribbon) below.
2. **Library.** Takes you back to the shelf of all your novels.
3. **The book switcher**: your novel's title in slanted letters with a small ▾ after it. Click it for a list of every novel on your shelf, each with its stage (what you are doing with it now, such as *First draft* or *Revising*) in gray. Click another title to open that novel right where you left it, without going through the Library. Move the mouse off the list to close it.
4. **The name of the view you are on**, in slanted purple, such as *Story Brain* or *Novel setup*. It is a label, not a button. It is hidden in the Writing Room, where the page itself is enough.
5. **Saved**, in the Writing Room only: a small circle and a word that tell you whether your typing is safe. There is no Save button for your writing. It saves by itself as you type.
6. **Capture**, a button for catching a passing thought without leaving what you are doing. The next chapter explains it.

**The Saved indicator, word by word:**

| You see | It means |
| --- | --- |
| A filled circle and **Saved** | Everything you typed is kept. |
| **Not saved yet** | You typed in the last moment; it will save within about a second of a pause. |
| A red message | Something went wrong saving. Your typing is still on screen. Don't close the program; the message says what to do. |

### The Return to Writing ribbon

When you leave the page to look something up, a pink bookmark ribbon hangs from the top left of the page, at the start of the header, with the words **Return to Writing**. Click it, or press **Ctrl+Shift+W**, and you are back in the scene you were writing.

- **What it is:** a bookmark that always points at the scene you were last writing in.
- **When it appears:** once you have been on the page in this sitting, or once the last scene you opened has words in it. It hides while you are in the Writing Room, because you are already there.
- **Why it matters:** writing gets interrupted all the time. You check a name, glance at the plan, answer a question. The ribbon means a detour never costs you your place.
- **What it remembers:** each time you leave the page while writing, Story Canon notes where you went. It calls each of these trips a **detour**. Coming back with the ribbon closes them. Detours are taught in [Writing Room: detours and Return to Writing](#writing-room-detours-and-return-to-writing).
- **Do most writers need it?** Yes. It is the simplest habit in the program: when you have looked up what you needed, click the pink ribbon.

**The common mistake** is clicking **The Writing Room** in the list on the right to get back. That works too, but the ribbon is one click from anywhere, and it also closes your detours.

**Example.** You are writing "The empty boat", the scene where Marisol finds a boat with nobody in it on her rocks, and you can't remember what the harbor town is called. You click **Story Brain**, find the town (Port Ellery), then click the pink **Return to Writing** ribbon. You are back in the scene, at the sentence you left.

### Switching novels keeps your place

Each novel keeps its own place. When you switch to another novel with the book switcher, or open it from the Library, it opens where you left it. A view that had something open in one novel never shows the other novel's things by mistake.

### Making everything bigger or smaller

If the words are too small to read comfortably, hold **Ctrl** and press **+** to make everything bigger, **Ctrl** and **-** to make it smaller, and **Ctrl** and **0** to go back to normal size.
