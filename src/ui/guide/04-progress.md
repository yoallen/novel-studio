# Goals, November and catching up

This chapter is about the number side of writing: daily goals, a **writing push** such as November and 50,000 words, how Story Canon works out what each day needs, what happens on the days you fall short, and how to plan a lighter day or a rest day without feeling you have failed.

All of it is optional. With no goal at all, Story Canon still shows how many words you wrote each day, and never judges a day.

## Where your progress shows

Your progress appears in three places:

- **Where was I**, just below the stage row: the drafting progress panel, with a column for each day and the figures under it. This is the main one, and most of this chapter is about it.
- **The Writing Room**, in the small line under the page: *1,204 words in this scene, today's goal 1,743*.
- **The Writing Room binder**, at the top: the whole book's word count, and "of" your target length if you set one.

## What counts as a day's words

Every number in this chapter starts from one idea: **a day's words are the words you typed that day, minus the words you cut.**

- Typing new words adds to the day.
- Cutting words takes away from it. A day of heavy cutting can come out below zero; point at its column and it says how many words were cut.
- **Notes for later**, `[[like this]]`, never count.
- **Not writing, so never counted:** bringing in a manuscript, accepting changes an editor sent back, restoring an earlier version of a scene, and choosing **Keep the saved version** after a crash. The words are in your book (they count in **The whole book**), but none of them is you writing that day.
- **Days follow your computer's clock and time zone.** Words typed at 11:58 at night count for that day; words at 12:02 count for the next.
- **Words count when they are saved as a version**: about twenty seconds after you stop typing, or when you leave the scene. If **Today** looks behind, wait a moment.

**Why it matters.** A tracker you can't trust is worse than none. Counting only what you typed means the columns are honest: a day with 3,000 imported words and 200 written ones shows 200.

## Everyday goals: a number for ordinary days

**What it is.** An **everyday goal** is the daily target that applies on any day that isn't part of a writing push. Most writers who use one choose a modest number of words per day, such as 500 or 1,000.

**Where it is.** **Settings**, in the section **Guidance and goals**, the dropdown **Everyday goal**.

**How to set one.**

1. Click **Settings** on the right edge of the window.
2. In **Guidance and goals**, open the **Everyday goal** dropdown and choose **Words per day**.
3. In **Goal amount**, type your number, such as 500.
4. Click **Save settings** at the bottom of the window.

Where was I now measures every day this month against 500: today's line says *Today, 74 words of your 500.*, each day's column gets a thin dashed line at the goal's height, and the columns turn black on days you reach it.

**The choices in the dropdown:**

| Choice | What it does now |
| --- | --- |
| **No goals** | No daily number. Where was I still shows what you wrote each day |
| **Words per day** | A number of words each day. Where was I tracks it every day you write |
| **Scenes per week** | Kept as your goal in Settings. Where was I counts words for now, so the columns show no goal line |
| **Minutes per day** | Kept as your goal in Settings. Where was I counts words for now |
| **Milestones** | No daily number. A box, **My milestones**, holds the milestones you are working toward, one per line, such as *Act One drafted by March*. They are kept in Settings for you to read and tick off yourself |

**When to use it.** When you want a steady habit across ordinary months. If you only want a number for one stretch, such as November, use a writing push instead and leave the everyday goal at **No goals**.

**The common mistake** is setting the everyday goal to your best-day number. A goal you meet four days out of five builds a habit; one you miss four days out of five builds dread. Set the number you can reach on a tired Tuesday.

**Example.** A lighthouse keeper's novel moves slowly through the autumn. The author sets **Words per day** to 300. Most days she writes 400 or 500, and the columns are black. On the day she writes 150, the column is grey and striped, and the next day's goal is still just 300. Everyday goals don't carry a shortfall forward; only a writing push does that.

## Reading the drafting progress columns

The panel on Where was I starts with a heading in slanted purple type: the month's name, or your push's name. Under it is a sentence about today, such as *Today, 600 words of your 1,743.* Then come the columns: one thin column per day of the month (or of the push), with the day numbers underneath.

### The marks on each column

The columns use the same marks as the rest of Story Canon: ink for done, pencil for in progress, a blank for nothing yet.

| What you see | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| A solid black column | **Ink.** You met that day's goal. With no goal set, any day with words is black |
| A grey, striped column | **Pencil.** You wrote that day, but less than its goal |
| No column, just the solid base line | **Blank.** No words that day |
| A dotted grey base line | A day still ahead |
| A dotted pink base line | A rest day you set, still ahead or with no words |
| A thick pink base line, and the day's number in bold pink | **Today** |
| A thin dashed line across a column | That day's goal, at its height |
| A solid pink line across a column | The goal of a day you set yourself (a lighter, rest or bigger day) |

The day numbers under the columns show only the first day, today, and every fifth day, to keep the row readable. The height of each column is in proportion to the biggest day shown, so a short column next to a tall one is a real difference.

### Pointing at a column

Point at any column to see its exact story, for example:

- *November 3: 900 words of 1,661*
- *November 8: 2,400 words, goal met*
- *November 13 (today): 600 words of 1,743*
- *November 26, still ahead, a day you set at 300*

### The figures under the columns

| Figure | What it says |
| --- | --- |
| **So far** (or **October so far**, without a push) | Words written in this push or this month, and "of" the push's goal |
| **To finish on time** | During a push: about how many words a day you need from here, and how many days are left (today included) |
| **Against the plan** | During a push: how far ahead or behind you are, counted to the end of yesterday |
| **Days in a row** | How many days in a row you have written |
| **The whole book** | Every word in the manuscript, and "of" your target length if you set one |

Each is explained in full below. Under the figures, during a push, are **Set a lighter day** and the door-closing link.

## A writing push: November and 50,000 words

**What it is.** A **writing push** is a stretch of days with a word goal: the classic is November and 50,000 words, the challenge many writers take on each year. You can make a push of any length, such as a two-week retreat or a spring quarter.

**Why it helps.** A push turns a big number into a small daily one, and then keeps that daily number honest as the days go by: if you fall short, it tells you exactly what each remaining day needs, and if you get ahead, it lets you ease off.

### Setting up a November push

1. Click **Settings** on the right edge of the window.
2. Find **A writing push** and click **Plan a writing push**. Story Canon fills in November: **Name it** *November*, **First day** November 1, **Last day** November 30, **Words to write in it** 50,000.
3. Change anything you like.
4. Read the line under the boxes: *That is about 1,667 words a day for 30 days.* That is your daily goal on the push's days only. The rest of the time, your everyday goal applies.
5. Click **Save settings** at the bottom of the window.

To remove the push, click **No push** in the same section, then **Save settings**.

### Before November starts

Where was I starts showing the push **two weeks before its first day**, so you can see it coming. The heading says **November**, and the panel says *November starts on November 1. 50,000 words in 30 days is about 1,667 a day.* Under it: *Today, 74 words. The chart below fills in once November begins.* Today's words still count toward your everyday goal, if you have one.

This is a good time to set any lighter or rest days you already know about (see [Lighter days and rest days](#lighter-days-and-rest-days)) and to decide whether to close the door for the month (see [Closing the door from the progress panel](#closing-the-door-from-the-progress-panel)).

### During November

From November 1, the columns are November's thirty days, and every figure is about the push. The sentence at the top changes with the day:

- *Today, 600 words of your 1,743.* while you are working toward today's goal.
- *Today, 1,800 words of your 1,743. The day's goal is met.* once you reach it. **To finish on time** then switches to what the days after today need.
- *Today is a rest day you set.* on a rest day, with *You wrote 412 words anyway, and they all count.* if you did.

### After November ends

For a week after the last day, the panel says *November is done: 51,204 words of 50,000.* with the month's columns still showing. After that week, it goes back to showing the current month with your everyday goal.

## Catch-up math: how a short day is shared

**What it is.** During a push, each day's goal is **an even share of what is still left**. Story Canon takes the words still to write, subtracts any days you have set yourself, and divides the rest evenly over the days that remain, today included.

**What that means in practice:**

- **A short day spreads its difference over the rest of the month.** Each later day asks a few words more, not one day asking for a mountain.
- **A big day lowers every later day a little.** Getting ahead buys you slack.
- **Today's goal is worked out from what you wrote before today**, so it never moves while you are writing. Today's words only change tomorrow's goal.

**Worked example.** November, 50,000 words, so the month starts at 1,667 a day.

| Day | Written | That day's goal | What happened next |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| November 1 | 1,800 | 1,667 | A bit ahead |
| November 2 | 1,700 | 1,663 | Still ahead |
| November 3 | 900 | 1,661 | A short day: 761 words under |
| November 4 | 2,100 | 1,689 | The 761 words were shared over the 27 days left, so each asked about 28 more |
| November 6 | 0 | 1,670 | A blank day |
| November 7 | 1,500 | 1,740 | November 6's 1,670 words, shared over 24 days, added about 70 to each |

By the morning of November 13, you have written 18,630 words in twelve days. 31,370 are left, over 18 days counting today, so the panel says *Today, 0 words of your 1,743.* and **To finish on time** says *about 1,743 a day, 18 days left*.

**The common mistake** is trying to make up a short day the very next day. The math already spreads it out. Write today's number, not yesterday's and today's.

## Lighter days and rest days

November has days when 1,667 words won't happen: a late shift, a birthday, Thanksgiving. Story Canon lets you plan for them ahead of time, so they are part of the plan instead of a hole in it.

**What it is.** A **set day** is a day in your push whose goal you choose yourself. A **lighter day** has a smaller number, such as 300. A **rest day** has zero. You can also set a **bigger day**, such as 3,000 for a free Saturday. On a set day, your number is that day's goal, and meeting it counts as meeting the day.

**Where it is.** On Where was I, under the progress figures, the link **Set a lighter day**. It only appears during a push that has a word goal (and before it starts).

**How to set a day.**

1. Click **Set a lighter day**. A row opens with **Day**, **Words**, **Set it**, **Make it a rest day** and **Cancel**, and a grey line: *That day's goal becomes your number, and the days you have not set share the difference. Nothing is held against you.*
2. In **Day**, choose the date. You can choose any day from today (or from the push's first day, before it starts) to its last day.
3. Either type the words in **Words** (such as 300) and click **Set it**, or click **Make it a rest day**.

The day you set now appears in a list under the figures, such as *November 26: 300 words* or *November 27: rest day*, each with a **Clear** link that puts it back to an ordinary day. Its column shows a solid pink goal line (a lighter or bigger day) or a dotted pink base line (a rest day). **To finish on time** adds *counting the days you set*.

**What happens to the difference.** The days you have not set share it. In the example above, on November 13 you set November 26 to 300 and November 27 as a rest day. Those two days now carry 300 words instead of about 3,486, so the other 16 days share the rest: today's goal rises from 1,743 to 1,942.

**Why plan ahead.** Spread over sixteen days, the two lighter days cost about 200 words each. Left until the day itself, they would arrive as a sudden gap at the end of the month.

**The common mistake** is waiting until the bad day to admit it. If you know Thanksgiving will be a 300-word day, set it now. The other days absorb it a little at a time.

**Example.** A writer's daughter has a recital on November 14 and the family is visiting on November 22 and 23. On November 1 she sets the 14th to 500, and the 22nd and 23rd as rest days. Her daily goal for the other days rises from 1,667 to 1,834, and those three days show pink goal lines in the columns. On the 22nd, the panel says *Today is a rest day you set.*, and her **Days in a row** stays unbroken.

### When set days leave words with nowhere to go

If you set so many days so low that the remaining words have no day left to be written in, **To finish on time** says so plainly: *Your set days leave 2,400 words with no day to write them. Raise a set day or clear one.* Click **Clear** beside a set day, or set it again with a bigger number.

If your set days already cover everything left, it says *Your set days cover what is left.* and an unset day's goal becomes zero: *Your set days cover what is left, so today is yours to spend.*

## Against the plan: ahead or behind

**What it is.** **Against the plan** compares what you have written with what an even pace would have given you, **up to the end of yesterday**. It says *1,374 behind, spread over the days ahead* or *620 ahead*.

**How it is counted.** The plan as it stood on the first day: the push's goal divided evenly over its days (1,667 a day for November). A past day you set counts as your number, so a rest day is never held against you; what it left undone is shared by the other days, so a real gap still shows. Days you set ahead of time never change this figure.

**When it shows.** Only during a push, only once you are 50 words or more ahead or behind, and not once the goal is reached.

**In the example:** twelve days at 1,667 is 20,004 words; you wrote 18,630; so *1,374 behind*. Today isn't counted yet, because today isn't over.

**How to use it.** As information, not a verdict. *Behind* doesn't mean you need one big day; the math in **To finish on time** has already spread it over the days ahead. *Ahead* means you have earned a lighter day, if you want one.

## Days in a row

**What it is.** How many days in a row, up to today, you have written at least one word.

- **Today is still open.** If you haven't written yet today, the count runs up to yesterday, and doesn't drop to zero until today ends with no words.
- **A rest day you set never breaks the run.** It doesn't add a day either; it just doesn't count against you.
- When there is no run, it says *Starts with your next words*.

**Why it helps.** A run of days is one of the most reliable ways to finish a draft. The number is there to protect the habit, which is why a planned rest day doesn't cost you the run.

**The common mistake** is writing ten words at 11:55 at night to keep the run alive, every night. If that's what keeps you coming back, fine. If it starts to feel like a chore, set a rest day instead.

## The whole book total and your target length

**The whole book** is every word in your manuscript right now, in every scene that isn't deleted. Unlike the day columns, it includes words you brought in, restored or accepted from an editor. Notes for later aren't counted.

To see it as "of" a target, such as *52,310 of 80,000 words*:

1. Open **Settings**.
2. In the first section, type a number in **Target length in words (optional)**.
3. Click **Save settings**.

The binder in the Writing Room shows the same total and target at its top.

**Why both numbers.** The push measures your writing days; the whole book measures the book. In November, the first matters more. In March, when you are deciding whether the book is too long, the second does.

## Closing the door from the progress panel

**Door closed** stops Story Canon from judging your draft until a day you choose: no red spelling underlines, no echoes, watch words or long sentences in Read it back, no story review or misspelled names in Narrative Zoom, and the collaborator stays quiet about problems unless you ask. Your plans, notes, search and reading aloud all stay. (See also [Door closed while you draft](#door-closed-while-you-draft).)

**Why pair it with a push.** A push is about getting words down. Judging them is a different kind of attention, and it pulls you into fixing. Closing the door for the push keeps the two apart.

**How to close it from Where was I.**

1. Under your push's figures, find the link **Close the door through November 30** (it always names your push's last day).
2. Click it.

The link is replaced by *The door is closed through November 30. Nothing judges the draft until then.* It takes effect at once; there is nothing to save.

**To open it early, or choose another day,** open **Settings** and find **Door closed**. It says *The door is closed through November 30.* with **Change the day** (choose a date, then **Save**) and **Open the door now**. These take effect at once too, without **Save settings**. The door opens by itself the day after the date you chose.

Anything that waits while the door is closed shows a **Look anyway** link, so you can still check something on purpose.

## A November, day by day

Here is how the pieces fit together over one month.

**October 18.** Delia, a first-time novelist, opens **Settings**, clicks **Plan a writing push**, keeps November and 50,000, and saves. On Where was I, a **November** panel appears: *November starts on November 1. 50,000 words in 30 days is about 1,667 a day.* She knows Thanksgiving week will be hard, so she clicks **Set a lighter day** and sets November 26 to 300 and November 27 as a rest day. She clicks **Close the door through November 30**.

**November 1.** She opens the book, reads **Next** and **Where you stopped**, and clicks **Write**. She does two 30-minute sprints and writes 1,800 words. The column is black.

**November 6.** Her son is sick. She writes nothing. The column is blank. She doesn't panic: tomorrow's goal is about 70 words higher, that's all.

**November 13.** The panel says she's 1,374 behind. She reads **To finish on time** instead, *about 1,942 a day*, and writes that. **Days in a row** says 7.

**November 27.** Rest day. The panel says *Today is a rest day you set.* She reads a book instead.

**November 30.** She writes 2,300 words and the panel says **Done. Everything now is extra.** under **To finish on time**. For a week, the panel shows *November is done*, with thirty columns of her month. On December 1 the door opens by itself, and Read it back's echoes and long sentences are waiting for revision.

## When the numbers look wrong but are working

| What you see | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Today** is behind what the page says | Words count when they are saved as a version, about twenty seconds after you stop typing. Pause or leave the scene |
| Today's goal didn't drop while you wrote | Today's goal is fixed from what you wrote before today. Today's words change tomorrow's goal |
| The goal changed from yesterday | That is catch-up math: a short day raises the days after it a little, a big day lowers them |
| A day with lots of new words shows few | Brought-in, restored or accepted words aren't counted as writing. Cutting words subtracts |
| The panel shows October, though you set up November | The push appears two weeks before it starts. Before that, you see the current month |
| The November panel disappeared in December | It shows for a week after the push ends, then returns to the current month |
| **Against the plan** isn't there | It only shows during a push, when you are 50 or more words ahead or behind |
| **Set a lighter day** isn't there | It only appears with a writing push that has a word goal, until the push ends |
| Days in a row didn't reset after a missed day | The missed day was a rest day you set, or today isn't over yet |
| Scenes per week or Minutes per day shows no goal line | Those goals are kept in Settings, but Where was I measures words for now |
