# The Writing Room

**The Writing Room** is the view where you write your book. Everything else in Story Canon exists to serve what happens here: words going onto the page. This chapter walks through the room in the order you meet it on screen: how you get in, the page and how it saves, the list of chapters and scenes on the left, the plan for each scene on the right, and the links above the page for the moments when writing gets hard. Read it before your first writing session, and come back to it whenever something in the room puzzles you. Daily word goals, sprints and November have their own chapter, [Goals, sprints and November](#goals-sprints-and-november).

## Writing Room: getting in

The Writing Room is the second tab on the right edge of the window, under **Where was I?** (your novel's home page, described in [Where was I? Your novel's home page](#where-was-i-your-novels-home-page)). There are four ways in, and each lands you in a slightly different place.

| You click | Where you land |
| --- | --- |
| **The Writing Room** tab | The scene you had open. If none, the scene Where was I? suggests under **Next**. If Next is your next planned scene card and it has no page yet, Story Canon makes its page first, just as **Write** does (see [Writing Room: planned, not written yet](#writing-room-planned-not-written-yet)). If there is nothing to suggest, the scene you worked on most recently. If the book has no scenes at all, a new empty scene (and a first chapter to hold it) |
| **Write** on Where was I? | The scene named in **Next**. If that is a planned scene with no page yet, Story Canon makes its page first |
| **Return to Writing**, the pink ribbon at the top left (or **Ctrl+Shift+W**) | The scene you were writing in, with the cursor exactly where you left it (see [Writing Room: detours and Return to Writing](#writing-room-detours-and-return-to-writing)) |
| A scene in the list on the left, a note in **Left for later**, or **Open the page** in Narrative Zoom | That scene |

When a scene opens, the page shows *Opening the page…* for a moment, then puts your cursor back where it was the last time you were in that scene. Wait for the words to appear before you type, so nothing you type lands before the page is ready.

## Writing Room: the room at a glance

The room has three columns and a row of links above the page.

| Where | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Left: the binder** | Your chapters and scenes in book order, with word counts and a small circle showing how far along each scene is. Below them, **Planned, not written yet** |
| **Middle: the page** | The open scene: its title, your text, and a small line underneath with the scene's word count |
| **Right: the side panel** | Usually the **Scene plan** for the open scene. It can show **Read it back** instead |
| **Above the page: the links** | **Hide scene plan** (or **Show scene plan**), **Read it back**, **Show in Narrative Zoom**, **I'm stuck**, **Ask about this scene**, **Writing Trail**, **Sprint** and **Focus** |
| **Top of the window** | The **Saved** indicator, beside the **Capture** button |

If no scene is open, the page says *Choose a scene on the left, or add one, and start writing.*

## Writing Room: the page

The page is the middle of the room, and the only part you truly need.

**The title.** The scene's name sits at the top of the page in purple. To change it, click it, type, then press **Enter** or click anywhere else. If you empty it, the old name comes back: a scene always has a name.

**The text.** Click under the title and type. A brand-new scene shows the gray words *Write anything. A sentence counts.* until you start. **Ctrl+B** makes words bold, **Ctrl+I** makes them italic, **Ctrl+Z** undoes your last bit of typing and **Ctrl+Y** redoes it.

**The word line.** Under the text is a small gray line, for example *63 words in this scene, today's goal 1,743*. Today's goal appears only once you have set a daily goal or a writing push (see [Goals, sprints and November](#goals-sprints-and-november)). On the right of the line is a reminder, *Ctrl+Shift+L leaves a note for later*, which [Writing Room: notes for later](#writing-room-notes-for-later) explains. (The reminder is hidden while the door is closed; see [Goals: door closed](#goals-door-closed).) Notes for later are never counted in the scene's words.

**If the scene is kept for you.** When a scene is marked in **Kept for you to write or decide** (see [Writing Room: kept for you to write or decide](#writing-room-kept-for-you-to-write-or-decide)), the marker, such as *Mine to write*, appears just above the title, so you see it every time you open the page.

**Example.** You open the Writing Room for the first time on *The Keeper of Gull Point*. The book has no scenes yet, so Story Canon makes Chapter 1 with one empty scene, *Scene 1*, and opens it. You click the title, type *The empty boat*, press **Enter**, and start: *The boat came in on the morning tide with nobody in it.* The word line says *11 words in this scene*.

## Writing Room: how saving works

There is no Save button for your writing, and you never need one. Here is exactly what happens, so you can trust it.

### Writing Room: the Saved indicator

At the top right of the window, beside **Capture**, is a small dot and a word. It appears only while you are in the Writing Room.

| What it says | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| A filled dot and **Saved** | Everything you have typed is safe on this computer |
| A hollow dot and **Not saved yet** | You are typing. It changes to Saved within about a second of a pause |
| Red **Not saved** | Something went wrong saving. Keep the window open and keep typing; Story Canon tries again with every pause |
| Red **Not saved: service unreachable** | The part of Story Canon that stores your work is not answering. Keep the window open, and do not close it until it says Saved |

### Writing Room: the two layers of saving

Saving happens in two layers.

1. **Within a second of every pause**, your typing is written to a safety copy on this computer, and the indicator says **Saved**. Even a power cut now loses nothing.
2. **After about twenty seconds without typing, when you leave the scene, or when you close the window**, that typing becomes a **saved version** of the scene: a permanent step in its history, listed under **Earlier versions** (see [Writing Room: earlier versions of a scene](#writing-room-earlier-versions-of-a-scene)).

**Looks wrong, but isn't.** A few things wait for the saved version, not the safety copy, so they can lag behind your typing by up to twenty seconds:

- **Today's words** on Where was I?, and the shape of your book there.
- **This week**, page 3 of Where was I?.
- **The scene's status** moving from a not-started step to Drafting (see [Writing Room: a scene's status](#writing-room-a-scenes-status)).
- **Left for later**, for a note you just typed.

If a number seems stuck, pause for twenty seconds or click another scene, and it catches up. Your words were safe the whole time. What to do after a crash, or when the same scene is open in two windows, is at the end of this chapter, in [Writing Room: when the program closed or two windows disagree](#writing-room-when-the-program-closed-or-two-windows-disagree).

## Writing Room: the binder of chapters and scenes

The **binder** is the left column. Like a ring binder on a desk, it holds your manuscript in order: each chapter, with its scenes under it. Click a scene to open it. The open scene is marked with a purple bar down its left side.

**Why it matters.** The binder is your map of the written book. You see at a glance how many scenes each chapter has, how long they are, and how far along each one is.

### Writing Room: word counts in the binder

At the top of the binder is the whole book's word count, and "of" your target length if you set one in Settings (for example *12,480 words of 80,000*). Each chapter shows its total on the right, and each scene its own count. The open scene's count changes as you type.

### Writing Room: adding chapters and scenes

1. **Add scene**, under each chapter, adds a new scene at the end of that chapter, named *Scene 2*, *Scene 3* and so on, and opens it.
2. **Add chapter**, the button at the bottom of the chapters, adds a new chapter at the end of the book with one empty scene in it, and opens that scene.

If a scene you want to write is already planned in Narrative Zoom, do not use **Add scene** for it. Use **Write it** instead, as [Writing Room: planned, not written yet](#writing-room-planned-not-written-yet) explains.

### Writing Room: renaming chapters and scenes

- **A chapter:** double-click its name in the binder, type the new name, and press **Enter** (or **Esc** to cancel).
- **A scene:** click its title at the top of the page, type, and press **Enter**.

### Writing Room: the status circles

Before each scene's name is a small circle. It shows how far along the scene is, using the scene's **status**: the step it has reached on its way from idea to finished. (A scene's status is not the same thing as the status of a fact, such as Decided or Developing. It is a separate list of steps that belongs to scenes only. You choose it in the scene plan, described in [Writing Room: a scene's status](#writing-room-a-scenes-status).)

| Circle | Meaning | Statuses that give it, with the usual steps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Empty | Not started | Idea, Planned, Ready to draft |
| Half filled | Being written | Drafting |
| Filled | Written | First draft complete, Revising, Revised, Finalized |

Point at a circle to see the scene's status in words, for example *Drafting: being written*.

### Writing Room: moving a scene

The open scene has three links under its name: **↑ Up**, **↓ Down** and **Delete**.

1. Click the scene you want to move, so it is the open one.
2. Click **↑ Up** or **↓ Down** to move it one place. At the top or bottom of a chapter, it moves into the chapter before or after.

Chapters themselves can't be moved in the binder.

### Writing Room: deleting a scene or a chapter

- **Delete**, under the open scene, removes that scene. Story Canon asks first: *Move "the scene" to Deleted? You can restore it from Story Trail, under Deleted things.*
- **Delete chapter**, under each chapter, removes the chapter and every scene in it, and asks the same way, naming how many scenes go with it.

Nothing deleted is really gone. **Story Trail** is the view (in the lower group of tabs on the right) that keeps the history of every change in your book. Open it, click **Deleted things**, and put the scene or chapter back, whole. See [Finding things: Search and Story Trail](#finding-things-search-and-story-trail).

### Writing Room: parts, for grouping chapters

**What it is.** A **part** is a group of chapters with its own heading, such as *Part One: The Tide*.

**Do you need it?** Most novels never use parts. Use them when your book has big movements, separated in time or place.

**How to start one.**

1. Under the chapter where the part should begin, click **Start a part here**.
2. Type the part's name, and press **Enter**.

The part's name appears in slanted purple type above that chapter. The part runs from that chapter until the next part heading.

**To rename it,** double-click the part's name. **To remove it,** click **Remove this part heading**, which replaces **Start a part here** under the chapter that starts a part. The chapters from there on are no longer a separate part. No chapters or words are removed.

**Example.** Your book has two halves: the weeks before the empty boat is explained, and the storm after. Under Chapter 1 you click **Start a part here** and type *Part One: The Boat*. Under Chapter 14 you start *Part Two: The Storm*.

## Writing Room: planned, not written yet

**What it is.** Below the chapters in the binder is a section headed **Planned, not written yet**. It lists the scene cards in your plan (made in Narrative Zoom, the view where you plan the book's shape on beats) that don't have a page in the manuscript yet, in story order. Each shows the card's name and, in slanted type, the beat it sits on, or *might go at* a beat if it isn't settled there yet. When every card has a page, the section does not appear.

**Why it matters.** Your plan and your manuscript are two views of the same book. This list is the bridge between them: it shows what you planned and haven't written.

**Do you need it?** Only if you plan scenes before writing them. If you write first and plan later, this section stays empty, and that is fine.

**How to use it.**

1. Find the card in **Planned, not written yet**.
2. Click **Write it** beside it.

Story Canon makes the scene's page, named for the card, and opens it. It goes where the plan says: right after the written scene of the nearest card before it in story order, or at the very start of the first chapter if nothing before it is written yet. The card leaves this list, and its plan comes with it: you will see it in the scene plan under **From the card**.

**The common mistake** is clicking **Add scene** and retyping a planned scene's name. That makes a second, unconnected scene, and the card stays in this list as if unwritten. Use **Write it**, so the page and the card stay one scene. If you already made the mistake, [Writing Room: joining a written scene to its card](#writing-room-joining-a-written-scene-to-its-card) fixes it.

**Example.** You planned twenty scene cards for *The Keeper of Gull Point* and have written none. The Writing Room shows all twenty under Planned, not written yet. You click **Write it** beside *Ana's first house call*. A page called *Ana's first house call* appears in Chapter 1, with the card's plan beside it, and you start typing.

## Writing Room: the scene plan

The right column is the **Scene plan**: your plan for the open scene, beside the page, so you can glance at what the scene must do without leaving it. A gray line under the heading says *Everything here saves by itself when you click away. Earlier versions are kept.* When you change something and click elsewhere, a small green **Saved** appears beside its label for a moment.

The scene plan and the scene card in Narrative Zoom are the same plan, seen from two places. What you type in one shows in the other. The scene plan is described below page by page, in the order you see it.

**Do you need it?** No. You can write any scene with an empty plan. Many writers ignore the panel while drafting and fill it in when a scene stalls.

### Writing Room: showing and hiding the scene plan

**Hide scene plan**, the first link above the page, gives the page the whole width. It changes to **Show scene plan** to bring it back. Story Canon remembers your choice the next time you open the room.

### Writing Room: the scene plan's three pages

The scene plan holds more than fits beside a page, so it is split into three small numbered tabs under its heading, like the pages of a record (see [The numbered pages and All pages](#the-numbered-pages-and-all-pages)), with **All pages** at the end of the row. One page shows at a time.

| Page | What is on it |
| --- | --- |
| **1 The plan** | **Is this scene on your plan?** (when it applies), **From the card**, the plan's core fields, and **The turn, and around it** |
| **2 About it** | **Status** and the circle key, **Viewpoint character**, **Kept for you to write or decide**, **This draft is settled**, **Who is here** (when it applies), and **Drafting readiness** |
| **3 Notes** | **Left for later** in this scene (when it has any), **Attached to this scene**, **Scene notes**, and **Earlier versions** |

**How to use them.** Click a tab to turn to that page, or use the links at the bottom of each page, such as **Page 2: About it**. Click **All pages** to see all three, one after another. A page number turns ink blue when that page has something on it: **1 The plan** once the scene has a card or anything typed in its plan, **3 Notes** once something is attached to the scene or its scene notes have something in them. **2 About it** is always blue, because a scene always has a status.

Story Canon remembers which page of the scene plan you were on while the program is open, and keeps that page as you move from scene to scene. So if you are working through the status of every scene in a chapter, the plan stays on **2 About it** as you click each one.

**The common mistake** is thinking the status or the scene notes have gone, because the plan opens on another page. Look at the tabs. Status is on **2 About it**; notes and earlier versions are on **3 Notes**.

**Example.** You are drafting *The empty boat* and want the plan's job line beside you, so you keep the scene plan on **1 The plan**. When the draft is done, you click **2 About it** and set the status to **First draft complete**.

### Writing Room: scene plan page 1, The plan

The first page holds the plan itself: what the scene must do and how it turns.

### Writing Room: joining a written scene to its card

**What it is.** A section headed **Is this scene on your plan?** appears when the open scene is not yet on a beat and your plan has scene cards with no page. It lets you join this written scene to the card that plans it.

**Why it matters.** If you wrote a scene before planning it, or used **Add scene** for a scene you had already planned, the page and the card are two separate things. Story Canon can't show the page on your plan, and the card keeps sitting in Planned, not written yet. Joining them makes them one scene again.

**How to join them.**

1. Open the written scene.
2. In **Is this scene on your plan?**, open the dropdown **Choose its scene card…** and pick the card. Each card shows its beat in brackets, if it has one.
3. Click **Join them**.

The scene takes the card's place in the plan, the card's planning shows under **From the card**, and the card leaves Planned, not written yet.

**The common mistake** is filling in the scene plan of a scene first and joining it afterward. The lines you typed belong to the scene's own little plan, which stays behind as a separate card and then shows up under Planned, not written yet. Nothing is lost, but you have two plans to tidy. Join first, then plan.

**Example.** You wrote *The boat* with **Add scene** before you remembered you had planned it as the card *The empty boat* on the Catalyst beat. In the scene plan you choose *The empty boat (Catalyst)* and click **Join them**. The card's plan appears beside the page, and the card leaves Planned, not written yet.

### Writing Room: From the card

If the scene has a card in your plan with something on it, **From the card** shows it:

- **Beat:** the beat the card sits on, *Might go at* one or more beats, or *Not on a beat yet*.
- **Every other field** filled in on the card that is not already among the plan's own fields, further down this page.
- **Open the full card** opens the card in Narrative Zoom, where you can change any of it.

### Writing Room: the plan's fields

Under **From the card** (or at the top of the page, when there is no card to show) are the plan's own fields, first a short group with no heading of its own. Each has a gray hint under it.

| Field | What to put |
| --- | --- |
| **What this scene must do** | The job it does for the book. If you cut it, what would the story lose? |
| **The viewpoint character's objective** | What the viewpoint character is trying to get or do here |
| **Central conflict** | Who or what is in the way, and who wins |
| **Intended outcome or change** | How things stand differently when the scene ends |
| **What you want the reader to feel** | Funny, shocking, profound, relatable, or your own word |

Then, under the heading **The turn, and around it**:

| Field | What to put |
| --- | --- |
| **Value at the start** | What matters to the character here, and whether it is going well (+) or badly (-). For example: Trust, + |
| **Value at the end** | The same value as the scene ends. A change from + to - (or back) is the scene turning |
| **How we got here** | The causes: what earlier events lead to this scene, not only the scene before it |
| **What must follow** | The consequences: what has to happen later because of this scene |
| **When (story time)** | When it happens, in your own words: the morning after the funeral, day 3 of the feud |
| **Year** | The calendar year as a number, such as 1987. With birth years on characters, it shows everyone's age here |
| **Where** | Type a place, or pick one of your Places from the list that appears as you type |

**How it saves.** Type in a field and click away. What you type is saved as Developing, in pencil gray, and the label shows *(developing)* until you decide it on the card in Narrative Zoom, when it turns to Decided ink blue. Each field is taught in full, with the common mistake and examples, in [Narrative Zoom](#narrative-zoom) and in [Every kind and every field](#every-kind-and-every-field).

**Example.** For *The empty boat*: **What this scene must do**, *Put the mystery in Marisol's hands, so she can't ignore it.* **Central conflict**, *Marisol wants to report the boat and forget it; Cyrus wants her to say nothing. Cyrus wins, for now.* **Value at the start**, *Safety, +*. **Value at the end**, *Safety, -*.

### Writing Room: scene plan page 2, About it

The second page holds facts about the scene as a piece of your manuscript: how far along it is, whose eyes it is told through, and whether it is ready to write.

### Writing Room: a scene's status

**What it is.** **Status** is a dropdown with the steps a scene goes through, from first idea to finished. Remember, this is the scene's status, not a fact's. The usual steps are:

| Step | Meaning | Circle in the binder |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Idea | You know a scene goes here, roughly | Empty |
| Planned | You have planned what it must do | Empty |
| Ready to draft | The plan is enough to write from | Empty |
| Drafting | You are writing it | Half |
| First draft complete | It has a whole first draft | Filled |
| Revising | You are reworking it | Filled |
| Revised | The revision is done | Filled |
| Finalized | It is finished | Filled |

Under the dropdown, a small key shows which steps give which circle, with the open scene's line highlighted. Status and its key are at the top of page **2 About it**.

**Why it matters.** Status is how you see the shape of your progress across the whole book: which scenes are only ideas, which are drafted, which are done.

**It moves by itself once.** When the first words of a not-started scene become a saved version, its status moves to Drafting by itself. After that, every move is yours: choose **First draft complete** when the draft is done, and so on.

**The common mistake** is expecting the status to move to First draft complete on its own when you finish. Story Canon can't know when a scene is done. Only you can.

### Writing Room: changing the status steps

The steps are yours to change, in **Settings**, on page **3 Writing**, in the section **Scene status steps**:

1. Rename a step by typing in its box. Use **↑** and **↓** to reorder, **Remove** to take one away, and **Add a step** for a new one.
2. Tick **Counts as drafted** for every step where the scene has prose.
3. Click **Save settings** at the bottom of the window.

**How the circles follow.** Steps before the first one ticked **Counts as drafted** give an empty circle. The first ticked step gives a half circle. Every ticked step after it gives a filled circle. More in [Settings and your other computers](#settings-and-your-other-computers).

### Writing Room: viewpoint character

The **viewpoint character** is the character whose eyes and thoughts a scene is told through. **Viewpoint character** is a dropdown of the characters in your Story Brain (the view that holds every record about your story). Choose the character, or **Not set** for none. If you have no characters yet, it says *Add characters in the Story Brain.*

**Example.** *The empty boat* is told through Marisol, so you choose **Marisol Vega**. A later scene in the harbor office is told through Cyrus, so there you choose **Cyrus Hale**.

### Writing Room: kept for you to write or decide

**What it is.** This dropdown marks the scene as yours alone in some way, such as **Mine to write** or **Mine to choreograph**. The collaborator may talk about a marked scene, but it never writes or decides that part for you. It is the same **Kept for you** mark that records and beats have, with the same choices; every choice is explained in [Kept for you: work only you will do](#kept-for-you-work-only-you-will-do).

**Do you need it?** Only if you use the collaborator. Without it, the marker is simply a reminder to yourself above the title.

**How to use it.** Open the dropdown and choose a marker; choose **Not marked** to clear it. The gray line under the dropdown explains the choice you picked, and the marker appears above the scene's title on the page.

**Example.** The scene where Marisol finally says Eli's name aloud is one you want to write without help. You choose **Mine to write**, and *Mine to write* appears above the scene's title.

### Writing Room: this draft is settled

**What it is.** A checkbox: **This draft is settled: the page wins over the plan**. Tick it once the scene reads the way you want. From then on, where the page and the plan disagree, the page is the truth and the old plan becomes history.

**Why it matters.** Plans change in the writing. Ticking this box tells Story Canon, and the collaborator, to believe the page.

**When to tick it.** When you have finished a draft of the scene you are happy with, not while you are still finding it.

**Example.** You planned for Marisol to send Ana away at the lighthouse door. On the page, Ana stayed for tea. Once you like the scene, you tick the box, so nobody "corrects" the page back to the plan.

### Writing Room: who is here, and how old

A section headed **Who is here** appears when the scene has a **Year**, or when characters connected to the scene have birth years or fixed ages. It lists each character who appears in the scene with their age that year, or *age not known yet*. If the year is set but no characters are connected to the scene, it says *Connect characters to this scene (appears in) to see their ages.*

**Example.** The flashback to the night Eli drowned has the Year 2012. Marisol (born 1983) and Eli (born 1995) are both connected to it, so Who is here shows *Marisol Vega, 29* and *Eli Vega, 17*.

### Writing Room: drafting readiness

**What it is.** **Drafting readiness** lists what is still open in the plan, item by item, each marked **Open:** or **Done:**. The items are: Viewpoint character; What this scene must do; The viewpoint character's objective; Central conflict; Intended outcome or change; and How we got here or what must follow. It sits last on page **2 About it**. When everything is done, it says *Ready to draft.* Otherwise it says *Still open on The plan page. You can write anyway.* All the items except **Viewpoint character** are fields on page **1 The plan**; Viewpoint character is on this page, a little higher up.

**It is information, never a gate.** You can write any scene at any time, planned or not. The list is there for when a scene stalls: often the reason is one of these open items.

### Writing Room: scene plan page 3, Notes

The third page holds the notes that gather around a scene while you write it, and its earlier versions.

### Writing Room: Left for later in the scene plan

If the open scene has notes for later in it, page **3 Notes** starts with **Left for later** and the number, listing just this scene's notes. Click one to select it on the page. When the scene has no notes, this section doesn't appear. Notes for later are taught in full in [Writing Room: notes for later](#writing-room-notes-for-later).

### Writing Room: attached to this scene

When you capture a thought, question or idea while writing in this scene and leave **Attach to this scene** ticked (see [Writing Room: Capture while writing](#writing-room-capture-while-writing)), it waits here. Each item shows its kind (**Question**, **Idea** or **Thought**), *needed to keep writing* if you said so, and its text. The buttons depend on the kind:

- A question: **Answered**, or **No longer relevant**.
- An idea: **Adopt**, or **Set aside**.
- A thought: **Done**.

Each button takes the item off this list. It stays in the Story Brain with its new state. When nothing is attached, the section says *Nothing open. Capture a thought with Ctrl+Shift+N and it will wait here.*

### Writing Room: scene notes

**Scene notes** is a free box for anything about the scene that isn't story: a reminder to check a fact, a line you might use. It saves when you click away. Below it are the **Earlier versions** button and a reminder that moving and deleting the scene happen in the binder.

### Writing Room: earlier versions of a scene

**What it is.** Every saved version of a scene is kept. **Earlier versions**, at the bottom of page **3 Notes** in the scene plan, opens the list.

**Why it matters.** You can cut boldly, because nothing you cut is ever really gone.

**How to compare and restore.**

1. Click **Earlier versions**. A list of saved versions appears on the left, each with its date, time and word count. A new scene says *No earlier versions yet. Each saved stretch of writing becomes one.*
2. Click a version. The right side shows it compared with the current text: **struck through** words are only in the earlier version, **highlighted** words are only in the current one.
3. To go back to it, click **Restore this version**. The current text stays in the list, so restoring is never a loss.
4. Click **Close** to leave without changing anything.

Restoring a version is not writing, so it never counts toward your words for the day.

**Example.** Yesterday you cut Cyrus's speech about the tide tables. Today you miss it. You open **Earlier versions**, click yesterday afternoon's version, see the speech struck through, and copy the lines you want back into the page, without restoring the whole scene.

## Writing Room: the links above the page

The row of links above the page holds the tools for the moments when writing gets hard: hearing it, being stuck, needing to see the bigger picture, and shutting out the world. They are described here from left to right, after **Hide scene plan**, which is covered above.

### Writing Room: Read it back

**What it is.** **Read it back** swaps the scene plan for a panel that reads your scene aloud and points out three kinds of things worth a second look. It never changes a word. Click **Read it back** again to close the panel, then **Show scene plan** to bring the plan back.

**Why it matters.** Your ear catches what your eye skims: rhythm, repetition, a joke's timing, a voice that slips.

**Hear it.**

- **Read the scene** reads from the top. **From the cursor** starts where your cursor is.
- While it reads: **Pause**, then **Go on**; or **Stop**.
- **Default voice** is a dropdown of the voices on this computer. **Speed** is a slider.
- **Follow along**, when ticked, selects each word on the page as it is read, so you can see where you are.

**Echoes.** An **echo** is the same word, or a word from the same family (*walked* and *walking*), used twice or more within about 120 words. Only words of four letters or more count, and common little words and your characters' and places' names are left out. Each echo shows as a small chip, such as *tide ×2*. Click a chip to select the first use on the page; click again for the next. Sometimes a repeat is rhythm and you keep it. Sometimes it is an echo and you change one.

**Your watch words.** **Watch words** are words you know you lean on too often. Story Canon starts you with a starter list many writers use (*just, really, very* and 15 more), and says so. Any that appear on the page show as chips with a count; click to step through them. **Change the list** opens a box: type one word or phrase per line, or separate them with commas, and click **Save**. Once you change it, the list is yours, for this novel.

**Long sentences.** Any sentence over 40 words is listed with its length and its first words. Click one to select it on the page. A long sentence is not wrong; it is worth reading aloud once.

While the door is closed (a setting that stops Story Canon judging your draft until a day you choose, taught in [Goals: door closed](#goals-door-closed)), Echoes, watch words and Long sentences wait, and a line says so, with **Look anyway** to see them on purpose. **Hear it** always works.

**The common mistake** is fixing every chip. The panel points; you decide.

### Writing Room: Show in Narrative Zoom

**Show in Narrative Zoom** opens Narrative Zoom on this scene's own page: its place on a beat, the characters in it, the start of its text, and its full plan. It is how you see where this scene sits in the shape of the whole book. From there, **Open the page** brings you back.

**If the scene is not on your plan yet**, a note appears above the page instead: *This scene is not on your plan yet, so Narrative Zoom has no place to show it. Fill in any line of its scene plan and it becomes a card you can put on a beat.* It offers **Open Narrative Zoom anyway** and **Close**. This is not an error: a scene you started with **Add scene** has no card until it has a plan. If the card already exists in your plan, join them instead (see [Writing Room: joining a written scene to its card](#writing-room-joining-a-written-scene-to-its-card)).

### Writing Room: I'm stuck

**What it is.** **I'm stuck** opens a box that offers one small thing to do, so the empty page becomes a single sentence.

**What it shows.** If your book has a beat sheet (a writing method's list of beats, in order; where to choose one is in [Choosing a beat sheet for a novel](#choosing-a-beat-sheet-for-a-novel)), the box shows one beat:

- A line at the top: *Read only this beat. Do not look at the others.* If the open scene sits at this beat, the line starts by saying so. The point is to shrink the problem to one beat.
- The beat's number and name, such as **4. Catalyst**.
- **What this beat does**: the teaching for that beat, and sometimes a short example from a published book or film, marked as an example.
- **One sentence of what happens at this beat**: a box for one sentence, and **Save the sentence**. Your sentence is saved in pencil (Developing) on that beat, under *What it is (your plan)*, and a note confirms where, with **See it there** to open the beat.
- **Or the next smallest step:** **Fill in one line about a character** (opens the Story Brain), or **Write one line in a narrator's voice, complaining about anything** (opens Capture).
- **A different beat**, a dropdown at the bottom right, if you would rather think about another beat. **Close** closes the box.

**Where it starts.** Opened from a scene that sits at a beat (or might go at one), it starts at that beat. Otherwise it starts at the first beat.

**If the book has no beat sheet**, the box is headed **Start here** and says *Write one sentence of anything. It counts.* The button **Write one sentence** opens Capture.

**The common mistake** is reading all the beats looking for inspiration. That is the opposite of the tool. One beat, one sentence.

**Example.** You are stuck in *The empty boat*. You click **I'm stuck**. The box opens at **4. Catalyst**, saying *"The empty boat" sits at this beat.* You read what a Catalyst does, type *Marisol finds Eli's old knife in the boat's locker*, and click **Save the sentence**. Now you know what the scene is for.

### Writing Room: Ask about this scene

**Ask about this scene** opens a new conversation with the collaborator, titled *About the scene (the scene's name)*, already focused on this scene, so the collaborator reads its plan. If the collaborator isn't turned on yet, the conversation says so, with a link to turn it on in Settings; nothing is sent anywhere until you do. See [Conversations and the collaborator](#conversations-and-the-collaborator).

Asking leaves the page, so it is a detour: use **Return to Writing** to come back.

### Writing Room: detours and Return to Writing

Writing gets interrupted. You check Ana's age in the Story Brain, look at a beat in Narrative Zoom, read a conversation. Each of these is a **detour**: a time you left the page while writing. Story Canon keeps a record of each one, called the **Writing Trail**, so stepping away never costs you your place.

**What counts as a detour.** Leaving the Writing Room for any other tab, while a scene is open, records a detour named for where you went, such as *Went to Story Brain*. These do not count:

- Opening the **Writing Trail** itself, because the trail is part of the Writing Room.
- Opening **Capture**, because it opens over the page instead of leaving it.
- Pressing **F1** for Help. (Clicking the **Help** tab does count.)

**Return to Writing.** Whenever you are away from the page with a scene open, a pink ribbon, **Return to Writing**, hangs at the top left of the window. (It appears once there is writing to return to: you have been on the page this session, or the scene has words in it.) Click it, or press **Ctrl+Shift+W** from anywhere, and you land back in the scene you left, cursor and all. It also closes every open detour.

Clicking **The Writing Room** tab takes you back to the page too, but it does not close detours. That is why **Detours still open** on Where was I? can keep listing a detour after you came back: it closes the next time you use the ribbon or Ctrl+Shift+W, or when you mark it resolved.

### Writing Room: the Writing Trail page

**What it is.** **Writing Trail**, above the page (or under **Detours still open**, on page **2 Still open** of Where was I?), opens the list of your detours, newest first. The **The Writing Room** tab stays lit while you are there. Each detour shows:

- **Where you went**, in bold, and the date and time.
- A gray status line: *Returned to writing* or *Not returned yet*, and *resolved* or *not resolved*.
- A box, *What came of it, if you like: an answer, a decision*, for a note about what the detour was for.
- **Mark resolved** (or **Mark unresolved**), and **Go back to that scene**.

**Returned and resolved are two different things.** *Returned* means you came back to the page. *Resolved* means you did what you went to do. You might return without finding the answer and resolve it later. Marking a detour resolved also closes it, so it leaves Detours still open on Where was I?.

**To keep a note about a detour:**

1. Type in **What came of it**: *Checked: Ana arrived in Port Ellery in March*.
2. Click **Mark resolved**. The note is saved with it. Typing in the box alone does not save it; the button does.

**Why it matters.** A week later, the trail answers "Why did I change that?" and "Did I ever check when Ana arrived?" without searching.

### Writing Room: Sprint

**Sprint** starts a set time of nothing but the page: 15, 30 or 60 minutes, or a length you choose, with a small clock in the corner and everything else hidden until it runs out. Sprints are taught in full in [Goals, sprints and November](#goals-sprints-and-november).

### Writing Room: Focus

**What it is.** **Focus**, the last link above the page, hides everything but the page: the binder, the scene plan, the links, the tabs, the story strip across the top of the window (see [The story strip across the top of the window](#the-story-strip-across-the-top-of-the-window)), and the header with the Saved indicator and the Capture button. Only your scene, its title and its word line remain.

**Why it matters.** Sometimes the screen itself is the distraction: you keep glancing at the word count, the binder, the open questions.

**How to use it.**

1. Click **Focus**, or press **Ctrl+Shift+F**.
2. Write.
3. To come back, press **Esc**, press **Ctrl+Shift+F** again, or click **Leave focus** at the top right. Beside it a faint line reminds you: *Esc leaves focus. Ctrl+Shift+N still captures a thought.*

**Focus or a sprint?** Focus has no clock and no end; it lasts until you leave. A sprint is like Focus with a clock and a count at the end.

## Writing Room: notes for later

**What it is.** A **note for later** is a reminder you leave inside the page itself, between double square brackets: `[[how many steps up to the lamp room?]]`. You type it and keep going.

**Why it matters.** The fastest way to lose a writing day is to stop and look something up. A question about a date becomes twenty minutes of research, and by the time you come back the scene has gone cold. A note keeps the question without stopping the scene.

**Do you need it?** Almost every writer does, every day.

**How to leave one.**

- Type `[[`, your note, then `]]`.
- Or press **Ctrl+Shift+L**. It puts in the brackets with the cursor between them, ready to type. When the note is done, press the right arrow key twice to step past the closing brackets (or **End**, if you were writing at the end of the paragraph) and keep writing.
- To turn words you already wrote into a note, select them (within one paragraph) and press **Ctrl+Shift+L**. They are wrapped in brackets, exactly as written.

A note can't contain square brackets or run across a line break.

**What happens to it.**

- On the page, a note looks like pencil in the margin: gray, slanted, on a faint shaded background, so it never reads as story. Point at it to see *A note for later. It is not counted as story words.*
- **It is not counted.** Notes never add to a scene's words, a day's words or your push total.
- It is listed under **Left for later** on page **3 Notes** of the scene plan (this scene's notes) and on Where was I? (every note in the book, in book order, with the words you wrote just before it).
- Clicking a note in either list opens its scene with the note selected. Type the answer, and it replaces the note.
- A note leaves the lists when its brackets leave the page.
- Notes are printed exactly as written if you export with them still in the text, so clear them before you send the book out.

**The common mistake** is answering the note right away "since it's only a minute." It is rarely a minute. Leave it.

**Example.** You are writing Marisol's climb to the lamp room and can't remember how many steps you gave the stairs in Chapter 2. You press **Ctrl+Shift+L**, type *how many steps?*, press **End**, and go straight on to the light sweeping the water. On Saturday morning, Left for later shows the note. You check Chapter 2, click the note, type *one hundred and twelve*, and the note is gone.

## Writing Room: Capture while writing

**Capture** catches a thought without losing your place. Click **Capture** at the top right, or press **Ctrl+Shift+N**. The shortcut works in Focus and during a sprint too.

1. Type the thought, exactly as it comes. Your words are kept exactly as written.
2. Choose what it is. A gray line under the choices says where each goes:
   - **Just a thought**: anything, unsorted. It waits in Captured thoughts until you file it as a question or an idea, or leave it.
   - **A question I have**: something you need to find out or decide. It goes to Questions I have, where you can answer it later.
   - **An idea I am thinking about**: something you might use: a scene, a twist, a line. It goes to Things and ideas I am thinking about.
3. In the Writing Room, two more choices appear. **Attach to this scene** (ticked at first) puts it in the scene plan, on page **3 Notes**, under **Attached to this scene**. Then choose **Resolve later**, or **I need this to keep writing** for something that is blocking you.
4. Click **Save it**, or press **Ctrl+Enter**. **Cancel** or **Esc** closes without saving.

The box closes and your cursor is back on the page. A short line beside the Capture button says where the thought went (*Saved. It is attached to this scene, in the scene plan.*), then fades after a few seconds.

**A note for later or Capture?** Use a note for later for something that belongs at an exact spot in the text: a missing name, a date, a number of steps. Use Capture for a thought about the story: a question, an idea for another scene. It lands in the Story Brain, where you can sort it.

**Example.** Mid-sentence, you wonder whether Cyrus already knew the boat's name before Marisol told him. You press **Ctrl+Shift+N**, type the question, choose **A question I have** and **I need this to keep writing**, and press **Ctrl+Enter**. It now waits in the scene plan, on page 3 Notes, under Attached to this scene, marked *needed to keep writing*, and on Where was I? under **Questions still open**.

## Writing Room: when the program closed or two windows disagree

These two boxes are rare. When one appears, the page will not take typing until you choose. That is on purpose: it means nothing can be overwritten by accident.

### Writing Room: typing kept after the program closed

If Story Canon closes before your latest typing became a saved version (a crash, a power cut, a laptop battery running out), the safety copy is still there. Two things happen:

- **Where was I?** shows *Unsaved typing was kept for "the scene". Open the scene to choose which version to keep.*, with a button to open it.
- **When you open that scene**, a box appears over the page titled *Typing from (date and time) was kept*. It says: *The program closed before this typing became a saved version. Nothing has been lost. Choose which one to keep; the other stays in this scene's earlier versions.* It shows the end of the **Kept typing**.

Choose one:

- **Use the kept typing** makes the kept words the scene's text.
- **Keep the saved version** keeps what was saved before.

The one you don't choose is kept in **Earlier versions**, so this choice can never lose anything. Usually you want **Use the kept typing**: it holds your newest words.

### Writing Room: the same scene open in two windows

If the same scene is open in two Story Canon windows and you type in both, the second window to save would overwrite the first. Story Canon never lets that happen silently. A box appears: *This scene was changed in another window. Nothing has been overwritten. Choose what to keep.* It shows the end of the other window's version, and three buttons:

- **Keep both as separate scenes** keeps the other window's text in this scene, and makes a new scene right after it, named *(the scene's name) (my version)*, holding this window's text, and opens it. You can compare them and merge by hand.
- **Keep this window's** saves what you see in this window.
- **Keep the other window's** replaces what you see with the other window's text.

**The common mistake** is clicking the first button that looks safe without reading. If you are not sure which version is better, **Keep both as separate scenes** is the one that never loses a word.

## Writing Room: keys

"Ctrl" is the key in the bottom left corner of your keyboard. Press the keys together.

| Keys | What they do |
| --- | --- |
| Ctrl+Shift+N | Capture a thought |
| Ctrl+Shift+L | Leave a note for later, or turn the selected words into one |
| Ctrl+Shift+F | Focus on or off |
| Esc | Leave Focus |
| Ctrl+Shift+W | Return to Writing, from anywhere in the book |
| Ctrl+B, Ctrl+I | Bold, italic |
| Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y | Undo and redo typing |
| Enter | Save a title, a chapter name or a part name |

## Writing Room: when it looks wrong but is working

| What you see | Why |
| --- | --- |
| The scene's status is still Idea after you wrote in it | The status moves to Drafting when the words become a saved version: after about twenty seconds' pause, or when you leave the scene |
| The binder's circle is empty while the scene has words | Same reason. Or you chose a not-started status yourself; the circle follows the Status dropdown |
| The page won't take typing | A box is waiting for a choice (kept typing, or two windows), or the page still says *Opening the page…* |
| The status, scene notes or Earlier versions are missing from the scene plan | They are on other pages of the plan. Status is on **2 About it**; notes and **Earlier versions** are on **3 Notes**. Click the tab, or **All pages** |
| The scene plan disappeared | **Read it back** was clicked twice, or **Hide scene plan** once. Click **Show scene plan** |
| Show in Narrative Zoom says the scene is not on the plan | The scene has no card yet. Fill any line of its scene plan, or join it to its card |
| No red underlines and no echoes | The door is closed (see [Goals: door closed](#goals-door-closed)), or a sprint is running |
| A new scene isn't in Planned, not written yet | That list only holds cards from your plan with no page. A scene you added is already in the binder |
| A card you already wrote is still in Planned, not written yet | Its page was made with **Add scene**, so they are not joined. Use **Is this scene on your plan?** on page **1 The plan** of the scene plan |
| A note for later isn't in the Left for later list yet | The list reads the saved scene. Pause a moment, or leave the scene, and it appears |
| No Return to Writing ribbon | You are in the Writing Room already, no scene is open, or there is no writing to return to yet |
