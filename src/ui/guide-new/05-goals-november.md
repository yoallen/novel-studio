# Goals, sprints and November

This chapter is about the number side of writing: how Story Canon counts your words each day, a daily goal for ordinary days, a **writing push** such as November and 50,000 words, how Story Canon works out what each day needs, what happens on the days you fall short, and how to plan a lighter day or a rest day without feeling you have failed. It also covers **sprints** (writing against a clock), **door closed** (no judging until a day you choose), and notes you dictate away from the desk. Read it when you want a goal, before a November push, or the first time the columns on Where was I puzzle you.

All of it is optional. With no goal at all, Story Canon still shows how many words you wrote each day, and never judges a day.

## Goals: where your progress shows

Your progress appears in three places:

- **Where was I**, your novel's home page, under **Next**: the **drafting progress panel**, with a thin column for each day and a few figures under it. This is the main one, and most of this chapter is about it.
- **The Writing Room**, in the small line under the page: *1,204 words in this scene, today's goal 1,743*. Today's goal appears there only once you have a goal.
- **The Writing Room binder**, at the top: the whole book's word count, and "of" your target length if you set one.

With no goal and no push, the panel shows this month's columns and says *Set a daily word goal, or a writing push such as November, and this shows how you are tracking.*, with a **Settings** link.

## Goals: what counts as a day's words

Every number in this chapter starts from one idea: **a day's words are the words you typed that day, minus the words you cut.**

- Typing new words adds to the day.
- Cutting words takes away from it. A day of heavy cutting can come out below zero; point at its column and it says how many words were cut.
- **Notes for later**, `[[like this]]`, never count (see [The Writing Room](#the-writing-room)).
- **Not writing, so never counted:** bringing in a manuscript, accepting changes an editor sent back, restoring an earlier version of a scene, and choosing **Keep the saved version** after a crash. The words are in your book (they count in **The whole book**), but none of them is you writing that day.
- **Days follow your computer's clock and time zone.** Words typed at 11:58 at night count for that day; words at 12:02 count for the next.
- **Words count when they become a saved version of the scene**: about twenty seconds after you stop typing, or when you leave the scene. If **Today** looks behind, wait a moment.

**Why it matters.** A tracker you can't trust is worse than none. Counting only what you typed keeps the columns honest: a day with 3,000 imported words and 200 written ones shows 200.

## Goals: an everyday goal for ordinary days

**What it is.** An **everyday goal** is the daily target that applies on any day that isn't part of a writing push. Writers who use one usually choose a modest number of words per day, such as 500 or 1,000.

**Do you need it?** Only if a daily number helps you. If you only want a number for one stretch, such as November, use a writing push instead and leave the everyday goal at **No goals**.

**How to set one.**

1. Click **Settings** on the right edge of the window.
2. In the section **Guidance and goals**, open the **Everyday goal** dropdown and choose **Words per day**.
3. In **Goal amount**, type your number, such as 500.
4. Click **Save settings** at the bottom of the window.

Where was I now measures every day this month against 500. The top line says *Today, 74 words of your 500.*, each day's column gets a thin dashed line at the goal's height, and the columns turn dark on days you reach it.

**The choices in the dropdown.** A gray line under the dropdown says what the one you picked does.

| Choice | What it does now |
| --- | --- |
| **No goals** | No daily number. Where was I still shows what you wrote each day |
| **Words per day** | A number of words each day. Where was I tracks it every day you write |
| **Scenes per week** | Kept as your goal in Settings. Where was I counts words for now, so the columns show no goal line |
| **Minutes per day** | Kept as your goal in Settings. Where was I counts words for now |
| **Milestones** | No daily number. A box, **My milestones**, holds the milestones you are working toward, one per line, such as *Act One drafted by March*. They are kept in Settings for you to read and tick off yourself |

**The common mistake** is setting the everyday goal to your best-day number. A goal you meet four days out of five builds a habit; one you miss four days out of five builds dread. Set the number you can reach on a tired Tuesday.

**Example.** *The Keeper of Gull Point* moves slowly through the autumn. You set **Words per day** to 300. Most days you write 400 or 500, and the columns are dark. On the day you write 150, the column is gray and striped, and the next day's goal is still just 300. An everyday goal never carries a shortfall forward; only a writing push does that.

## Goals: reading the drafting progress panel

The panel on Where was I starts with a heading in slanted purple type: the month's name, or your push's name (or *Your writing push* if you left the name empty). Under it is a sentence about today, such as *Today, 600 words of your 1,743.* Then come the columns: one thin column per day of the month (or of the push), with day numbers underneath.

### Goals: the marks on each column

The columns borrow the look of the status inks: a dark solid mark for done, pencil for in progress, a blank for nothing yet. Pink is always you: today, and the days you set yourself.

| What you see | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| A solid dark column | You met that day's goal. With no goal set, any day with words is dark |
| A gray, striped column | Pencil. You wrote that day, but less than its goal |
| No column, just the solid base line | A blank: no words that day |
| A dotted gray base line | A day still ahead |
| A dotted pink base line | A rest day you set, still ahead or with no words |
| A thick pink base line, and the day's number in bold pink | Today |
| A thin dashed line across a column | That day's goal, at its height |
| A solid pink line across a column | The goal of a day you set yourself (a lighter or bigger day) |

The numbers under the columns show only the first day, today, and every fifth day, to keep the row readable. The height of each column is in proportion to the biggest day shown, so a short column next to a tall one is a real difference.

### Goals: pointing at a column

Point at any column to see its exact story, for example:

- *November 3: 900 words of 1,661*
- *November 8: 2,400 words, goal met*
- *November 13 (today): 600 words of 1,743*
- *November 26, still ahead, a day you set at 300*

### Goals: the figures under the columns

| Figure | What it says | When it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **So far** (or **October so far**, without a push) | Words written in this push or this month, and "of" the push's goal | Except before a push begins |
| **To finish on time** | About how many words a day you need from here, and how many days are left, today included | During a push with a word goal |
| **Against the plan** | How far ahead or behind you are, counted to the end of yesterday | During a push, when you are 50 or more words ahead or behind |
| **Days in a row** | How many days in a row you have written | Except before a push begins |
| **The whole book** | Every word in the manuscript, and "of" your target length if you set one | Always |

Each is explained in full below. Under the figures, during a push, are **Set a lighter day** and the link to close the door.

## Goals: a writing push, such as November

**What it is.** A **writing push** is a stretch of days with a word goal. The classic is November and 50,000 words, the challenge many writers take on each year. You can make a push of any length, such as a two-week retreat or a spring quarter.

**Why it matters.** A push turns a big number into a small daily one, and then keeps that daily number honest as the days go by. If you fall short, it tells you exactly what each remaining day needs. If you get ahead, it lets you ease off.

**Do you need it?** Only for a stretch with a target. You can have an everyday goal and a push at once: the push's daily goal applies on the push's days, and the everyday goal the rest of the time.

### Goals: setting up a November push

1. Click **Settings** on the right edge of the window.
2. Find the section **A writing push** and click **Plan a writing push**. Story Canon fills in November: **Name it** *November*, **First day** November 1, **Last day** November 30, **Words to write in it** 50,000.
3. Change anything you like.
4. Read the line under the boxes: *That is about 1,667 words a day for 30 days.* That is your daily goal on the push's days only. The rest of the time, your everyday goal applies.
5. Click **Save settings** at the bottom of the window.

To remove the push, click **No push** in the same section, then **Save settings**.

### Goals: before the push starts

Where was I starts showing the push **two weeks before its first day**, so you can see it coming. The heading says **November**, and the panel says *November starts on November 1. 50,000 words in 30 days is about 1,667 a day.* Under it: *Today, 74 words. The chart below fills in once November begins.* Today's words still count toward your everyday goal, if you have one.

This is the time to set any lighter or rest days you already know about (see [Goals: lighter days and rest days](#goals-lighter-days-and-rest-days)) and to decide whether to close the door for the month (see [Goals: door closed](#goals-door-closed)).

### Goals: during the push

From November 1, the columns are November's thirty days, and every figure is about the push. The sentence at the top changes with the day:

- *Today, 600 words of your 1,743.* while you are working toward today's goal.
- *Today, 1,800 words of your 1,743. The day's goal is met.* once you reach it. **To finish on time** then switches to what the days after today need.
- *Today is a rest day you set.* on a rest day, with *You wrote 412 words anyway, and they all count.* if you did.

### Goals: after the push ends

For a week after the last day, the panel says *November is done: 51,204 words of 50,000.* with the month's columns still showing. After that week, it goes back to showing the current month with your everyday goal.

## Goals: catch-up math, or how a short day is shared

**What it is.** During a push, each day's goal is **an even share of what is still left**. Story Canon takes the words still to write, sets aside the days you have set yourself, and divides the rest evenly over the days that remain, today included.

**What that means in practice.**

- **A short day spreads its difference over the rest of the month.** Each later day asks a few words more, instead of one day asking for a mountain.
- **A big day lowers every later day a little.** Getting ahead buys you slack.
- **Today's goal is worked out from what you wrote before today**, so it never moves while you are writing. Today's words only change tomorrow's goal.

**Worked example.** You are drafting *The Keeper of Gull Point* in November, 50,000 words, so the month starts at 1,667 a day.

| Day | Written | That day's goal | What happened next |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| November 1 | 1,800 | 1,667 | A bit ahead |
| November 2 | 1,700 | 1,663 | Still ahead |
| November 3 | 900 | 1,661 | A short day: 761 words under |
| November 4 | 2,100 | 1,689 | The 761 words were shared over the 27 days left, so each asked about 28 more |
| November 6 | 0 | 1,670 | A blank day |
| November 7 | 1,500 | 1,740 | November 6's 1,670 words, shared over 24 days, added about 70 to each |

By the morning of November 13, you have written 18,630 words in twelve days. 31,370 are left, over 18 days counting today, so the panel says *Today, 0 words of your 1,743.* and **To finish on time** says *about 1,743 a day, 18 days left*.

**The common mistake** is trying to make up a short day the very next day. The math already spreads it out. Write today's number, not yesterday's and today's.

## Goals: lighter days and rest days

November has days when 1,667 words won't happen: a late shift, a birthday, Thanksgiving. Story Canon lets you plan for them ahead of time, so they are part of the plan instead of a hole in it.

**What it is.** A **set day** is a day in your push whose goal you choose yourself. A **lighter day** has a smaller number, such as 300. A **rest day** has zero. You can also set a **bigger day**, such as 3,000 for a free Saturday. On a set day, your number is that day's goal, and meeting it counts as meeting the day.

**Where it is.** On Where was I, under the progress figures, the link **Set a lighter day**. It appears only with a push that has a word goal, from two weeks before it starts until its last day.

**How to set a day.**

1. Click **Set a lighter day**. A row opens with **Day**, **Words**, **Set it**, **Make it a rest day** and **Cancel**, and a gray line: *That day's goal becomes your number, and the days you have not set share the difference. Nothing is held against you.*
2. In **Day**, choose the date. You can choose any day from today (or from the push's first day, before it starts) to its last day.
3. Either type the words in **Words** (it starts at 500) and click **Set it**, or click **Make it a rest day**.

The day you set now appears in a list under the figures, such as *November 26: 300 words* or *November 27: rest day*, each with a **Clear** link that puts it back to an ordinary day. The list shows the set days from today on. The day's column shows a solid pink goal line (a lighter or bigger day) or a dotted pink base line (a rest day). **To finish on time** adds *counting the days you set*.

**What happens to the difference.** The days you have not set share it. In the worked example, on November 13 you set November 26 to 300 and November 27 as a rest day. Those two days now carry 300 words instead of about 3,486, so the other 16 days share the rest: today's goal rises from 1,743 to 1,942.

**Why plan ahead.** Spread over sixteen days, the two lighter days cost about 200 words each. Left until the day itself, they would arrive as a sudden gap at the end of the month.

**The common mistake** is waiting until the bad day to admit it. If you know Thanksgiving will be a 300-word day, set it now. The other days absorb it a little at a time.

**Example.** The Port Ellery library has asked you to give a talk on November 14, and family visits on November 22 and 23. On November 1 you set the 14th to 500, and the 22nd and 23rd as rest days. Your daily goal for the other days rises from 1,667 to 1,834, and those three days show pink marks in the columns. On the 22nd, the panel says *Today is a rest day you set.*, and your **Days in a row** stays unbroken.

### Goals: when set days leave words with nowhere to go

If you set so many days so low that the remaining words have no day left to be written in, **To finish on time** says so plainly: *Your set days leave 2,400 words with no day to write them. Raise a set day or clear one.* Click **Clear** beside a set day, or set it again with a bigger number.

If your set days already cover everything left, it says *Your set days cover what is left.* On a day you have not set, the top line then says *Your set days cover what is left, so today is yours to spend.*

## Goals: against the plan, ahead or behind

**What it is.** **Against the plan** compares what you have written with what an even pace would have given you, **up to the end of yesterday**. It says *1,374 behind, spread over the days ahead* or *620 ahead*.

**How it is counted.** The plan as it stood on the first day: the push's goal divided evenly over its days (1,667 a day for November). A past day you set counts as your number, so a rest day is never held against you; what it left undone is shared by the other days, so a real gap still shows. Days you set ahead of time never change this figure.

**When it shows.** Only during a push, only once you are 50 words or more ahead or behind, and not once the goal is reached.

**In the worked example:** twelve days at 1,667 is 20,004 words; you wrote 18,630; so *1,374 behind*. Today isn't counted yet, because today isn't over.

**How to use it.** As information, not a verdict. *Behind* doesn't mean you need one big day; **To finish on time** has already spread it over the days ahead. *Ahead* means you have earned a lighter day, if you want one.

## Goals: days in a row

**What it is.** **Days in a row** is how many days in a row, up to today, you have written at least one word.

- **Today is still open.** If you haven't written yet today, the count runs up to yesterday, and doesn't drop to zero until today ends with no words.
- **A rest day you set never breaks the run.** It doesn't add a day either; it just doesn't count against you.
- When there is no run, it says *Starts with your next words*.

**Why it matters.** A run of days is one of the most reliable ways to finish a draft. The number is there to protect the habit, which is why a planned rest day doesn't cost you the run.

**The common mistake** is writing ten words at 11:55 at night to keep the run alive, every night. If that is what keeps you coming back, fine. If it starts to feel like a chore, set a rest day instead.

## Goals: the whole book and your target length

**The whole book** is every word in your manuscript right now, in every scene that isn't deleted. Unlike the day columns, it includes words you brought in, restored or accepted from an editor. Notes for later aren't counted.

To see it as "of" a target, such as *52,310 of 80,000 words*:

1. Open **Settings**.
2. In the first section, type a number in **Target length in words (optional)**.
3. Click **Save settings**.

The binder in the Writing Room shows the same total and target at its top.

**Why both numbers.** The push measures your writing days; the whole book measures the book. In November, the first matters more. In March, when you are deciding whether the book is too long, the second does.

## Goals: sprints

**What it is.** A **sprint** is a stretch of focused writing against a clock. While it runs, everything but the page goes away.

**Why it matters.** A deadline you can see makes starting easier: you only have to write until the clock stops, not finish the chapter. Taking everything else off the screen removes the small temptations, like checking the word count or renaming a scene.

**Do you need it?** No, but on a November day it is often the easiest way to get the number down: two 30-minute sprints are most of a day's goal for many writers.

### Goals: starting a sprint

1. In the Writing Room, click **Sprint** above the page. A box opens, headed **Sprint**.
2. Choose **15 minutes**, **30 minutes** or **60 minutes**, or type a number from 1 to 240 in **Other**.
3. Leave **Fill the whole screen** ticked for the window to fill the screen, or untick it to keep the window as it is.
4. Click **Start the sprint** (or press **Enter** in the Other box). **Cancel** or **Esc** closes the box.

The cursor goes straight to the end of the page, so the first words you type land in the scene. Story Canon remembers your length and screen choice for next time. If no scene was open, the sprint opens the one you would get from **The Writing Room** tab.

### Goals: during a sprint

Gone: the binder, the scene plan, the links above the page, the tabs, the strip across the top, the header (with the Saved indicator and the Capture button), most notices, the word line under the page, and red spelling underlines. Keys that lead away from the page (**Ctrl+K** for Search, **Ctrl+Shift+W** for Return to Writing, **F1** for Help) do nothing until the sprint ends.

Still there:

- **A small, faint clock** in the bottom right corner, in slanted type: *30 min*, then *Under a minute*. Click it to show **Pause** and **End the sprint**. While paused, it says *Paused* and the time left; click it and choose **Resume**.
- **Ctrl+Shift+N** still opens Capture, for a thought that will not wait.
- **A save problem** is the one thing that breaks the quiet: if your writing can't be saved, a warning appears beside the clock.
- **A notice about newer work from your other computer**, if you write on more than one, because ignoring it would cost you later.

### Goals: when the time is up

Nothing stops you mid-sentence. A note appears at the bottom: **Time.** *(N) words in (N) minutes. Finish your sentence; nothing has stopped.* Then choose:

- **Keep going**: the page stays quiet, with no clock. The corner says *Still writing*; click it and choose **Done** when you finish, and a last note gives your count for the whole sprint.
- **Another sprint**: opens the sprint box again.
- **Done**: ends the sprint and brings everything back.

If you end a sprint early with **End the sprint**, the note says **Sprint over.** with your count, and offers **Another sprint** and **Done**.

**How the count works.** The count is every word you typed during the sprint, across every scene, minus what you cut, saved first so your last words count. If you cut more than you wrote, it says *You cut (N) words this sprint. That is writing too.* If you wrote nothing, it says *Time with the page counts too.* Sprint words count toward your day like any others.

**The common mistake** is choosing 60 minutes on a tired day and feeling beaten at minute 20. On a hard day, choose 15. A small sprint you finish beats a big one you abandon.

**Example.** It is November 8 and your goal is 1,680. After supper you click **Sprint**, choose **30 minutes**, and click **Start the sprint**. Marisol rows out to the reef where the boat was found. The clock says *Under a minute*, then the note says *Time. 912 words in 30 minutes.* You click **Another sprint**, and the second one takes you past the day's goal.

**Focus or a sprint?** **Focus** in the Writing Room hides everything too, but has no clock and no end (see [The Writing Room](#the-writing-room)). A sprint has a clock and a count at the end.

## Goals: door closed

**What it is.** **Door closed** is a setting that stops Story Canon from judging your draft until a day you choose. The phrase is Stephen King's: the first draft is written with the door closed, for you alone, and the door opens for revision.

**What waits while the door is closed:**

- No red spelling underlines on the page.
- No echoes, watch words or long sentences in **Read it back** (reading aloud still works).
- No story review or misspelled names in Narrative Zoom.
- The collaborator stays quiet about problems unless you ask.
- The *Ctrl+Shift+L leaves a note for later* reminder under the page is hidden.

Your plans, notes, search and reading aloud all stay. Anything that waits shows a **Look anyway** link, so you can still check something on purpose.

**Why pair it with a push.** A push is about getting words down. Judging them is a different kind of attention, and it pulls you into fixing. Closing the door for the push keeps the two apart.

**How to close it from Where was I.**

1. Under your push's figures, find the link **Close the door through November 30** (it always names your push's last day).
2. Click it.

The link is replaced by *The door is closed through November 30. Nothing judges the draft until then.* It takes effect at once; there is nothing to save.

**How to close it from Settings, with or without a push.**

1. Open **Settings** and find the section **Door closed**.
2. In **Keep it closed through**, choose a day. It suggests your push's last day, or a day thirty days from now.
3. Click **Close the door**.

**To open it early, or choose another day,** open the same section. It says *The door is closed through November 30.* with **Change the day** (choose a date, then **Save**) and **Open the door now**. These take effect at once too, without **Save settings**. The door opens by itself the day after the date you chose.

**The common mistake** is opening the door "just to fix one typo" on day four. That one typo leads to rereading Chapter 1. Use **Look anyway** for the single thing you need, and leave the door closed.

## Goals: notes from the road

November doesn't only happen at the desk. If you write on more than one computer and have turned on sharing (see [Settings and your other computers](#settings-and-your-other-computers)), each novel gets its own notes file in your shared folder. You can dictate into it from your phone, in Word, one note to a paragraph.

When Story Canon has the novel open, each new paragraph arrives as a captured thought, waiting under **Thoughts waiting to be sorted** on Where was I, usually within a minute of the file syncing, and a message says *A note from the road came in.* During a sprint that message waits, so the quiet isn't broken; the note still arrives.

**How it fits a push.** A note from the road is a thought, not manuscript, so it never counts toward your day's words. Use it for the line about the fog over Port Ellery that comes to you in the car, then write it into the scene when you sit down. Where to find the file and how to dictate into it are in [Settings and your other computers](#settings-and-your-other-computers).

## Goals: a November, day by day

Here is how the pieces fit together over one month of *The Keeper of Gull Point*.

**October 18.** You open **Settings**, click **Plan a writing push**, keep November and 50,000, and click **Save settings**. On Where was I, a **November** panel appears: *November starts on November 1. 50,000 words in 30 days is about 1,667 a day.* You know Thanksgiving week will be hard, so you click **Set a lighter day** and set November 26 to 300 and November 27 as a rest day. You click **Close the door through November 30**.

**November 1.** You open the book, read **Next** on Where was I, and click **Write**. You do two 30-minute sprints: Marisol finds the empty boat on her rocks. 1,800 words. The column is dark.

**November 6.** You are down with a cold and write nothing. The column is blank. You don't panic: tomorrow's goal is about 70 words higher, that's all.

**November 13.** The panel says you're 1,374 behind. You read **To finish on time** instead, *about 1,942 a day*, and write that. Ana Ruiz knocks on the lighthouse door for the first time. **Days in a row** says 7.

**November 27.** Rest day. The panel says *Today is a rest day you set.* You walk the harbor wall instead, and dictate one line about Cyrus's boots into your notes from the road.

**November 30.** You write 2,300 words and **To finish on time** says **Done. Everything now is extra.** For a week, the panel shows *November is done*, with thirty columns of your month. On December 1 the door opens by itself, and Read it back's echoes and long sentences are waiting for revision.
