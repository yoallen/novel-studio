# Bringing things in and sending things out

To **import** is to bring a file from somewhere else into Story Canon: a manuscript you started in Word, a story bible, a plotting spreadsheet, a copy your editor marked up. To **export** is to make a file from your book to use somewhere else: a Word document for an agent, a PDF to print, an e-book for your reader.

Both live on one tab, and both keep the same promises:

- **Nothing comes into your novel until you have looked it over.** Every import shows you a preview first.
- **Your words come in exactly as written,** and go out exactly as written. Every file is checked word for word.
- **Importing never overwrites anything,** and bringing in the same document twice never makes duplicates.
- **Exporting never changes your novel.**

## The Import and export tab

**Where:** the **Import and export** tab, under the word *and* on the right edge.

Across the top are three smaller tabs. The one you are on is darker and underlined.

| Tab | What it is for |
| --- | --- |
| **Bring something in** | Importing a file, and the list of everything you imported before. |
| **Send something out** | Exporting your book or your planning, and your plotting sheet. |
| **Your documents** | Every planning document you brought in, kept whole and readable. |

## Bring something in: the four kinds of file

On **Bring something in**, first choose what you have. There are four choices, each a radio button with a short description. Then click **Choose a file** and pick the file. While Story Canon reads it, the button says **Reading the file…**. Then the preview opens.

| Choose | For | File types |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Chapters of my manuscript** | Your book's text, from Word or another writing program. | Word (.docx), Rich Text (.rtf), Markdown (.md), OpenDocument (.odt), plain text (.txt) |
| **A planning document** | A story bible, an archive of decisions, character notes. | .docx, .rtf, .md, .txt, .odt |
| **A plotting spreadsheet** | Your beat sheet in Excel. | Excel (.xlsx) |
| **My editor's changes** | A Word file you exported from Story Canon that someone revised with Track Changes. | .docx |

(If you pick an Excel file under any choice, Story Canon knows it is a spreadsheet and treats it as one.)

### Chapters of my manuscript

**What happens:** Story Canon reads the file, finds where chapters and scenes begin, and proposes how to split it. You check the split, fix anything, and click **Add these chapters to my novel**. The chapters go in after your existing ones, word for word. Italics, bold and other formatting come with them.

**When to use it:** you started the book somewhere else and want to keep writing here, or you wrote a chapter on another computer that does not have Story Canon.

### A planning document

**What happens:** Story Canon breaks the document into **passages** (one paragraph or labeled block each) and shows each passage beside the record and blank where it would go. You tick the passages you want in your records. The whole document is kept in **Your documents** either way, so passages you leave unticked are still there and still searchable.

When you choose this, a small dropdown appears: **It is a** story bible, archive of decisions, or other planning document. This only names what kind of document it is.

**When to use it:** you kept notes about your characters, world or plot in Word and want them in the Story Brain, each with how sure you were.

### A plotting spreadsheet

**What happens:** Story Canon reads an Excel beat sheet (one beat per row, with where each falls in the book) and saves where it puts each beat as another way to place your beats, beside Save the Cat's own. Or, if you choose, it turns the sheet into a beat sheet of your own.

**When to use it:** you plotted in a spreadsheet, perhaps from a class or a template, and want its numbers here.

### My editor's changes

**What happens:** Story Canon compares the Word file with the scenes it came from, shows each tracked change with who made it, and lets you accept or turn down each one. Comments can come in as notes beside each scene. Nothing changes until you decide.

**When to use it:** you exported a Word file, sent it to an editor or a critique partner, and they sent it back with Track Changes on.

**The common mistake** is bringing in an edited Word file that did not come from Story Canon's own export. Story Canon finds its scenes in the file by the export's own layout, so start from a Word file you made on **Send something out**.

## Looking over an import before it goes in

Every import opens a preview first. At its top is **All imports** (back to the list) and the file's name. Any warnings show in shaded boxes. At the bottom, a bar that stays in view holds three things:

- **The button that brings it in.** Its words depend on the kind: **Add these chapters to my novel**, **Add the checked passages**, **Use this sheet** (or **Make it my beat sheet**), or **Make my decisions**.
- The reminder "One step. You can undo it from the Story Trail." The whole import is one change, and one **Undo** in the [Story Trail](#story-trail-the-history-of-every-change) takes it all back.
- **Don't import this**, which drops the import. Your novel is not touched.

If you leave the preview without deciding, the import waits under **Waiting for you to look over** on **Bring something in**. Click its name to pick up where you left off.

### The manuscript preview

The preview opens with a line such as "Here is how the file reads: 2 chapters, 39 words. Rename, join or leave out anything before it goes in. The words themselves are never changed."

Under it, a shaded note says exactly where the chapters will land. On a book with no chapters yet: "These become the first chapters of your novel." On a book with chapters: "They go in after your last chapter ("Chapter 1"), so the first one becomes chapter 2. Your chapter stays just as it is. You can move chapters later in the Writing Room."

Then each proposed chapter, with its scenes listed under it:

| On each chapter | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| The tick box at the left | Ticked, the chapter goes in. Untick it to leave it out. Left-out chapters fade. |
| The title box | The chapter's title. Click in and type to rename it. |
| **Part of the chapter before** | Joins this chapter to the one above it, for when a break was misread as a new chapter. |

| On each scene | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| The tick box | Include or leave out this scene. |
| The title box | The scene's name. Rename it here. |
| The word count and first line | So you can recognize it. |
| **Same scene as before** | Joins this scene to the one above it, for when a stray break split one scene in two. |

Warnings you may see, and what they mean:

- "No chapter headings were found, so everything is in one chapter." Story Canon looks for chapter headings. Mark chapter starts as headings in the file (in Word, the Heading style) and import again, or split the chapter later.
- "Text before the first chapter is kept as its own chapter. Leave it out if it is a title page."
- "3 very short scenes (under 40 words). These may be epigraphs or stray breaks; join them to the scene before if so." An **epigraph** is a short quotation at the start of a chapter.

At the bottom: "39 words will go in. Checked afterwards, word for word." After you click **Add these chapters to my novel**, a "Done" page shows how many chapters, scenes and words were added and the result of that check, with **Go to the Writing Room** and **Bring in something else**.

Bringing in a manuscript does not count toward your daily word goal. It is not new writing.

**Example.** You wrote two chapters of a lighthouse novel in Word. The preview finds "Chapter One: The Lamp" with two scenes and "Chapter Two: The Fog" with one. The second scene is only nine words, so you tick **Same scene as before** to join it to the first, then **Add these chapters to my novel**.

### The planning document preview

The preview opens with "Each passage keeps your label, and its exact words stay in the document. Checked passages go into the record shown beside them."

**Document date.** A date box sits at the top. Story Canon uses it to keep your decisions in the right order: "Your later decisions stay ahead of anything dated earlier." If the document has no date, a warning asks you to set one before applying. Set it to the day the document was last true.

**New records it would make.** If a heading names a character or set piece you do not have yet, the preview lists it here with a chip saying its kind and a box to fix its name before it is made.

**Which passages you see.** Three small tabs:

- **Going in** with a count: passages that are ticked.
- **Staying in the document** with a count: passages that are not ticked. They stay readable and searchable in **Your documents**.
- **All**.

A grey note may say how many passages are **already in your records** from an earlier import. Those are skipped automatically, never added twice.

**Each passage** is grouped under its top heading and shows:

| Part | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| The tick box | Ticked passages go in when you click **Add the checked passages**. |
| A small grey line | The headings above it in the document, such as "Characters > Mara Quill". |
| The passage | Its label in bold (if it had one), then its words. Long passages are shortened here, but go in whole. |
| The status dropdown | How sure the passage is. See the next section. |
| **into** and the record dropdown | Which record it goes into. **Stays in the document** means none. Beats show as "(beat)" and new records as "(new)". |
| **Which part…** | Which blank on that record it fills. |
| A grey note, sometimes | Why it was placed this way, or a caution, such as "You decided this later. That later decision stays current; this one is kept as history." |

**How Story Canon decides where a passage goes.** It reads your headings. A passage under a heading that names one of your records ("Kevin Wickley") goes to that record. A passage under a Save the Cat beat's name ("Catalyst") goes to that beat. A passage under a heading that names nothing it knows stays in the document unticked, for you to place by choosing a record in the **into** dropdown.

**Things that look odd but are working as designed:**

- **Going in (0).** If your document's headings do not name records you already have, nothing is ticked at first. Look under **Staying in the document**, choose a record for each passage you want, and tick it.
- **"Headings that match more than one record, left for you to place."** A heading could mean two records, so Story Canon will not guess. Place those passages yourself.
- **"This document follows the Save the Cat beats. Applying will turn the beats on for this novel so these passages have a home."**
- **The Done list says "kept whole, every passage searchable."** A planning document is always kept, even if you added no passages. That line is not an error.

After you click **Add the checked passages**, the Done page counts the passages added, by status word, and how many stay in the document, "where search and "Where did this come from?" still find them." Click **Open the Story Brain** to see them.

### Status words on imported passages

A **status word** is how Story Canon marks how sure you are about a fact: **Decided**, **Developing**, **Still open**, **Example**, or **Suggestion**. (You can rename these in Settings; see [Words for decisions](#words-for-decisions).)

When your document labels a passage, Story Canon reads the label exactly and turns it into the matching status:

| Your document says | It comes in as |
| --- | --- |
| Decided | **Decided** |
| Developing, or Decided in spirit | **Developing** |
| Still open, Open, Over to me, My move, Now I write | **Still open** |
| One way it could be done, Example, One way I could play it | **Example** |
| Here's a suggestion, Editorial possibility | **Suggestion** |
| Set aside | Set aside |

A passage with no label shows **No label** in the dropdown. For a character, an unlabeled passage comes in as **Developing**, with the note "No label in the document; it comes in as Developing. Change it if it is more or less settled."

You can change any passage's status in its dropdown before you bring it in. The label in your document is never changed.

**Why it matters.** A story bible is full of things you were sure of and things you were only trying out. If they all came in as plain text, a maybe would turn into a fact without anyone deciding it. Reading your own labels keeps each idea as settled as you said it was.

### The plotting spreadsheet preview

The preview shows:

- **Make this a beat sheet of my own.** Ticked, each row of the sheet becomes a beat, placed where the sheet puts it, and every novel can then use it, like Save the Cat. Unticked, the sheet only adds another set of placements for your existing beats.
- A line saying how many rows matched your Save the Cat beats. A row that did not match shows a **no match** chip.
- Warnings, such as a word count that does not agree with its page number: "Possible typo; 27,500 is suggested."
- **Use the numbers at 250 words per page** or **300 words per page**, when the sheet has both.
- **Use the suggested corrections for flagged numbers.**
- **Keep each beat's description**, so the sheet's notes stay with each beat as your own teaching.
- **Name for this placement choice**, the name it will have in lists.
- A table: **Row in the sheet**, **Beat**, **Words** at the chosen page density, and **Notes**.

Click **Use this sheet** (or **Make it my beat sheet**). The Done page tells you where to see it: Narrative Zoom, on the **Where beats fall** tab.

### The editor's changes preview

A line counts the changes, the scenes they are in, and any comments: "14 changes in 3 scenes, and 2 comments. Nothing changes until you decide. Anything you leave undecided stays as you wrote it."

- **Keep the editor's comments as notes beside each scene** (a tick box).
- For each changed scene: its name and chapter, **Accept all** and **Turn all down**.
- For each change: a few words before it, the removed words struck through, the added words highlighted, a few words after, and who made it and when. Then **Accept** and **Turn down**. Click a pressed button again to undo your choice.
- If you changed a scene yourself since you exported it, a note warns that "The editor's changes were made to the earlier text."
- If the editor changed text without Track Changes on, **Compare the editor's text with yours** opens the two side by side, with **Use the editor's version of this scene**.

Click **Make my decisions**. Each scene's earlier version is kept in its history, so you can still go back with [Earlier versions of a scene](#earlier-versions-of-a-scene).

## The words in the import lists

On **Bring something in**, under the four choices, two lists may appear.

**Waiting for you to look over** holds imports you opened but did not finish. Each shows its file name, its kind, and when you started it. Click the name to finish.

**Done** holds the last twenty finished imports. Each row shows the file name, its kind, and what became of it:

| Words | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **added** and a date | It was brought in on that date. |
| **kept whole, every passage searchable** | A planning document. It is in **Your documents** whether or not passages went into records. **Open it** opens it there. |
| **not brought in** | You chose **Don't import this**. Nothing was added. |
| **not confirmed** (a chip) | The word-for-word check after the import did not pass. Look at what came in before relying on it. |

## Your documents: every planning document, kept whole

**Your documents** lists every planning document you brought in, with how many passages it has and its date. Click a name to read it.

Inside a document:

- **All documents** goes back to the list.
- A line counts the passages, "kept word for word."
- **Find in this document** narrows the passages to ones containing what you type.
- Each passage shows where it sits (its headings and paragraph number), then its words.
- Under a passage that became part of a record, a chip names the record, the blank and its status, such as "Kevin Wickley, ghost: Decided."

**Why keep the whole document?** Because a passage you did not need today may matter later, and because **Where did this come from?** on any record can open the exact passage it came from.

## Send something out: making a file from your book

**Where:** the **Import and export** tab, then **Send something out**.

The page opens with "Exporting never changes your novel. Each file is read back after it is made and checked against your chapters, word for word." Then four areas from top to bottom: **Start from**, **What goes in**, **How it looks**, and the summary and **Export** button. **Your plotting sheet** is at the very bottom.

### Start from: the export presets

A **preset** is a ready-made set of choices for a common job. Picking one fills in everything below. You can then change anything; once you do, no preset is selected, which is fine.

| Preset | What it makes |
| --- | --- |
| **Working manuscript** | Word, every chapter, easy to read and edit. No title page. This is where the page starts. |
| **Editorial manuscript** | For an editor or agent: Word, double-spaced 12 point, title page, your name and title in the header, # between scenes. |
| **Beta reader copy** | Word with a title page. The story only. No planning notes, nothing private. |
| **Print review** | A Letter-size PDF to read and mark up on paper. |
| **E-book** | EPUB for an e-reader, with a title page. |
| **Character profiles** | Every character, field by field, with your status words. Word document. No manuscript. |
| **Planning binder** | Characters, beats, places, set pieces, open decisions and setups and payoffs, in Word with a title page. No manuscript. |

Presets you saved yourself appear here too, with a **Remove** link.

### What goes in

**The manuscript** (ticked at first) includes your chapters. Under it:

- **Version**, only when you have kept a draft: **The book as it is now**, or any kept draft "as kept." See [Drafts: keeping a whole version of the book](#drafts-keeping-a-whole-version-of-the-book).
- **Every chapter** or **Only the chapters I pick**. See the next section.

Then the planning sections, each a tick box. Tick any to add them after the manuscript (or on their own, with the manuscript unticked):

| Section | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| **Character profiles** | Every character, field by field. |
| **Beat sheet** | Your beats in order with what you planned for each. |
| **Places** | Every place record. |
| **Set pieces** | Your big planned sequences. |
| **Open decisions** | What is still undecided. |
| **Setups and payoffs** | Each clue and where it is planted and pays off. |
| **Timeline** | Your scenes in story order. |
| **Soundtrack** | Your songs and where they play. |
| **Every other record** | All remaining records. |

With any section ticked, two more choices appear:

- **Include examples, labeled "Example"**: ticked at first. Examples stay clearly marked so a reader never takes one for a decision.
- **Include private writing aids (casting, theme music, playlists)**: unticked at first. These are the private fields on a character page. Leave it unticked for anything you send to another person.

### Choosing chapters to export

1. Under **The manuscript**, click **Only the chapters I pick**.
2. A list of every chapter appears, numbered, all ticked, under "Untick any chapter to leave it out."
3. Untick the chapters you do not want.

Click **Every chapter** to go back to the whole book. The file's name says which chapters it holds, such as "I Declare War (chapters 1, 2, 3).docx". (A file with planning sections and no manuscript has "planning" in its name.)

**When to use it:** sending three chapters to a contest, or one chapter to your writing group.

### How it looks: format, headings, scene breaks

| Choice | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Format** | The kind of file. See the next section. |
| **Chapter headings** | How each chapter starts: **Number and title** ("Chapter 2: The Fog"), **Number only** ("Chapter 2"), or **Title only** ("The Fog"). The dropdown shows the style using one of your own chapter titles, or "Its Title" if your chapters have no titles of their own. A chapter with no title of its own just says "Chapter 2" in any style. |
| **Between scenes** | What prints where one scene ends and the next begins. It starts as `* * *`. Type anything up to twenty characters, such as `#`. |
| **Title page** | Adds a page with the title and the author name. Not offered for plain text, Markdown or data files. The name is your pen name if you set one (see [Author name on this book](#author-name-on-this-book)). |
| **Manuscript format** | Double-spaced, 12 point, name and title in the header, the layout agents and editors expect. Offered for Word, Rich Text, OpenDocument and PDF. |
| **Show scene titles** | Prints each scene's name above it. Off at first, since scene names are usually working labels. |

### Every export format

| Format | Best for | What it cannot keep |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Word (.docx)** | Editors, agents, critique partners, your own editing in Word. | Nothing. |
| **PDF** | Printing, or reading on any device. | It cannot be edited as text. A few rare characters may print as blanks; you are warned which. |
| **E-book (.epub)** | Kindle, Kobo, Apple Books and other e-readers. | Manuscript layout and page numbers. E-readers choose their own page size. |
| **OpenDocument (.odt)** | LibreOffice and other free word processors. | Nothing. |
| **Rich Text (.rtf)** | Older writing programs, and almost any word processor. | Nothing. |
| **Markdown (.md)** | Simple text with light formatting, for websites and notes apps. | Underline, centered paragraphs, page layout. |
| **Plain text (.txt)** | The simplest copy, readable anywhere. | Italics, bold, underline, headings and page layout. |
| **Web page (.html)** | Opening in a web browser or posting online. | Page layout and manuscript format. |
| **Story Canon data (.json), for other programs** | A structured copy for software, not for reading. | Not meant for people. To move a novel between computers, use an archive instead (see [Save an archive of this novel](#save-an-archive-of-this-novel)). |

### Before you export: the summary above the Export button

Just above the **Export** button, a short summary updates as you choose, such as "1 chapter and Character profiles, as Plain text (.txt)." Under it, shaded notes warn you about anything this particular file would lose or include by surprise:

- What the format cannot keep, counted for your book: "Plain text does not keep italics (12 passages). Your manuscript itself is unchanged."
- "2 notes left for later, [[in double brackets]], are still in the text and will print as written. They are listed on Where was I." A **note for later** is a reminder you typed inside your pages between double square brackets. They print exactly as typed, so clear them first or leave them on purpose for a working copy.
- "Part titles (such as "Part One") are not printed yet. The chapters export without them."

Read these before you click. They are the reason nothing surprises you after the file is sent.

### After Export: the check and where the file goes

1. Click **Export**. The button reads **Making the file…**.
2. In the Windows program, a save window opens in your Documents folder, inside **Story Canon**, then **Exports**. Choose a place and a name, and click **Save**.
3. A notice shows the file name, its size, and the result of the check, such as "Checked: the chapter is in the file, word for word."
4. Under it, one sentence says where the file went: "Saved to" and the exact path, with **Show it in its folder**, which opens that folder in Windows.

**If you close the save window without saving,** the notice says "The file was made but not saved, because the save window was closed," with **Save it**. Click it to choose a place after all.

**If the check fails,** the notice is shaded as a warning and names what is missing. Do not send that file; export again.

(In a test version of Story Canon running in a web browser, the file goes where the browser puts downloads, usually the Downloads folder, and the notice says so, with **Save it again**.)

### Saving your own preset

If you set up choices you will want again, such as "Contest entry: chapters 1 to 3, editorial format":

1. Make your choices.
2. Type a name in the box **Save these choices as…**, beside the **Export** button.
3. Click **Save**.

Your preset now appears under **Start from**, with a **Remove** link. Presets belong to this novel.

## Your plotting sheet

At the bottom of **Send something out** is **Your plotting sheet**: "Your beat sheet layout, filled in with this novel's length, both page densities, and what is planned at each beat." It is an Excel file with one row per beat, showing where each beat falls in words and pages, what you planned for it, and how many scene cards are placed there.

**Choose how beats are placed** in the dropdown:

- **Novel percentages (Brody)** places each beat at a percentage of a novel, from Jessica Brody's *Save the Cat! Writes a Novel*, which adapted the beats for novels.
- **Screenplay proportions (Snyder, 110 pages scaled)** uses Blake Snyder's original page numbers from *Save the Cat!*, which was written for a 110-page screenplay, scaled to your book's length.
- Any placement you brought in from a spreadsheet also appears, by the name you gave it.

If you are writing a novel and unsure, choose **Novel percentages (Brody)**.

Then click **Export the sheet (.xlsx)**. It saves the same way as any export, and the check says "Checked: all 15 beats are in the sheet."

**Where the numbers come from.** Word counts come from your **Target length in words** in Settings, and page counts from **Words per page for page estimates**. With no target set, the sheet uses the words you have drafted so far, and if you have drafted nothing, 90,000 words.

**This looks broken but is not:** a sheet full of tiny numbers, like "words 2" at the Midpoint, means there is no target length and the book has only a few hundred words drafted. Set a target in [Target length in words](#target-length-in-words) first.

The sheet needs a beat sheet. On a novel without one, Story Canon says "Choose a beat sheet for this novel first."
