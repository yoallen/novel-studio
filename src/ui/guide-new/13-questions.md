# Common questions

These are the questions new users actually ask, many of them at the moment something looks broken. Most of the time it is working as designed, and the answer is a sentence or two. Each answer names the exact buttons to use and links to the chapter that teaches the whole thing. Read this chapter when something surprises you, or skim it once after your first week to learn the things people most often trip over.

Press **F1** from anywhere, including the Library, to open Help, and type a word in **Search the guide** at the top of Help to find it in any chapter.

## Questions about finding your way

### Where is my novel's home page?

It is **Where was I**, the first view in the list on the right. The list shows the words "your novel's home page" under its name, and the top of the page says "This is (your title)'s home page." Story Canon opens every novel there, and it picks up where you left off: the next thing to write, how your words are going, notes you left for later, and questions still open. Whenever you lose your place, click **Where was I**. See [Where was I: your novel's home page](#where-was-i-your-novels-home-page).

### How do I get back to all my novels?

Click **Library** at the top left of any page. The Library lists every novel on this computer under **Your novels**. Click a title to open it. See [Your first hour: the Library and a new novel](#your-first-hour-the-library-and-a-new-novel).

### Where is the help for the screen I am on?

Press **F1**, or click **Help**, the last view in the list on the right. In the Library, before any novel is open, there is a **Help** button at the top right. Many screens also have a **Learn more** link that opens Help at the right section, and under many fields a **Why it matters** link teaches what that field is for. On a record, **Read the full chapter in Help** opens the reference for that kind of record (see [Every kind and every field](#every-kind-and-every-field)).

### I pressed Capture. Where did my thought go?

For a few seconds after you capture, a short line beside the **Capture** button says where the thought went; then it fades. Captured thoughts wait in the Story Brain under **Questions and ideas**: a thought in **Captured thoughts**, a question in **Questions I have**, an idea in **Things and ideas I'm thinking about**. Unsorted thoughts also show on **Where was I** under **Thoughts waiting to be sorted**, and a thought captured inside a scene also waits in that scene's plan, under **Attached to this scene**. See [The words Story Canon uses, and getting help](#the-words-story-canon-uses-and-getting-help).

### What is the difference between "Just a thought" and "An idea I am thinking about"?

A thought is unsorted: you catch it without deciding what it is. An idea is something you already know you might use. When in doubt, choose **Just a thought** and sort it later with **Make it a question** or **Make it an idea**. See [The words Story Canon uses, and getting help](#the-words-story-canon-uses-and-getting-help).

## Questions about records and their pages

### I opened a character and most of it is missing. Where is the rest?

It is on the other pages. A record is laid out like a notebook with numbered pages, and one page shows at a time. Look under the record's name for the row of numbered page tabs: a character has eleven, from **1 Who they are** to **11 Notes, guardrails and writing aids**. Click a tab to turn to that page, or click **All pages**, at the end of the row, to see everything on one long page. Links at the bottom of each page go to the page before and the page after. See [The words Story Canon uses, and getting help](#the-words-story-canon-uses-and-getting-help).

### Why are some page numbers blue and others not?

A page number turns ink blue once that page has something written on it. A number that is not blue means the page is still empty. It is a quick way to see, on Marisol Vega's record, that you have filled in **Who they are** and **Inner life** but not yet **Looks and voice**. See [The words Story Canon uses, and getting help](#the-words-story-canon-uses-and-getting-help).

### A page I expected is not there.

Pages with nothing to show are left out. Some pages are made only of fields that a method adds, and those appear once that method is turned on for the novel in Novel setup. If you are looking for something specific, click **All pages**, or use Search: it opens the record on the page that holds what you found. See [Your first hour: the Library and a new novel](#your-first-hour-the-library-and-a-new-novel) for Novel setup.

### I accepted a suggestion and the record jumped to another page.

That is on purpose. Suggestions waiting for a record sit above the page tabs, so you see them whichever page you are on. When you accept one, the record turns to the page where it landed, so you can see it in place. See [The words Story Canon uses, and getting help](#the-words-story-canon-uses-and-getting-help).

### What does "Low-energy day? One line counts" do?

It sits above the page tabs on a character. Click **Fill in one line**, and Story Canon picks one empty field for you, turns to the page it is on, and puts your cursor in it. One line is a real day's work on a tired day. See [The words Story Canon uses, and getting help](#the-words-story-canon-uses-and-getting-help).

### Why are some words blue, some gray, and some on a slip of paper?

The color of the words shows each fact's status, like ink on a page:

| Status | How it looks |
| --- | --- |
| **Decided** | Ink blue, with a small blue square. |
| **Developing** | Pencil gray, with a dashed underline. |
| **Still open** | A blank line with a gray prompt, waiting for you. |
| **Example** | Sepia italic, with a small boxed label reading "Example." Never a decision. |
| **Suggestion** | On a white slip, tucked in at a slight angle, in a handwriting typeface. Someone else's idea, waiting for you. |
| **Set aside** | Folded away under the field, out of sight. Click **Show earlier and set-aside versions** to see it, faded, with a line through it. Retired, but kept. |

The legend under a record's name shows a small sample of the first five, so you can glance at it whenever you forget which look is which. Set aside is not in the legend, because set-aside facts are folded away. See [The words Story Canon uses, and getting help](#the-words-story-canon-uses-and-getting-help).

### I renamed "Decided" in Settings. Did the blue ink change?

No. Renaming changes only the word. A fact that is Decided keeps its blue ink and small blue square whatever you call it. See [Settings: renaming the six statuses](#settings-renaming-the-six-statuses).

## Questions about saving and getting things back

### Do I have to press Save anywhere?

Almost never. Your pages save themselves within a second of typing; the top of the page says **Saved**. A scene's plan, a record's name and its notes save when you click away from them. A new fact on a record is kept when you click **Save** on its small form, which is part of adding it. Novel setup saves every choice as you make it.

The one exception is **Settings**, where most sections wait for **Save settings** at the bottom of the window. See [Settings: Save settings, and sections that take effect right away](#settings-save-settings-and-sections-that-take-effect-right-away).

### I changed a setting and it went back. Is Settings broken?

No. You most likely left the page without pressing **Save settings**. The line beside that button tells you: "You have changes not saved yet" means the change is not kept. Make the change again and press the button. See [Settings: Save settings, and sections that take effect right away](#settings-save-settings-and-sections-that-take-effect-right-away).

### I deleted a scene by mistake. Is it gone?

No. Deleting moves it to **Deleted things**. Click **Story Trail** in the list of views, then **Deleted things**, then **Restore** beside the scene. A deleted chapter comes back whole with **Restore with its scenes**. See [Finding things: Search and Story Trail](#finding-things-search-and-story-trail).

### I clicked Undo, but the old row is still in the Story Trail. Did it work?

Yes. The trail never erases history. Look at the original row: its text is now struck through and it says "Not in effect." A new row at the top, starting **Undid:**, has **Put it back** in case you change your mind. See [Finding things: Search and Story Trail](#finding-things-search-and-story-trail).

### Undo says something depends on it. What should I do?

Read the notice. It tells you what later work relies on the change. **Undo anyway** goes ahead; **Leave it** cancels. See [Finding things: Search and Story Trail](#finding-things-search-and-story-trail).

### Can I get back yesterday's version of a scene?

Yes. Open the scene, click **Earlier versions** at the bottom of its scene plan, click yesterday's time, and click **Restore this version**. Today's text stays in the list, so you can change your mind. See [The Writing Room](#the-writing-room).

### Earlier versions shows only one version, or none. Is it saving?

It is. Your typing is saved within a second, always. A separate *version* is made after a pause of about twenty seconds, when you leave the scene, and when you close the program. A short burst of typing makes one version. See [The Writing Room](#the-writing-room).

### Can I open one of the automatic backup files?

Not from inside Story Canon. Backups are whole-library safety copies for a computer that fails completely. To keep a copy of one novel that you can open yourself, use **Save an archive of this novel** in Settings, and bring it back from the Library under **Bring in an archive**. See [Settings: backups and archives](#settings-backups-and-archives).

### Where should my backups go?

Somewhere other than this computer: a second drive, a USB stick, or a OneDrive folder. In **Settings**, under **Backups**, click **Choose a folder**. A backup kept only on this computer is lost along with it. See [Backups: choosing where they go](#backups-choosing-where-they-go).

## Questions about two computers

### How do I set up Story Canon on a second computer?

Install it, open it, and stay in the Library. You do not need a novel open. Scroll to **Your other computers**, below **Your novels**, give this computer a name, click **Choose the shared folder**, and pick the same folder your first computer shares through. A list headed **On your other computers** appears; click **Bring to this computer** beside each novel. See [Sharing: setting up your second computer from the Library](#sharing-setting-up-your-second-computer-from-the-library).

### The Library on my new computer offers to bring in a novel. Should I click it?

Not if that novel is already on your other computer. Bringing it in that way makes a second, separate book. Turn on sharing in the Library first; once the other computer's copy is in the shared folder, the offer goes away, and **Bring to this computer** brings over the same book. See [Sharing: setting up your second computer from the Library](#sharing-setting-up-your-second-computer-from-the-library).

### How do I move a novel to a new computer?

Two ways. For a one-time move, click **Save an archive of this novel** in Settings, and bring it in on the new computer from the Library under **Bring in an archive** (see [Backups: getting a novel back from an archive](#backups-getting-a-novel-back-from-an-archive)). To keep writing on both, set up sharing (see [Your other computers: writing on two machines](#your-other-computers-writing-on-two-machines)).

### My other computer's work is not showing up.

Check four things:

1. Both computers chose the same shared folder. Each shows it under **Your other computers**, after "sharing through."
2. OneDrive has finished syncing on both.
3. The other computer has shared. It shares about every five minutes while there are changes, and when it closes. Click **Share now** there to send at once.
4. You clicked **Bring it in** on the notice above the page. With **Ask me before bringing in work from my other computer** ticked, nothing comes in until you do.

See [Sharing: bringing in work from your other computer](#sharing-bringing-in-work-from-your-other-computer).

### Story Canon asked me to choose between two versions. Will I lose one?

No. Both computers changed the novel before sharing, so it asks which one is the main version. The other is kept beside it in your Library, on both computers, named for where it came from. See [Sharing: when both computers changed a novel](#sharing-when-both-computers-changed-a-novel).

### A note I dictated on my phone never arrived.

The novel has to be open in Story Canon, on a computer that shares through that folder. The note arrives about a minute after the file stops changing and syncs, and a few minutes longer just after Story Canon starts or the computer wakes. Look under **Thoughts waiting to be sorted** on Where was I. See [Sharing: notes from the road](#sharing-notes-from-the-road).

## Questions about the collaborator

### The collaborator says "not set up." What do I do?

Add your Claude API key in **Settings**, under **The collaborator**, click **Use this key**, and tick **Use the collaborator for** this novel. If it says "off for this novel," the key is there and only the tick is missing. See [Conversations and the collaborator](#conversations-and-the-collaborator).

### Does the collaborator read my manuscript?

Only if you allow it, and only one scene at a time. Tick **Also share the prose of a scene when I ask about that scene** in Settings, then ask from the scene with **Ask about this scene**. Otherwise it sees your plans and records, never your pages. See [Conversations and the collaborator](#conversations-and-the-collaborator).

### Does the collaborator cost money?

Yes, a little per question, billed by Anthropic to your own API key. Longer questions and answers cost more, and **Opus** costs the most per question. Everything else in Story Canon works without it. See [Settings: the collaborator](#settings-the-collaborator).

### Does anything get sent anywhere without me knowing?

No. Nothing goes to the collaborator until you have set a key, ticked **Use the collaborator for** this novel, and asked a question. There is no background sending. (Sharing between your own computers, if you turn it on, writes only to the folder you chose.) See [Conversations and the collaborator](#conversations-and-the-collaborator).

### The collaborator raised a question I never asked. Did it change my story?

No. A question the collaborator raises is a suggestion. It waits in **Suggestions waiting** in the Story Brain, apart from your own questions, until you **Accept** it, say **Not yet**, or say **No**. See [Conversations and the collaborator](#conversations-and-the-collaborator).

### I said "sounds good" to an idea in the chat. Is it in my story now?

No. Agreeing in a conversation decides nothing. Accept the idea on purpose in **Suggestions waiting**, or select its words and use **Add as a possibility**. See [Conversations and the collaborator](#conversations-and-the-collaborator).

### My messages are signed with someone else's name.

Your messages are signed with **Your name** in Settings. A novel that came from someone else, such as an archive a friend sent or a book built from another person's planning, arrives with their name there. Type yours and press **Save settings**. See [Settings: Your name](#settings-your-name).

### I started from "Ask about this scene," but "Focus on…" shows nothing chosen.

That is fine. The scene goes along with every question anyway; the focus list holds records, and a page of prose is not a record. If the scene has a scene card, the card is chosen for you. See [Conversations and the collaborator](#conversations-and-the-collaborator).

### Why did the collaborator give a short answer when I wanted a full one?

Check two things: the way you asked (choose **Teach it** or **Critique** instead of **Talk it through**), and your guidance level (**Quiet** answers only what you asked). With the door closed, questions from a scene are always quiet. See [Settings: the guidance levels](#settings-the-guidance-levels) and [Conversations and the collaborator](#conversations-and-the-collaborator).

## Questions about conversations and search

### Why are there stars and pound signs in my imported chat?

ChatGPT and Claude use them for bold text and headings. Story Canon keeps every character exactly as it was written, so they stay. See [Conversations and the collaborator](#conversations-and-the-collaborator).

### My chat says it has hundreds of behind-the-scenes steps. Did something go wrong?

No. Those are the other app reading files, running tools and taking notes to itself. They are kept but hidden, because they are not conversation. Click **Show them** to see them faintly. See [Conversations and the collaborator](#conversations-and-the-collaborator).

### Harvest shows "None." under every heading. Is it broken?

No. Harvest gathers what *you* caught. It never picks things out for you. Go back to the conversation, select the words that matter, keep or file them, and Harvest fills up. See [Conversations and the collaborator](#conversations-and-the-collaborator).

### I selected some words but no bar appeared.

Your selection probably ran across two messages. Select within one message only. See [Conversations and the collaborator](#conversations-and-the-collaborator).

### I chose "Add as a possibility" on ChatGPT's words, and it arrived as a Suggestion, not Developing.

That is on purpose. Words an AI said always arrive as a Suggestion, so an AI's idea never becomes yours just by being selected. Accept it when you are ready. See [Conversations and the collaborator](#conversations-and-the-collaborator).

### If I bring in the same Claude or ChatGPT export twice, will I get duplicates?

No. Chats you already brought in say **already here** in the list. See [Conversations and the collaborator](#conversations-and-the-collaborator).

### Search did not find something I said in ChatGPT. Why?

Search only sees what is inside Story Canon. Bring the chat in first, from the Conversations view. See [Finding things: Search and Story Trail](#finding-things-search-and-story-trail).

### Search shows notes with codes I do not recognize.

They came in with planning documents you brought in. Detailed planning files often carry their own reference numbers and notes to self, and Story Canon keeps them word for word so you can always check where a fact came from. Click a code to see what it points to. Novels you plan inside Story Canon will not have them. See [Finding things: Search and Story Trail](#finding-things-search-and-story-trail).

### There are 176 results. How do I narrow them?

Use the row above the results: **Your pages**, **Story records**, **Conversations** or **Documents**. See [Finding things: Search and Story Trail](#finding-things-search-and-story-trail).

## Questions about importing and exporting

### I imported a planning document and nothing went in. Why?

Passages go in on their own only where a heading names a record you already have (or a beat). Look under **Staying in the document**, choose a record and a field for each passage you want, tick it, and click **Add the checked passages**. See [Bringing things in and sending things out](#bringing-things-in-and-sending-things-out).

### The Done list says "kept whole, every passage searchable." Did the import fail?

No. A planning document is always kept in **Your documents**, whether or not any passage went into a record. See [Bringing things in and sending things out](#bringing-things-in-and-sending-things-out).

### Where will my imported chapters go?

After your last chapter. The preview says so exactly, such as "the first one becomes chapter 2." You can move chapters later in the Writing Room. See [Bringing things in and sending things out](#bringing-things-in-and-sending-things-out).

### Where did my exported file go?

In the Windows program you choose the place, starting in Documents, Story Canon, Exports. After saving, the notice says "Saved to" and the full path, with **Show it in its folder**. See [Bringing things in and sending things out](#bringing-things-in-and-sending-things-out).

### My [[notes for later]] printed in my export.

Notes for later print exactly as typed. The summary above the **Export** button warns you how many are in the text. Answer or delete them first, or leave them on purpose in a working copy. See [Bringing things in and sending things out](#bringing-things-in-and-sending-things-out).

### Can I export only some chapters?

Yes. Under **The manuscript**, choose **Only the chapters I pick**, then untick the ones to leave out. See [Bringing things in and sending things out](#bringing-things-in-and-sending-things-out).

### My plotting sheet is full of tiny numbers.

The novel has no target length, so the sheet measures against the few words drafted so far. Set **Target length in words** in Settings, press **Save settings**, and export again. See [Settings: Target length in words](#settings-target-length-in-words).

### Which do I use, "Novel percentages (Brody)" or "Screenplay proportions (Snyder)"?

For a novel, use **Novel percentages (Brody)**. The Snyder numbers come from a 110-page screenplay, scaled to your book. See [Bringing things in and sending things out](#bringing-things-in-and-sending-things-out).

## Questions about settings, drafts and the book's shape

### The "Kept for you" marker shows the wrong name, or no name.

It uses your name from **About you**, or the first word of **Your name** in Settings. Fix either one and press **Save settings**. See [Settings: the Kept for you marker](#settings-the-kept-for-you-marker).

### What is the difference between "Mine to write" and "Mine to choreograph"?

Each names the kind of work waiting for you: writing it, staging the movement, timing the reveal, and so on. The collaborator drafts none of them. See [Settings: other markers for work you keep](#settings-other-markers-for-work-you-keep).

### The circle beside my scene does not match how much I wrote.

The circle follows the scene's **Status** step, not its word count. It is empty before the first step ticked **Counts as drafted**, half filled at that step, and full after it. Change the scene's **Status** in its scene plan. See [Settings: Counts as drafted, and the circles in the binder](#settings-counts-as-drafted-and-the-circles-in-the-binder).

### Is a scene's "Status" the same as Decided or Developing?

No. A scene's **Status** (Idea, Planned, Drafting and so on) says how far along its writing is. Decided, Developing and the other statuses say how settled a fact is. They are two separate lists. See [Settings: scene status steps](#settings-scene-status-steps).

### My book says "Plotting and planning" and "First draft" at the same time.

Both are right. The stage is where the whole book stands; the draft is which pass of the pages you are on. A book can be in planning while its first draft is open. See [Drafts: draft and stage are different](#drafts-draft-and-stage-are-different).

### I finished my draft too early. Can I take it back?

Yes. On the Drafts page, click **I finished first draft too soon**, then **Reopen it**. Your writing is not touched. See [Drafts: I finished the draft too soon](#drafts-i-finished-the-draft-too-soon).

### Why can't I click "Save this method"?

It needs a name. Fill in **Name** at the top. See [My methods: making a method, step by step](#my-methods-making-a-method-step-by-step).

### I made a method, but my novel does not use it.

Methods belong to you; each novel chooses its own. Tick it in Novel setup under **My storytelling toolkit**. See [My methods: turning your method on for a novel](#my-methods-turning-your-method-on-for-a-novel).

### Story Brain says "Beats 21," but the Board shows 15.

The Story Brain counts every beat record, including the smaller steps inside beats, such as Stasis = Death inside Setup and the five steps of the Finale. The Board shows the 15 main beats. See [Narrative Zoom](#narrative-zoom).

### My planned scenes do not show in the Writing Room. Where are they?

Scene cards you planned but have not written wait in the Writing Room's binder, below your chapters, under **Planned, not written yet**, each with **Write it**. Clicking **Write it** gives the scene a page in the right place. See [The Writing Room](#the-writing-room).

### The pink "Return to Writing" ribbon is not showing.

It appears once you have something to return to: after you have been on a page in the Writing Room, or when the last scene you opened has words on it. In a brand-new book with nothing written, there is nothing to return to yet. See [The Writing Room](#the-writing-room).
