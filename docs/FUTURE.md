## Later: series, companion novels and starter kits

Shaped by her catalog and her reading (King, Rowling, Jemisin, Barker, Gaiman, Flynn, Miller).

- **Series and shared universes.** Characters, places and world facts shared across books; clues planted in one book and paid off in another (Rowling); towns that return book after book (King); each book in a series with its own genres (Mr. Mercedes turning supernatural).
- **Companion novels.** The Wife I Divorced and The Husband I Divorced: the same events told from opposite sides, each book revealing what the other hid. Events shared across both books, with what each narrator knows.
- **Genre starter kits.** Choosing a genre suggests a fitting beat sheet and switches on its fields.
- **Typical length by genre and readership,** offered when she sets a target.
- **A style sheet for each book:** invented words, spellings, capitalization.
- **A mood board:** pictures and links for places, faces and atmosphere.
- **Parts from imports.** Bringing in a manuscript should turn its "Part One" headings into parts, not chapter notes.
- **Parts in exports.** Parts show in the Writing Room; exported books should also print a part page (Part One: Winter) before its first chapter.

## AI only if you invite it (closer to selling)

- **An "I don't use AI" setting.** Hides every trace of the collaborator: no Ask about this, no Conversations prompts, no AI wording anywhere. With no key the program already sends nothing; this makes that visible.
- **Visible AI layers, chosen one by one.** Two kinds, shown separately:
  - Permissions, each switched on separately: Help me brainstorm, Teach me, Show me examples, Critique my work, Read my pages.
  - Boundaries, promises the program keeps whatever else is on: Don't write my story (also turns off Show me examples), Don't decide for me (always true; shown so writers can see it).
  - "I don't use AI" is simply every permission off.
- Marketing line to keep: "AI optional," never "AI-free." The program was built with AI, and some built-in teaching text was drafted with it.

## The value square (after November; Yolanda's idea, 2026-10-26)

- McKee's forces of antagonism drawn as a cross with four corners, laid out as McKee draws them in Story (pp. 319 to 323): Positive upper left, Contrary upper right, Negation of the Negation lower left, Contradictory lower right. His arrows run from the Positive to each of the other three.
- His definitions: the Contradictory is the direct opposite (justice, injustice). The Contrary sits between, somewhat negative but not fully the opposite (unfairness). The Negation of the Negation is a compound negative, qualitatively worse, at the limit of the dark powers of human nature (tyranny; hatred masquerading as love). The story must move through all three to reach the limit of human experience.
- A template the writer fills in, then pulls up whenever she loses the thread of what the story is fighting over.
- One square per value: each storyline can have its own (the A story and the B story each get one), and a novel can hold several.
- Builds on what exists: the character page's "What works against them" fields (McKee) already hold the value and its worst form.
- Open questions for her before any design: does a square belong to a storyline, a character, or the book; what does it show beside each corner (scenes, beats, characters standing there); where does it open from. Mockup and her approval first.

## Teaching in the moment (after November; Yolanda, 2026-11-09)

- Her point: most writers will start using Story Canon without reading the guide, then hit a wall. Help should arrive at the moment of need, inside the program, so there is no "record scratch."
- "Common Things You Can Do In This Room": a short list in each room (the Writing Room's would sit with the Binder), written as jobs in the writer's words ("Start a chapter here", "Move a scene", "Bring back something I deleted"), each opening a mini tutorial. Her idea.
- Show me, on her own book: a pen arrow points to the real button and walks the steps. First-time welcomes once per room, with "show me again". A practice book (The Keeper of Gull Point) to experiment in freely. A quiet "Looking for this?" when someone seems stuck. Help that understands a question in plain words, with or without AI. Thirty-second videos placed beside the feature, also used for the outside video library.
- Rule: help offers, never blocks, never interrupts writing; door closed silences it. Mockups and her approval first.
- "Remind me" questions on the same list (her idea): story memory, not how-to. "Remind Me, Who Are My Characters?", "Where did I leave this storyline?", "What does she know right now?", "What did I decide about...", "What did I promise the reader?", "What comes next in my plan?" Each answers in a peek beside the page (no detour, so the Writing Trail stays clean) and links to the place in Story Canon where the full answer lives: Story Brain, Narrative Zoom, Continuity checks.
- Timing: she wants this in December, after the November draft.
