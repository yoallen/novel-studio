# The Writing Room

**The Writing Room** is where you write your book. It is the second tab on the right edge of the window. Everything else in Story Canon (the Story Brain, Narrative Zoom, conversations) exists to serve what happens here: words going onto the page.

This chapter explains every part of the room, how your writing is saved, and the tools above the page for the moments when writing gets hard: reading it back, being stuck, stepping away, and writing against a clock.

## The Writing Room at a glance

The room has three columns and a row of links above the page.

| Where | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Left: the binder** | Your chapters and scenes in order, with word counts and a small circle for each scene's progress. Below them, **Planned, not written yet**: scene cards from your plan that have no page |
| **Middle: the page** | The open scene: its title, your text, and a line underneath with the scene's word count and today's goal |
| **Right: the side panel** | Usually the **Scene plan** for the open scene. It can show **Read it back** instead |
| **Above the page: the links** | **Hide scene plan** (or **Show scene plan**), **Read it back**, **Show in Narrative Zoom**, **I'm stuck**, **Ask about this scene**, **Writing Trail**, **Sprint** and **Focus** |
| **Top of the window** | The **Saved** indicator, beside the **Capture** button |

If no scene is open, the page says *Choose a scene on the left, or add one, and start writing.*

## Getting into the Writing Room

There are four ways in, and each lands you in a slightly different place.

| You click | Where you land |
| --- | --- |
| **The Writing Room** tab | The scene you had open. If none, the scene Next points to. If still none, the scene you worked on most recently, or a new blank scene |
| **Write** on Where was I | The scene named in **Next**, making its page first if it is a planned scene (see [What the Write button does](#what-the-write-button-does)) |
| **Return to Writing**, the pink ribbon at the top left (or **Ctrl+Shift+W**) | The scene you were writing in, with the cursor exactly where you left it. This also closes your open detours |
| A scene in the binder, a note in Left for later, or **Open the page** in Narrative Zoom | That scene |

When a scene opens, the page shows *Opening the page…* for a moment, then puts your cursor back where it was the last time you were in that scene. Wait for the page to appear before typing, so nothing you type lands before the page is ready.

## The page: writing, titles and word counts

**The title.** The scene's name sits at the top of the page in purple. Click it to change it, then press **Enter** or click anywhere else. If you empty it, the old name comes back: a scene always has a name.

**The text.** Click under the title and type. A brand-new scene shows the grey words *Write anything. A sentence counts.* until you start. Bold and italic work with **Ctrl+B** and **Ctrl+I**, and **Ctrl+Z** undoes your last bit of typing.

**The word line.** Under the text is a small grey line, for example *63 words in this scene, today's goal 1,743*, and on the right, *Ctrl+Shift+L leaves a note for later*. Today's goal only shows once you have set one (see [Goals, November and catching up](#goals-november-and-catching-up)). Notes for later are never counted in the scene's words.

**The binder's totals.** At the top of the binder is the whole book's word count (and "of" your target length, if you set one). Each chapter shows its total on the right, and each scene its own count.

**If the scene is kept for you.** When a scene is marked in **Kept for you to write or decide** (see below), the marker, such as *Mine to write*, appears just above the title, so you see it every time you open the page.

## How saving works in the Writing Room

There is no Save button for your writing, and you never need one. Here is exactly what happens, so you can trust it.

### The Saved indicator

At the top right of the window, beside **Capture**, is a small dot and a word. It only appears while you are in the Writing Room.

| What it says | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| A filled dot and **Saved** | Everything you have typed is safe on this computer |
| A hollow dot and **Not saved yet** | You are typing. It changes to Saved within about a second of a pause |
| Red **Not saved** | Something went wrong saving. Keep the window open and keep typing; Story Canon tries again with every pause |
| Red **Not saved: service unreachable** | The part of Story Canon that stores your work is not answering. Keep the window open; do not close it until it says Saved |

### When a stretch of typing becomes a saved version

Saving happens in two layers.

1. **Within a second of every pause**, your typing is written to a safety copy on this computer, and the indicator says **Saved**. Even a power cut now loses nothing.
2. **After about twenty seconds without typing, when you leave the scene, or when you close the window**, that typing becomes a **saved version** of the scene: a permanent step in its history, listed under **Earlier versions**.

**Looks wrong, but isn't.** A few things wait for the saved version, not the safety copy, so they can lag behind your typing by up to twenty seconds:

- **Today's words** on Where was I, and the drafting progress columns.
- **This week** on Where was I.
- **The status change** from a not-started step to Drafting (see [Status: the steps a scene moves through](#status-the-steps-a-scene-moves-through)).
- **Left for later** on Where was I, for a note you just typed.

If a number seems stuck, pause for twenty seconds or click another scene, and it catches up. Your words were safe the whole time.

### Kept typing after the program closed

If Story Canon closes before your latest typing became a saved version (a crash, a power cut), the safety copy is still there. Two things happen:

- **Where was I** shows *Unsaved typing was kept for "the scene"*, with a button to open it.
- **When you open that scene**, a box appears over the page titled *Typing from (date and time) was kept*. It explains: *The program closed before this typing became a saved version. Nothing has been lost.* It shows the end of the **Kept typing**.

Choose one:

- **Use the kept typing** makes the kept words the scene's text.
- **Keep the saved version** keeps what was saved before.

The one you don't choose is kept in **Earlier versions**, so this choice can never lose anything. The page will not take typing until you choose. That is on purpose: it means nothing can overwrite the kept typing by accident.

### The same scene open in two windows

If the same scene is open in two Story Canon windows and you type in both, the second window to save would overwrite the first. Story Canon never lets that happen silently. Instead, a box appears: *This scene was changed in another window. Nothing has been overwritten. Choose what to keep.* It shows the end of the other window's version, and three buttons:

- **Keep both as separate scenes** keeps the other window's text in this scene, and makes a new scene right after it, named *(the scene's name) (my version)*, holding this window's text. You can compare them and merge by hand.
- **Keep this window's** saves what you see in this window.
- **Keep the other window's** replaces what you see with the other window's text.

**The common mistake** is clicking the first button that looks safe without reading. If you are not sure which version is better, **Keep both as separate scenes** is the one that never loses a word.

### Earlier versions of a scene

At the bottom of the scene plan is a button, **Earlier versions**. It opens a list of every saved version of the scene, each with its date, time and word count.

1. Click a version on the left. The right side shows it compared with the current text: **struck through** words are only in the earlier version, **highlighted** words are only in the current one.
2. To go back to it, click **Restore this version**. The current text stays in the list, so restoring is never a loss.
3. Click **Close** to leave without changing anything.

Restoring a version is not writing, so it never counts toward your words for the day.

## The binder: chapters and scenes

The **binder** is the left column. It lists your chapters, each with its scenes, in book order. Click a scene to open it. The open scene is marked with a purple bar down its left side.

### Adding chapters and scenes

- **Add scene**, under each chapter, adds a new scene at the end of that chapter, named *Scene 2*, *Scene 3* and so on, and opens it.
- **Add chapter**, the button at the bottom of the chapters, adds a new chapter at the end of the book with one empty scene in it, and opens that scene.

### Renaming chapters and scenes

- **A chapter:** double-click its name in the binder, type the new name, and press **Enter** (or **Esc** to cancel).
- **A scene:** click its title at the top of the page, type, and press **Enter**.

### Parts: grouping chapters

A **part** is a group of chapters with its own heading, such as *Part One: Winter*. Many novels never need parts. Use them when your book has big movements, separated in time or place.

1. Under the chapter where the part should begin, click **Start a part here**.
2. Type the part's name, and press **Enter**.

The part's name appears in slanted purple type above that chapter. The part runs from that chapter until the next part heading. To rename it, double-click the part's name. Under the chapter that starts a part, the link changes to **End the part here**. Clicking it takes the part heading away from that chapter, so the chapters from there on are no longer a separate part.

### Status circles in the binder

Before each scene's name is a small circle. It shows, at a glance, how far along the scene is:

| Circle | Meaning | Statuses, with the usual steps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Empty | Not started | Idea, Planned, Ready to draft |
| Half filled | Being written | Drafting |
| Filled | Written | First draft complete, Revising, Revised, Finalized |

Point at a circle to see the scene's status in words, for example *Drafting: being written*. The circle follows the **Status** you choose in the scene plan, and the scene plan shows this same key under its Status dropdown. If you change the status steps in Settings, the circles follow your steps (see [Changing the status steps](#changing-the-status-steps)).

### Moving a scene

The open scene has three links under its name: **↑ Up**, **↓ Down** and **Delete**.

- **↑ Up** and **↓ Down** move the scene one place. At the top or bottom of a chapter, they move it into the chapter before or after.
- To move a different scene, click it first, so it is the open one.

Chapters themselves can't be moved in the binder.

### Deleting a scene or a chapter

- **Delete**, under the open scene, removes that scene. Story Canon asks first: *Move "the scene" to Deleted? You can restore it from Story Trail, under Deleted things.*
- **Delete chapter**, under each chapter, removes the chapter and every scene in it, and asks the same way, naming how many scenes go with it.

Nothing deleted is really gone. Open **Story Trail** and click **Deleted things** to put it back, whole.

### Planned, not written yet, and Write it

Below the chapters is a section headed **Planned, not written yet**. It lists the scene cards in your plan (in Narrative Zoom) that don't have a page in the manuscript yet, in story order. Each shows the card's name and, in slanted type, the beat it sits on, or *might go at* a beat if it isn't settled there yet.

**What it's for.** Your plan and your manuscript are two views of the same book. This list is the bridge: it shows what you planned and haven't written.

**How to use it.** Click **Write it** beside a card. Story Canon makes the scene's page, puts it in the right place among your chapters, and opens it. The card leaves this list, and its plan comes with it: you will see it in the scene plan under **From the card**.

**The common mistake** is clicking **Add scene** and retyping a planned scene's name. That makes a second, unconnected scene. Use **Write it**, so the page and the card stay one scene.

**Example.** A cozy mystery has twenty planned scene cards and no prose. The author opens the Writing Room and sees all twenty under Planned, not written yet. She clicks **Write it** beside *The bakery break-in*. A page called *The bakery break-in* appears in Chapter 1, with the card's plan beside it, and she starts typing.

## The scene plan panel

The right column is the **Scene plan**: your plan for the open scene, beside the page. A grey line under the heading says *Everything here saves by itself when you click away. Earlier versions are kept.* When you change a blank and click elsewhere, a small green **Saved** appears beside its label for a moment.

The scene plan and the scene card in Narrative Zoom are the same plan, seen from two places. What you type in one shows in the other. Every field is explained in full, with examples, in the reference chapter: [Scene cards: every field](#scene-cards-every-field).

### Showing and hiding the scene plan

**Hide scene plan**, the first link above the page, gives the page the whole width. It changes to **Show scene plan** to bring it back. Story Canon remembers your choice the next time you open the room.

### Left for later in the scene plan

If the open scene has notes for later in it, the scene plan starts with **Left for later** and the number, listing just this scene's notes. Click one to select it on the page. When the scene has no notes, this section doesn't appear.

### From the card

If the scene has a card in your plan with something on it, **From the card** shows it:

- **Beat:** the beat the card sits on, *Might go at* one or more beats, or *Not on a beat yet*.
- **Every other field** filled in on the card, such as *What happens* or *Planning notes*.
- **Open the full card** opens the card in Narrative Zoom, where you can change any of it.

### Status: the steps a scene moves through

**Status** is a dropdown with the steps a scene goes through. The usual steps are:

| Step | Meaning | Circle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Idea | You know a scene goes here, roughly | Empty |
| Planned | You have planned what it must do | Empty |
| Ready to draft | The plan is enough to write from | Empty |
| Drafting | You are writing it | Half |
| First draft complete | It has a whole first draft | Filled |
| Revising | You are reworking it | Filled |
| Revised | The revision is done | Filled |
| Finalized | It is finished | Filled |

Under the dropdown, a small key shows which steps give which circle, with the open scene's line highlighted.

**It moves by itself once.** When the first words of a not-started scene are saved, its status moves to the first drafting step (Drafting) by itself. After that, every move is yours: choose **First draft complete** when the draft is done, and so on.

**Why it helps.** Status is how you see the shape of your progress across the whole book: which scenes are only ideas, which are drafted, which are done.

### Viewpoint character

**Viewpoint character** is a dropdown of the characters in your Story Brain. Choose the character whose eyes and thoughts this scene is told through. **Not set** means none chosen. If you have no characters yet, it says *Add characters in the Story Brain.*

### Kept for you to write or decide

This dropdown marks the scene as yours alone in some way. The collaborator may talk about a marked scene, but it never writes or decides that part for you. The choices are:

| Choice | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Not marked** | Nothing kept |
| **(Your name), it's on you now** | Handed back to you: the collaborator offers nothing more here unless you ask |
| **Mine to write** | You write this one. The collaborator never drafts it |
| **Mine to build** | You work out how it is built: the mechanics, the setup, how the pieces fit |
| **Mine to design** | You design it: how it looks, the space, the shape of the gag or the trap |
| **Mine to choreograph** | You stage the movement: who goes where, the fight, the chase, the physical comedy |
| **Mine to time** | You set the timing: the pacing, when a reveal lands, the beat before the punchline |
| **Mine to escalate** | You decide how it builds, and how big it gets before it breaks |
| **Mine to settle** | You decide the open question here. Suggestions wait; nothing is settled for you |

Your own name in the first choice comes from **About you** in Settings, or from **Your name** at the top of Settings. You can rename these markers and add your own in **Settings**, under **Words for decisions**.

### This draft is settled: the page wins over the plan

A checkbox. Tick it once the scene reads the way you want. From then on, where the page and the plan disagree, the page is the truth and the old plan becomes history.

**Why it matters.** Plans change in the writing. Maybe you planned for Kevin to win the argument and, on the page, Anthony did. Ticking this box tells Story Canon (and the collaborator) to believe the page.

**When to tick it.** When you have finished a draft of the scene you are happy with, not while you are still finding it.

### Who is here, and how old

A section headed **Who is here** appears when the scene has a **Year**, or when characters connected to the scene have birth years or fixed ages. It lists each character who appears in the scene with their age that year, or *age not known yet*. If the year is set but no characters are connected, it says *Connect characters to this scene (appears in) to see their ages.*

### Drafting readiness

**Drafting readiness** lists what is still open in the plan, item by item, each marked **Open:** or **Done:**. The items are: Viewpoint character; What this scene must do; The viewpoint character's objective; Central conflict; Intended outcome or change; and How we got here or what must follow. When everything is done, it says *Ready to draft.* Otherwise it says *Still open below. You can write anyway.*

**It is information, never a gate.** You can write any scene at any time, planned or not. The list is there for when a scene stalls: often the reason is one of these blanks.

### The plan's blanks

Below readiness are the plan's own fields. Each has a grey hint under it.

| Field | What to put |
| --- | --- |
| **What this scene must do** | The job it does for the book. If you cut it, what would the story lose? |
| **The viewpoint character's objective** | What the viewpoint character is trying to get or do here |
| **Central conflict** | Who or what is in the way, and who wins |
| **Intended outcome or change** | How things stand differently when the scene ends |
| **What you want the reader to feel** | Funny, shocking, profound, relatable, or your own word |

Then, under the heading **The turn, and around it**:

| Field | What to put |
| --- | --- |
| **Value at the start** | What matters to the character here, and whether it is going well (+) or badly (-). For example: Trust, + |
| **Value at the end** | The same value as the scene ends. A change from + to - (or back) is the scene turning |
| **How we got here** | The causes: what earlier events lead to this scene, not only the scene before it |
| **What must follow** | The consequences: what has to happen later because of this scene |
| **When (story time)** | When it happens, in your own words: the morning after the funeral, day 3 of the feud |
| **Year** | The calendar year as a number, such as 1987. With birth years on characters, it shows everyone's age here |
| **Where** | Type a place, or pick one of your Places from the list that appears as you type |

What you type is kept in pencil (developing) until you decide it on the card; a label shows *(developing)* beside a field in that state. For the full teaching of each field, with the common mistake and examples, see [Scene cards: The scene](#scene-cards-the-scene) and [Scene cards: Around it](#scene-cards-around-it).

### Attached to this scene

When you capture a thought, question or idea while writing in this scene, and leave **Attach to this scene** ticked, it waits here. Each item shows its kind (**Question**, **Idea** or **Thought**), *needed to keep writing* if you said so, and its text. The buttons depend on the kind:

- A question: **Answered**, or **No longer relevant**.
- An idea: **Adopt**, or **Set aside**.
- A thought: **Done**.

Each button takes the item off this list. It stays in the Story Brain with its new state. When nothing is attached, the section says *Nothing open. Capture a thought with Ctrl+Shift+N and it will wait here.*

### Scene notes and Earlier versions

**Scene notes** is a free box for anything about the scene that isn't story: a reminder to check a fact, a line you might use. It saves when you click away. Below it are the **Earlier versions** button (see [Earlier versions of a scene](#earlier-versions-of-a-scene)) and a reminder that moving and deleting the scene happen in the binder.

## Changing the status steps

Your scene steps are yours to change.

1. Click **Settings** on the right edge, and find **Scene status steps**.
2. Rename a step by typing in its box. Use **↑** and **↓** to reorder, **Remove** to take one away, and **Add a step** for a new one.
3. Tick **Counts as drafted** for every step where the scene has prose.
4. Click **Save settings** at the bottom.

**How the circles follow.** Steps before the first one ticked **Counts as drafted** give an empty circle. The first ticked step gives a half circle. Every ticked step after it gives a filled circle. (The grey help line in Settings describes this differently; the binder and the key under the Status dropdown show how it really works.)

## Read it back: hearing and checking the page

**Read it back**, above the page, swaps the scene plan for a panel that reads your scene aloud and points out three kinds of things worth a second look. It never changes a word. Click **Read it back** again to close the panel, then **Show scene plan** to bring the plan back.

### Hear it

Your ear catches what your eye skims: rhythm, repetition, a joke's timing, a voice that slips.

- **Read the scene** reads from the top. **From the cursor** starts where your cursor is.
- While it reads: **Pause**, then **Go on**; or **Stop**.
- **Default voice** is a dropdown of the voices on this computer. **Speed** is a slider.
- **Follow along**, when ticked, selects each word on the page as it is read, so you can see where you are.

### Echoes

An **echo** is the same word (four letters or longer, not a common little word, not a character's or place's name) used twice or more within about 120 words. Each echo shows as a small chip, such as *proof ×2*. Click a chip to select the first use on the page; click again for the next. Sometimes a repeat is rhythm and you keep it. Sometimes it is an echo and you change one.

### Your watch words

**Watch words** are words you know you lean on too often. Story Canon starts you with a starter list many writers use (*just, really, very* and 15 more), and says so. Any that appear on the page show as chips with a count; click to step through them. **Change the list** opens a box: type one word or phrase per line, or separate them with commas, and click **Save**. Once you change it, the list is yours, for this novel.

### Long sentences

Any sentence over 40 words is listed with its length and its first words. Click one to select it on the page. A long sentence is not wrong; it is worth reading aloud once.

When the door is closed (see [Door closed while you draft](#door-closed-while-you-draft)), Echoes, watch words and Long sentences wait, and a line says so, with **Look anyway** to see them on purpose. **Hear it** always works.

## I'm stuck: one small next step

**I'm stuck** opens a box that offers one small thing to do, so the blank page becomes a single sentence.

**What it shows.** If your book has a beat sheet, the box shows one beat:

- A line at the top: *Read only this beat. Do not look at the others.* (If the open scene sits at this beat, the line starts by saying so.) The point is to shrink the problem to one beat.
- The beat's number and name, such as **8. Fun and Games**.
- **What this beat does**: the teaching for that beat, and sometimes a short example from a published book or film, marked as an example.
- **One sentence of what happens at this beat**: a box for one sentence, and **Save the sentence**. Your sentence is saved in pencil on that beat, under *What it is (your plan)*, and a note confirms where, with **See it there** to open the beat.
- **Or the next smallest step:** **Fill in one line about a character** (opens the Story Brain), or **Write one line in a narrator's voice, complaining about anything** (opens Capture).
- **A different beat**, a dropdown at the bottom right, if you would rather think about another beat. **Close** closes the box.

**Where it starts.** Opened from a scene that sits at a beat (or might go at one), it starts at that beat. Otherwise it starts at the first beat.

**If the book has no beat sheet**, the box says *Start here: Write one sentence of anything. It counts.* with a button that opens Capture.

**The common mistake** is reading all fifteen beats looking for inspiration. That is the opposite of the tool. One beat, one sentence.

## Ask about this scene

**Ask about this scene** opens a new conversation with the collaborator (Story Canon's AI writing partner), titled *About the scene (the scene's name)*, already focused on this scene, so the collaborator reads its plan. If the collaborator isn't turned on yet, the conversation says so, with a link to turn it on in Settings; nothing is sent anywhere until you do.

Asking leaves the page, so it is a detour: use **Return to Writing** to come back.

## Show in Narrative Zoom

**Show in Narrative Zoom** opens Narrative Zoom on this scene's own page: its placement on a beat, the characters in it, the start of its text, and its full plan. It is how you see where this scene sits in the shape of the whole book. From there, **Open the page** brings you back.

**If the scene is not on your plan yet**, a note appears above the page instead: *This scene is not on your plan yet, so Narrative Zoom has no place to show it. Fill in any line of its scene plan and it becomes a card you can put on a beat.* It offers **Open Narrative Zoom anyway** and **Close**. This is not an error: a scene you started with **Add scene** has no card until it has a plan.

## Focus: only the page

**Focus**, the last link above the page, hides everything but the page: the binder, the scene plan, the links, the tabs, the story strip across the top, and the header with the Saved indicator. Only your scene, its title and its word line remain.

- Press **Ctrl+Shift+F** to turn Focus on or off from the keyboard.
- Press **Esc**, or click **Leave focus** at the top right, to come back.
- **Ctrl+Shift+N** still opens Capture, for a thought that will not wait.

**When to use it.** When the screen itself is the distraction: you keep glancing at the word count, the binder, the open questions.

**Focus or a sprint?** Focus has no clock and no end; it lasts until you leave. A sprint (below) is Focus with a clock and a count at the end.

## The Writing Trail and detours

Writing gets interrupted. You check a character's eye color in the Story Brain, look at a beat in Narrative Zoom, read a conversation. Each of these is a **detour**: a time you left the page while writing. Story Canon keeps a record of each one, called the **Writing Trail**, so stepping away never costs you your place.

### What counts as a detour

Leaving the Writing Room for any other tab, while a scene is open, records a detour named for where you went, such as *Went to Story Brain*. Opening the **Writing Trail** itself is not a detour, because the trail is part of the Writing Room. Opening **Capture** is not a detour either, because it opens over the page instead of leaving it, and neither is pressing **F1** for Help (clicking the **Help** tab is).

### Returning to writing

Whenever you are away from the page with a scene open, a pink ribbon, **Return to Writing**, hangs at the top left of the window. Click it, or press **Ctrl+Shift+W** from anywhere, and you land back in the scene you left, cursor and all. It also closes every open detour.

Clicking **The Writing Room** tab takes you back to the page too, but it does not close detours. That is why **Detours still open** on Where was I can keep listing a detour after you came back: it closes the next time you use the ribbon or Ctrl+Shift+W.

### The Writing Trail page

Click **Writing Trail** above the page (or under **Detours still open** on Where was I). The page lists your detours, newest first. Each shows:

- **Where you went**, in bold, and the date and time.
- A grey status line: *Returned to writing* or *Not returned yet*, and *resolved* or *not resolved*.
- A box, *What came of it, if you like: an answer, a decision*, for a note about what the detour was for.
- **Mark resolved** (or **Mark unresolved**), and **Go back to that scene**.

**Returned and resolved are two different things.** *Returned* means you came back to the page. *Resolved* means you did what you went to do. You might return without finding the answer, and resolve it later.

**To keep a note about a detour:**

1. Type in **What came of it**: *Checked the outline: the party is on a Saturday*.
2. Click **Mark resolved**. The note is saved with it. (Typing in the box alone does not save it; the button does.)

**Why it helps.** A week later, the trail answers "Why did I change that?" and "Did I ever check the date of the party?" without searching.

## Notes for later: keep writing, fix it later

A **note for later** is a reminder you leave inside the page itself, between double square brackets: `[[what year did her husband die?]]`. You type it and keep going.

**Why it works.** The fastest way to lose a writing day is to stop and look something up. A question about a date becomes twenty minutes of research, and by the time you come back the scene has gone cold. A note keeps the question without stopping the scene.

**How to leave one.**

- Type `[[`, your note, then `]]`.
- Or press **Ctrl+Shift+L**. It puts in the brackets with the cursor between them, ready to type. When the note is done, press the right arrow key twice to step past the closing brackets (or **End**, if you were writing at the end of the paragraph) and keep writing.
- To turn words you already wrote into a note, select them (within one paragraph) and press **Ctrl+Shift+L**. They are wrapped in brackets, exactly as written.

A note can't contain square brackets or run across a line break.

**What happens to it.**

- On the page, a note looks like pencil in the margin: grey, slanted, on a faint shaded background, so it never reads as story. Point at it to see *A note for later. It is not counted as story words.*
- **It is not counted.** Notes never add to a scene's words, a day's words or your push total.
- It is listed under **Left for later** in the scene plan (this scene's notes) and on Where was I (every note in the book, in book order).
- Clicking a note in either list opens its scene with the note selected. Type the answer, and it replaces the note.
- A note leaves the lists when its brackets leave the page.
- Notes are printed exactly as written if you export with them still in the text, so clear them before you send the book out.

**The common mistake** is answering the note right away "since it's only a minute." It is rarely a minute. Leave it.

**Example.** You are writing Kevin and Anthony's hedge war and can't remember what day the block party falls on. You press Ctrl+Shift+L, type *what day of the week is the block party?*, press End, and go straight on to the raccoon on the mailbox. On Saturday morning, Left for later shows the note with the words you wrote just before it. You check your planning, click the note, type *Saturday*, and it is gone.

## Sprints: a set time of nothing but the page

A **sprint** is a stretch of focused writing against a clock. While it runs, everything but the page goes away.

**Why it works.** A deadline you can see makes starting easier: you only have to write until the clock stops, not finish the chapter. Taking everything else off the screen removes the small temptations, like checking the word count or renaming a scene.

### Starting a sprint

1. Click **Sprint** above the page.
2. Choose **15 minutes**, **30 minutes** or **60 minutes**, or type a number from 1 to 240 in **Other**.
3. Leave **Fill the whole screen** ticked for the window to fill the screen, or untick it to keep the window as it is.
4. Click **Start the sprint** (or press **Enter** in the Other box).

Story Canon remembers your length and screen choice for next time. If no scene was open, the sprint opens the one you would get from **The Writing Room** tab.

### During a sprint

Gone: the binder, the scene plan, the links, the tabs, the story strip, the header, notices, the word line and red spelling underlines. Keys that lead away from the page (search, Return to Writing, Help) do nothing until the sprint ends.

Still there:

- **A small, faint clock** in the bottom right corner, in slanted type: *30 min*, then *Under a minute*. Click it to show **Pause** and **End the sprint**. While paused, it says *Paused* and the time left; click it and choose **Resume**.
- **Ctrl+Shift+N** still opens Capture.
- **A save problem** is the one thing that breaks the quiet: if your writing can't be saved, a warning appears beside the clock.
- **A notice about newer work from your other computer**, if you write on more than one, because ignoring it would cost you later.

### When the time is up

Nothing stops you mid-sentence. A note appears at the bottom: **Time.** *(N) words in (N) minutes. Finish your sentence; nothing has stopped.* Then choose:

- **Keep going**: the page stays quiet, with no clock. The corner says *Still writing*; click it and choose **Done** when you finish.
- **Another sprint**: opens the sprint box again.
- **Done**: ends the sprint and brings everything back.

If you end a sprint early, the note says **Sprint over.** with your count. If you cut more than you wrote, it says so and adds *That is writing too.* If you wrote nothing, it says *Time with the page counts too.* Sprint words count toward your day like any others.

**The common mistake** is choosing 60 minutes on a tired day and feeling beaten at minute 20. On a hard day, choose 15. A small sprint you finish beats a big one you abandon.

## Door closed while you draft

**Door closed** is a setting that stops Story Canon from judging your draft until a day you choose. The phrase is Stephen King's: the first draft is written with the door closed, for you alone, and the door opens for revision.

**What changes in the Writing Room while the door is closed:**

- No red spelling underlines on the page.
- The *Ctrl+Shift+L leaves a note for later* hint is hidden.
- **Read it back** still reads aloud, but Echoes, watch words and Long sentences wait, with **Look anyway** if you want them.

Elsewhere, Narrative Zoom's story review and misspelled names wait too, and the collaborator doesn't point out problems unless you ask. Your plans, notes, search and reading aloud all stay.

**How to close it.** Under your writing push on Where was I, click **Close the door through (the push's last day)**, or open **Settings**, find **Door closed**, choose a day, and click **Close the door**. It opens by itself the day after. More in [Closing the door from the progress panel](#closing-the-door-from-the-progress-panel).

## Capture while writing

**Capture** catches a thought without losing your place. Click **Capture** at the top right, or press **Ctrl+Shift+N** (it works in Focus and during a sprint too).

1. Type the thought, exactly as it comes. Your words are kept exactly as written.
2. Choose what it is: **Just a thought** (anything, unsorted; it waits in Captured thoughts), **A question I have** (something to find out or decide; it goes to Questions I have), or **An idea I am thinking about** (something you might use; it goes to Things and ideas I'm thinking about). A grey line under the choices says where each goes.
3. In the Writing Room, two more choices appear. **Attach to this scene** (ticked at first) puts it in the scene plan under **Attached to this scene**. Then choose **Resolve later**, or **I need this to keep writing** for something that is blocking you.
4. Click **Save it**, or press **Ctrl+Enter**. **Cancel** or **Esc** closes without saving.

The box closes and your cursor is back on the page.

**Example.** Mid-sentence, you wonder whether the cats in the fish-guts scene are Anthony's or the whole street's. You press Ctrl+Shift+N, type the question, choose **A question I have** and **I need this to keep writing**, and press Ctrl+Enter. It now waits in the scene plan under Attached to this scene, marked *needed to keep writing*, and at the top of **Questions still open** on Where was I.

**A note for later or Capture?** Use a note for later for something that belongs at an exact spot in the text (a missing name, a date). Use Capture for a thought about the story (a question, an idea for another scene), so it lands in the Story Brain where you can sort it.

## Writing Room keys

"Ctrl" is the key in the bottom left corner of your keyboard. Press the keys together.

| Keys | What they do |
| --- | --- |
| Ctrl+Shift+N | Capture a thought |
| Ctrl+Shift+L | Leave a note for later, or turn the selected words into one |
| Ctrl+Shift+F | Focus on or off |
| Esc | Leave Focus |
| Ctrl+Shift+W | Return to Writing, from anywhere in the book |
| Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y | Undo and redo typing |
| Enter | Save a title, a chapter name or a part name |

## When the Writing Room looks wrong but is working

| What you see | Why |
| --- | --- |
| The scene's status is still Idea after you wrote in it | The status moves to Drafting when the words become a saved version: after about twenty seconds' pause, or when you leave the scene |
| The binder's circle is empty while the scene has words | Same reason. Or you chose a not-started status yourself; the circle follows the Status dropdown |
| The page won't take typing | A box is waiting for a choice (kept typing, or two windows), or the page is still *Opening the page…* |
| The scene plan disappeared | **Read it back** was clicked twice, or **Hide scene plan** once. Click **Show scene plan** |
| Show in Narrative Zoom says the scene is not on the plan | The scene has no card yet. Fill any line of its scene plan |
| No red underlines and no echoes | The door is closed, or a sprint is running |
| A new scene isn't in Planned, not written yet | That list only holds cards from your plan with no page. A scene you added is already in the binder |
| A note for later isn't in the Left for later list yet | The list reads the saved scene. Pause a moment, or leave the scene, and it appears |
