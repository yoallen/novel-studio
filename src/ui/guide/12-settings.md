# Settings, methods, drafts and two computers

**Settings** is where you tell Story Canon how you want this novel to work: your name on it, your goals, how much the collaborator speaks up, the words it uses for decisions, and where your backups go. This chapter walks through every section in order, then covers three places you reach from Settings or nearby: **My methods** (making your own beat sheet), **Drafts** (keeping whole versions of the book), and sharing between two computers.

Nothing in Settings is required. Every choice has a sensible starting value, and a new writer can leave the whole page alone.

## Finding your way around Settings

**Where:** the **Settings** tab, under the word *and* on the right edge.

The page is headed **Settings for this novel**. Most of what is on it belongs to the novel you have open. A few sections belong to you across all your novels, and they say so.

From the top:

- **Novel setup**, in a shaded box, with the link **Open novel setup**. Novel setup is a separate page of questions about what kind of book this is: genres, story types, how the story is told, and your storytelling toolkit. Your answers there decide which blanks and tools the program shows you. See [Novel setup: telling Story Canon what kind of book this is](#novel-setup-telling-story-canon-what-kind-of-book-this-is).
- A line explaining how saving works on this page (next section).
- **On this page**, a row of links that jump to each section: **This novel**, **Guidance and goals**, **Writing push**, **Scene status steps**, **Words for decisions**, **The collaborator**, **About you**, **For all my novels**, **Appearance**, **Sharing**, **Backups**. (**Sharing** jumps to the section headed **Your other computers**.)

At the very bottom, a small line gives the program's version number.

### Save settings, and sections that take effect right away

Most of this page is kept only when you press **Save settings**. That button sits in a bar along the bottom of the window, so it is always in view, however far you scroll. Beside it, a line tells you where things stand:

| The line says | It means |
| --- | --- |
| **Everything here is saved.** | Nothing to do. |
| **You have changes not saved yet.** | You changed something. Press **Save settings** before you leave this page, or the change is lost. |
| **Saved at 3:25 PM.** | Your last press worked. |

A few sections save the moment you change them. Their headings carry a small tag, **Takes effect right away**: **The collaborator**, **Appearance** and **Backups**. **Door closed** and **Your other computers** also act at once through their own buttons.

**The common mistake** is changing your name or your goal, clicking another tab, and finding the old value next time. Watch the line beside **Save settings**. If it says you have unsaved changes, press the button.

## This novel: title, names and length

The first section has five boxes in two columns.

### Title

The novel's title. It shows on the book's cover in the Library, at the top of every page, and on exports. Change it whenever you like; nothing else changes with it.

### Your name

The name Story Canon uses for you in this novel. A grey line under the box says which name it calls you by. If you gave a name in **About you** (further down the page), it calls you that: "Story Canon calls you Dana, the name in About you below." If not, it uses the first word of this box.

This box is used for:

- The name on your messages in conversations. Messages you send are signed with this box exactly.
- The **Kept for you** marker, through your About you name or first name (see [The marker for what is yours to write or decide](#the-marker-for-what-is-yours-to-write-or-decide)).
- The author name on exports, unless you set a pen name below.

**This looks wrong but is fixable in one step:** a novel brought in from someone else's planning (such as *I Declare War*) arrives with that planner's name in this box. Your messages in its conversations will be signed with it. Type your own name here and press **Save settings**.

### Author name on this book

The full label reads **Author name on this book (your pen name, if it has one)**. Whatever you type is printed on the title page when you export. Leave it empty and your name from the box above is used; the empty box shows that name in grey as a hint.

**When to use it:** you publish romance as "Nadia Brooks" and literary fiction under your own name. Each novel can carry its own.

### Target length in words

The full label reads **Target length in words (optional)**. It is how long you expect the finished book to be, between 1,000 and 2,000,000 words.

**Why it matters:** beat positions are percentages of a book. Without a target, Story Canon can only measure against what you have drafted so far, which makes beat word counts tiny early on. With a target of 90,000, it can tell you the Midpoint falls around word 45,000. The **Where beats fall** tab in Narrative Zoom and the plotting sheet both use it.

**Example.** A cozy mystery runs around 70,000 words. Type 70000 and save. Every beat's word and page estimate now reflects a 70,000-word book.

### Words per page for page estimates

How many words make a printed page, used wherever Story Canon estimates pages. It starts as **250, 300**: two estimates, because manuscript pages differ (250 for a double-spaced manuscript page, 300 closer to a printed book). Type one number, or several separated by commas, each between 100 and 600.

### Your next writing action

Under its own heading, **Your next writing action** is one line for what you mean to write next, such as "Draft the opening scene in the driveway." It shows as **Next** on **Where was I**, so the first thing you see when you open the book is what to do. Leave it empty and the program suggests one from where you left off, usually the next planned scene.

## Guidance and goals

The line under the heading explains it: "How much the program and the collaborator speak up about concerns, while you draft and while you plan."

### Setting the guidance levels

Two dropdowns, **Guidance while drafting** and **Guidance while planning**, each with three choices:

- **Quiet: note concerns, never interrupt**
- **Advisory: small notices you can ignore**
- **Deep: full detail on everything it notices**

Drafting starts at **Quiet**, planning at **Advisory**. These levels shape how the collaborator answers: drafting applies when you ask from a scene, planning everywhere else. What each level does, with an example, is in [Guidance levels: Quiet, Advisory and Deep](#guidance-levels-quiet-advisory-and-deep).

### Everyday goal

**Everyday goal** is the goal you work to on ordinary days. Under the dropdown, a grey line explains whichever choice is showing.

| Choice | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **No goals** | No daily number. Where was I still shows what you wrote. This is the start. |
| **Words per day** | A number of words each day. Where was I tracks it every day you write. |
| **Scenes per week** | A number of scenes each week. It is kept as your goal, but for now Where was I counts words. |
| **Minutes per day** | Minutes of writing each day. It is kept as your goal, but for now Where was I counts words. |
| **Milestones** | No daily number. A box, **My milestones**, opens for you to write the milestones you are working toward, one per line, such as "Act One drafted by March." They are yours to tick off. |

For **Words per day**, **Scenes per week** and **Minutes per day**, a box called **Goal amount** appears beside the dropdown for the number.

How the goal shows on Where was I is in [Everyday goals: a number for ordinary days](#everyday-goals-a-number-for-ordinary-days).

**The common mistake** is setting a daily goal you would hit on a perfect day. Set the number you can hit on a tired Tuesday. Meeting a small goal every day builds a book; missing a big one builds guilt.

## A writing push

A **writing push** is a stretch of days with a word goal, such as November and 50,000 words, the challenge many writers know as NaNoWriMo. While a push runs, its daily share replaces your everyday goal, and **Where was I** tracks you against it, starting two weeks before the first day so you can see it coming.

**How to set one up:**

1. Under **A writing push**, click **Plan a writing push**.
2. Four boxes appear, filled in for November and 50,000 words: **Name it**, **First day**, **Last day**, **Words to write in it**. Change any of them.
3. A line works out the daily share: "That is about 1,667 words a day for 30 days." It goes on to say the share is your goal on those days only, that a short day's difference is shared by the days after it, and that on Where was I you can set a lighter day or a rest day ahead of time.
4. Press **Save settings**.

To remove the push, click **No push** and save.

A push can run up to a year. The last day cannot come before the first.

**Lighter days, rest days and catch-up math** are set from **Where was I**, under your progress. See [A writing push: November and 50,000 words](#a-writing-push-november-and-50-000-words) and [Lighter days and rest days](#lighter-days-and-rest-days).

## Door closed: drafting without judgment

**Door closed** is a phrase from Stephen King: a first draft is written with the door closed, for the writer alone, and opened to critics only in revision. In Story Canon it is a setting that stops everything that judges your pages until a day you choose.

**While the door is closed:**

- No red spelling underlines on the page.
- **Read it back** still reads aloud, but its echoes, watch words and long-sentence notes wait.
- In Narrative Zoom, the **Story review** questions and the possibly-misspelled **Names** wait.
- The collaborator stays quiet about problems in the draft unless you ask for them by name.

**What stays:** your plans, notes, search, the Story Brain and reading aloud. Anything that waits shows a short note, "The door is closed until November 30, so the story review waits until then," with a **Look anyway** link, so you can still check something on purpose.

**How:**

1. In **Settings**, find **Door closed**.
2. In **Keep it closed through**, pick the last day. It suggests the last day of your writing push if one is coming, or thirty days from now.
3. Click **Close the door**. This acts at once; you do not need **Save settings**.

The section then says "The door is closed through November 30." To move the day, pick a new one under **Change the day** and click **Save**. To open it early, click **Open the door now**. Otherwise it opens by itself the day after your date. You can also close it from **Where was I**, under your progress (see [Closing the door from the progress panel](#closing-the-door-from-the-progress-panel)). What it changes in the Writing Room is in [Door closed while you draft](#door-closed-while-you-draft).

**Why it works.** Drafting and judging use different parts of your attention. A flagged long sentence pulls you into fixing mode while you are still finding the scene. Keeping the two apart is how first drafts get finished.

## Scene status steps

A **status step** is a stage a scene moves through, from first idea to finished. You choose a scene's step from the **Status** dropdown in the Writing Room's scene plan (see [Status: the steps a scene moves through](#status-the-steps-a-scene-moves-through)). This section is where you decide what the steps are.

The starting steps are **Idea**, **Planned**, **Ready to draft**, **Drafting**, **First draft complete**, **Revising**, **Revised** and **Finalized**.

Each step is a row:

| On the row | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| The name box | Rename the step. Type over it. |
| **Counts as drafted** | Tick it for steps where the scene has prose on the page. |
| **↑** and **↓** | Move the step earlier or later. |
| **Remove** | Takes the step out. (You cannot remove the last one.) |

Under the list, **Add a step** adds one named "New step" at the end, ticked as drafted. Rename it and move it where it belongs. Press **Save settings** when done.

### Counts as drafted, and the circles in the binder

In the Writing Room's binder, each scene has a small circle before its name. The circle follows the scene's status step, not its word count:

| Circle | Meaning | For the starting steps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Empty | Not started | Idea, Planned, Ready to draft |
| Half filled | Being written | Drafting |
| Full | Written | First draft complete, Revising, Revised, Finalized |

The rule: steps before the first one ticked **Counts as drafted** show an empty circle. The first ticked step shows a half circle. Every step after it shows a full circle. The scene plan in the Writing Room shows this key under the **Status** dropdown, with your own step names.

**Heads up:** the grey line in this section of Settings describes the circles differently ("from Revising on the circle is half filled"). The table above is how the binder actually works.

**Example.** You add a step called "Read aloud" after Revised and tick **Counts as drafted**. Scenes at "Read aloud" show a full circle.

## Words for decisions

The line under the heading says: "The words shown everywhere for how settled something is. Change any of them to your own."

### The six status words

Story Canon marks every fact with how sure you are about it. Six boxes let you rename those marks. Each is labeled with the word it replaces:

| Box | The starting word means |
| --- | --- |
| **Shown instead of "Decided"** | You have made up your mind. |
| **Shown instead of "Developing"** | You are still working on it. |
| **Shown instead of "Still open"** | A question not answered yet. |
| **Shown instead of "Example"** | An illustration of what an answer could look like. Never a decision. |
| **Shown instead of "Suggestion"** | Someone else's idea, waiting for you. |
| **Shown instead of "Set aside"** | Retired, but kept. |

**When to use it:** if your own notes already say "Locked" instead of "Decided," or you think in "Ink" and "Pencil," use your words. They then appear everywhere: on records, in search, in exports, and in what the collaborator is told.

Renaming changes only the word on screen. It never changes what the status means.

### The marker for what is yours to write or decide

**Kept for you** is a mark you can put on any record or scene to say "this one is mine." The collaborator never drafts anything carrying it.

The box **The marker for what is yours to write or decide** holds the words of that mark. It starts as "your name, it's on you now," for example "Dana, it's on you now." A grey line says where the name comes from: About you, or **Your name** above, and the mark follows the name if you change it.

**If the marker shows the wrong name** (or no name), fix the name in **About you** or **Your name** and save. If neither has a name, the marker simply reads "It's on you now."

### Other markers for work you keep

**Other markers for work you keep (one per line)** lists more specific "mine" marks. They describe what kind of work waits for you, so a glance at a record tells you whether it needs writing or choreographing. The starting list, with what each means:

| Marker | What it says |
| --- | --- |
| **Mine to write** | You write this one. The collaborator never drafts it. |
| **Mine to build** | You work out how it is built: the mechanics, the setup, how the pieces fit. |
| **Mine to design** | You design it: how it looks, the space, the shape of the gag or the trap. |
| **Mine to choreograph** | You stage the movement: who goes where, the fight, the chase, the physical comedy. |
| **Mine to time** | You set the timing: the pacing, when a reveal lands, the beat before the punchline. |
| **Mine to escalate** | You decide how it builds, and how big it gets before it breaks. |
| **Mine to settle** | You decide the open question here. Suggestions wait; nothing is settled for you. |

Add your own, one per line, such as "Mine to research." Markers you add have no explanation line, since you know what you meant. Delete a line to remove a marker. Press **Save settings**.

## The collaborator section of Settings

This section turns the AI collaborator on for this novel, holds your API key, controls whether a scene's prose is shared, and picks which Claude model answers. It is tagged **Takes effect right away**.

Every choice in it is explained step by step in [Turning the collaborator on](#turning-the-collaborator-on). In short:

- **Claude API key** and **Use this key** (or **Remove key** once set).
- **Use the collaborator for** this novel: nothing is sent until this is ticked.
- **Also share the prose of a scene when I ask about that scene**.
- **Which Claude answers**: Opus, Sonnet, Haiku or Fable.

## About you in Settings

**About you** is the same set of questions Story Canon asks the first time you open it. Every answer is optional. It stays on this computer, goes to the collaborator only when you talk with it, and shapes nothing but how the collaborator speaks with you.

| Question | What it is used for |
| --- | --- |
| **What should I call you?** | The name the program and the collaborator call you. It also goes into the **Kept for you** marker. |
| **Your pronouns** | So the collaborator never assumes. Click a suggestion (she/her, he/him, they/them, she/they, he/they, ze/zir, any pronouns, just my name) or type your own. |
| **How do you find your story?** | I plot first, I find the story as I write, Somewhere in between, or your own words. The collaborator fits its help to how you work. |
| **How long have you been writing?** | Years, books finished, or "this is my first." |
| **How do you like to be helped and taught?** | For example, "teach the idea fully before you show me an example." |
| **Anything that drains you, or that the collaborator should stay away from?** | Topics, styles of feedback, anything. |
| **Anything else about you as a writer** | Whatever else you want it to know. |

Here in Settings, these answers are saved with **Save settings**. They belong to you, not to one novel, so every book uses them.

## For all my novels

Everything in this section belongs to you across every novel. It is saved with **Save settings**.

### My craft shelf

**My craft shelf** lists the craft books and teachers you learn from. When you ask the collaborator **Where to learn more**, it starts with your shelf, by title and author only.

The shelf is a small table with three columns:

| Column | What goes in it |
| --- | --- |
| **Title** | The book's title. |
| **Author** | Who wrote it. |
| **What I use it for** | Your own note, such as "structure for comedy" or "when a character feels flat." This is what tells the collaborator which book fits which problem. |

Each row ends with **Remove**. **Add a book** adds an empty row. A row with no title is dropped when you save.

**Example.** Add *The Hidden Tools of Comedy*, Steve Kaplan, "why a gag works or doesn't." Next time you ask **Where to learn more** about a joke that falls flat, that book comes first.

### My methods

A line with the link **Open my methods**, where you make methods and beat sheets of your own. See [My methods: making your own beat sheet](#my-methods-making-your-own-beat-sheet).

### Stages a book can be in

A **stage** is where a whole book stands in its life, chosen from a dropdown on the book's cover in the Library and on Where was I. The box **Stages a book can be in** lists them, one per line: Idea, Plotting and planning, First draft, Second draft, Third draft, Revising, Editing, With beta readers, With an editor, Querying, On submission, Published, Resting.

Add, rename, reorder or delete lines to make your own list. Stages are labels only. They never lock or change anything. How a stage differs from a draft is in [Stages and drafts: two different questions](#stages-and-drafts-two-different-questions).

## Appearance: light, dark or match Windows

Three choices, tagged **Takes effect right away**:

- **Match Windows** follows your Windows setting: when Windows is dark, Story Canon is too. This is the start.
- **Light**: the purple cover and cream page, always.
- **Dark**: a deep eggplant cover and a dark page, always.

The choice belongs to this computer and applies to every novel.

## Your other computers: writing on two machines

You can write on two computers, such as a laptop at home and a Surface at work, and keep them in step. Story Canon calls this **sharing** between computers.

**How it works.** Each computer keeps its own full copy of your novels, so each works with no internet at all. Both use one folder they can both see, such as a folder in your OneDrive. When a novel changes, Story Canon puts a sealed copy of it in that folder: a few minutes after you stop making changes, and when you close the program. The other computer looks in the folder every minute while it is open, and when it opens. Neither computer ever writes into a file the other made, which is what keeps OneDrive from damaging anything.

**Why it is built this way.** Programs that keep one shared file in OneDrive can end up with two computers changing the same file at once, and the file breaks. Story Canon never shares one file. It shares sealed copies and checks which is newer.

### Setting up sharing

1. On the first computer, open **Settings** and find **Your other computers**.
2. In **Call this computer**, type a name, such as *Laptop*.
3. Click **Choose the shared folder** and pick a folder in your OneDrive (or any folder both computers can see).
4. A message says "Sharing is on." Your novels are copied into a folder called **Story Canon shared** inside the folder you chose.
5. On the second computer, install Story Canon. You do not need a novel open there: the **Library** has its own **Your other computers** section, below your novels. Name this computer (*Surface*) and choose **the same folder**.
6. Still in the **Library**, a list headed **On your other computers** appears with each novel the first computer shared. Click **Bring to this computer** beside each one (see [On your other computers](#on-your-other-computers)).

**The common mistake:** on a brand-new install, the Library also offers to bring in *I Declare War* from its planning files. On your second computer, do not use that for a book you already have. Turn on sharing in the Library first: once the other computer's copy is in the shared folder, that offer goes away by itself, and **Bring to this computer** brings over the same book.

Once sharing is on, the section shows:

- "This computer is **Laptop**, sharing through" the folder.
- Whether this novel has changes not shared yet, or when it was last shared, and when work last came in from another computer.
- **Also sharing:** the other computers and when each was last seen. Or "No other computer has joined yet."
- **Ask me before bringing in work from my other computer** (next section).
- **Notes from the road** (see below).
- **Share now** sends this computer's changes at once. **Change folder** picks a different shared folder. **Stop sharing** turns sharing off on this computer, after asking; nothing is deleted here or in the shared folder.

### Day to day on two computers

When you open a novel and your other computer has shared newer work, a notice appears above the page: "Your Surface shared newer work on this novel. Bring it in before you write," with **Bring it in**. Nothing changes until you click it, and a backup is made first.

The tick box **Ask me before bringing in work from my other computer** controls this:

- **Ticked** (the start): nothing changes until you click **Bring it in**.
- **Unticked**: newer work comes in on its own, after a backup, when there is nothing to choose.

If the novel is open on the other computer with changes it has not shared yet, a notice says so: "This novel is open on your Surface with changes it has not shared yet." You can click **Write here anyway**; you will then be asked which version to keep, and nothing is lost.

**Good habit:** close Story Canon on one computer before you start on the other. It is not required, but you will almost never be asked to choose.

### When both computers changed a novel

Say you wrote at lunch on the Surface, and the laptop at home also had changes it had not shared. Story Canon will not pick for you. A notice shows both computers' names with two buttons:

- **Keep this computer's as the main version**
- **Make the Surface's the main version**

Whichever you choose becomes the main novel on both computers. The other is not thrown away. It appears in your Library beside the main one, on both computers, named for where it came from.

### Notes from the road

Once sharing is on, each novel gets its own notes file in your shared folder, inside **Story Canon notes**, named for the novel (for example, `Story Canon notes\I Declare War.docx`). The section shows exactly where it is, with **Copy where it is** to copy that location.

**How to use it from your phone:**

1. Open the file from the OneDrive app in Word.
2. Tap the microphone and dictate. Start each note on a new line.
3. When Story Canon has that novel open, each new paragraph arrives as a captured thought, waiting under **Thoughts waiting to be sorted** on Where was I, and a message tells you a note came in.

A few things to know:

- **Your file is never changed.** Leave old notes in it or delete them; either is fine. Each note comes in only once.
- **A note comes in about a minute after you stop,** once the file has been still for a minute and has synced. A note still being dictated arrives whole, not in pieces.
- **The novel must be open** in Story Canon on a computer that shares through that folder.
- **Only one computer brings each note in,** even with the novel open on both.
- If you change an old note in the file, the changed version comes in as a new thought.
- No Word on your phone? A plain text file with the same name (`I Declare War.txt`) works too.

## My methods: making your own beat sheet

A **method** is a way of building a story taught by a particular writer, teacher or course. A **beat sheet** is a method's list of turning points, in order, with roughly where each falls in the book. Story Canon comes with well-known ones, such as Save the Cat, The Hero's Journey, Story Circle, Seven-Point Structure and Three-Act Structure. **My methods** is where you add your own.

**When to use it:** you learned a structure in a class or from a book that is not built in, or you adapted Save the Cat to the way you actually work.

**Where:** **Settings**, under **For all my novels**, the link **Open my methods**. Or, in Novel setup, **Make a method of my own**. The **Settings** tab stays lit while you are there.

The **My methods** page lists your methods. Each row shows the name (click to edit), where it comes from, how many beats and fields it has (or "no beat sheet"), and "in this novel" if this novel uses it. **Novel setup** at the top goes to Novel setup.

### Making a method, step by step

1. Click **Make a method of my own**.
2. Fill in **Name**. It is the one required box; the empty box says "Needed to save," and **Save this method** stays greyed out until it has a name.
3. Fill in **Where it comes from (teacher, book, course)**, such as "Ms. Alvarez's plotting workshop."
4. Fill in **What it does for me**, in your own words.
5. Add beats, questions and fields if the method has them (the next sections).
6. Click **Save this method**. A message confirms it is saved.

**Cancel** or **All my methods** leaves without saving.

**The common mistake** is copying a book's text into a method word for word. Describe it in your own words. It is more useful to you, and it respects the author.

### Its beats: each row explained

Under **Its beats**, two grey notes explain the section. Leave beats empty for a method that is a lens or a habit rather than a beat sheet. With beats, the method can drive the Board, placement and Narrative Zoom just like Save the Cat.

To start, click one of the **Start from** links: **A blank page**, **Save the Cat**, **The Hero's Journey**, **Story Circle**, **Seven-Point Structure** or **Three-Act Structure**. Starting from one copies its beats in for you to change. A blank page gives you one empty beat. **Where it comes from** is filled in for you if it is empty.

Each beat is a numbered row:

| Part | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Beat name** | What you call the beat, such as "The Lie Exposed." |
| **at** __ **%** **to** __ **%** | Where it falls in the book, as a percentage of the length. 50 means halfway. For a beat that is a single moment, put the same number in both. |
| The act dropdown | **Act 1**, **Act 2, first half**, **Act 2, second half** or **Act 3**. Decides which row of the Board it sits on. |
| **Lines up with… (optional)** | The shared moment this beat matches. See below. |
| **↑**, **↓**, **Remove** | Reorder or delete the beat. |
| **What this beat is and the job it does** | Your teaching for the beat. It shows on the beat's page when you plan. |
| **A test it must pass** (optional) | A question to check the beat against, such as "Has the hero chosen, or only reacted?" |

**Add a beat** adds an empty row at the end.

**Lines up with** names the moment in the story that many beat sheets share, under their different names. The choices include **The opening** (Opening Image, Ordinary World), **The inciting event** (Catalyst, Call to Adventure), **The hesitation**, **Into Act Two**, **The new world**, **The midpoint**, **The lowest point**, **Into Act Three**, **The climax** and **The ending**.

**Why it matters:** if you switch a novel from Save the Cat to your method, a scene placed on Save the Cat's Midpoint can find your method's midpoint beat, but only if that beat lines up with **The midpoint**. A beat with nothing chosen simply is not carried across, which is fine for a beat no other sheet has.

### Questions it asks and fields it adds

**Questions it asks of a story**: one question per line, such as "What does the hero believe on page one that is false?" The collaborator and Novel setup ask them of any novel using this method.

**Fields it adds**: blanks the method needs you to fill. For each, choose where it goes (**Each character**, **Each character arc**, **Each scene card**, **Each beat**, **The story world**, **Each relationship**, **Each place**, **Each storyline** or **Each set piece**), type a **Field name**, and optionally **A prompt** that appears as the hint under the blank. **Add a field** adds a row; **Remove** deletes one. In a novel using the method, these blanks appear grouped under the method's name.

### Turning your method on for a novel

A method belongs to you, so every novel can use it, but each novel chooses its own.

1. Open the novel, then **Settings**, then **Open novel setup**.
2. Under **My storytelling toolkit**, tick your method (see [My storytelling toolkit](#my-storytelling-toolkit)). It carries a **yours** chip and an **Edit** link.
3. If the method has beats and the novel now has more than one beat sheet ticked, choose which drives the Board in **The beat sheet that drives the board** (see [The beat sheet that drives the board](#the-beat-sheet-that-drives-the-board)). Tick **Offer my placed scenes on the matching beats** to carry scene placements across using **Lines up with**. They arrive as "might go here," waiting for you to decide.

Novel setup saves each choice as you make it.

**Changing a method later.** Edit it in **My methods** and save. If the open novel uses it, the message says "This novel has been updated to match; anything placed on its beats stays where it is."

**Example.** Your workshop teacher uses a five-beat shape: Wound, Want, Wall, Wreck, Win. Start from **A blank page**, add five beats at 0, 25, 50, 75 and 95 percent, line Wall up with **The midpoint** and Wreck with **The lowest point**, and add a field on each character called "The wound" with the prompt "What happened before page one?" Save, then tick it in Novel setup.

## Drafts: keeping a whole version of the book

A **draft** is one complete pass through your book. When you finish one and start revising, Story Canon can keep an exact copy of the whole book as it stood, so any scene can be compared with it later.

**Where:** on **Where was I**, next to the **Stage** dropdown, a line says **Draft:** and the current draft's name, with the link **Drafts**. Click it. (The **The Writing Room** tab stays lit while you are on the Drafts page.)

**Draft and stage are different.** A line on the page explains it: a draft is a version of the pages; the stage on the book's cover is where the book as a whole stands. The two move separately. A book can be in "Plotting and planning" while its first draft is open, and every new book starts in its first draft. This is working as designed.

**Why it helps.** Revision is braver when the old version is one click away. You can cut a scene to the bone without saving a copy "just in case."

### Keeping a draft, step by step

1. Open **Drafts**. Under **Now:** and the draft's name, a line says when it began.
2. Click **This draft is done**.
3. If you like, write **A note about this draft (optional)**, such as "What this draft did, what the next one is for."
4. If you like, fill in **Name for the next draft (optional)**, such as "Second draft" or "The voice pass."
5. Click **Keep first draft and start the next** (the button names your current draft). **Not yet** cancels.

A message confirms: "First draft is kept, 82,400 words. You are now in the second draft." You keep writing in the same place. Nothing in your manuscript changes.

Under **Kept drafts**, each kept draft shows its name, when it was finished, its word count and number of scenes, your note, and **Compare with now**.

### Comparing a draft with now

Click **Compare with now** beside a kept draft. A section opens with a summary, such as "82,400 words then, 79,150 now. 31 scenes changed, 12 untouched, 2 new, 3 no longer in the book." Then three lists:

- **Changed**: each scene with its chapter and its word count then and now. Click a scene's name to see it side by side.
- **New since then**: click to open the scene.
- **In that draft, not in the book now**: scenes you cut, still kept in that draft.

On a single scene's comparison, choose which kept draft to compare with under **Compare now with**. Struck text was in the older draft; highlighted text is new since. **Back to the comparison** returns.

To send out a kept draft instead of the current book, choose it under **Version** when you export (see [What goes in](#what-goes-in)).

### I finished the draft too soon

Under the kept drafts is a link, **I finished first draft too soon** (with your draft's name). Click it, then **Reopen it**. The kept copy is released and you carry on in that draft. Your writing is not touched. **Keep it finished** cancels.
