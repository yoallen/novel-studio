# Where was I: your novel's home page

**Where was I** is the home page of every novel. This chapter takes the page from top to bottom, then page by page through its three numbered pages, in the order the sections appear on screen, and ends with a two-minute routine for starting a writing day and a table of things that look wrong but aren't. Read it after your first hour, then come back to the routine whenever a writing day is hard to start.

## Where was I: what the page is for

**What it is.** Where was I is the first view in the list on the right, with *your novel's home page* in small type under its name. Story Canon opens a novel on this page every time you come back to it. Its job is to answer one question before you have finished your coffee: *what do I do next?*

It does that by gathering in one place everything you would otherwise have to remember: the next scene to write, how your writing days are going, the last lines you wrote, the notes you left in your pages, the questions still open, the decisions you made lately, the thoughts you saved and have not sorted, the times you stepped away from the page, and what you did this week.

**Why it helps.** Most writing days are lost in the first ten minutes, while you try to remember where you were. Where was I hands you your place back, so the first ten minutes go to the page.

**Do most writers need it?** Every writer lands here, so yes. But nothing on it is homework. Every list is there to read and click when it helps, and to ignore when it doesn't.

While the page gathers everything, it says *Gathering where you left off…* for a moment.

### Where was I at a glance

The page has two parts. The top part is always in view. Under it, the rest of the page is laid out like a small notebook with three numbered pages, the same way a record's fields are laid out on numbered pages (see [Record pages: the numbered tabs](#record-pages-the-numbered-tabs)).

**Always at the top:**

| Section | What it is | When you see it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The page's top | *Where was I*, *This is (your title)'s home page.* and a short paragraph | Always |
| **Next** and **Write** | The one next step, in large type, and the orange button that takes you there | Always |
| *Unsaved typing was kept* | Typing from a window that closed unexpectedly, waiting for you | Only after the program closed unexpectedly |

**Page 1, Today:** what you need to start writing now.

| Section | What it is | When you see it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The stage row | **Stage**, **Draft** and a **Drafts** link: where the book is in its life | Always |
| **Plant the seed** | Six optional questions to start an empty novel | Only on a novel with no characters yet |
| Drafting progress | One column per day, and your word figures | Always (plain until you set a goal) |
| The *Set up this novel* note | An invitation to tell Story Canon what kind of book this is | Until your first choice in Novel setup |
| **Where you stopped** | The last lines of the scene you were in | Once you have opened a scene |
| **Left for later** | Notes you left in your pages, between double square brackets | Always (it says *Nothing waiting* when empty) |

**Page 2, Still open:** the things you are carrying in your head.

| Section | What it is | When you see it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Questions still open** | Your newest open questions | Always |
| **Latest decisions** | The last things you decided | Always |
| **Thoughts waiting to be sorted** | Thoughts and ideas you saved but have not filed | Always |
| **Detours still open** | Times you left the page and have not come back yet | Only when there are some |

These sit side by side in columns on a wide window, and one under another on a narrow one.

**Page 3, This week:** what you did in the last seven days.

The rest of this chapter takes each section in turn, in that order.

### Where was I: the three pages

**What they are.** Under **Next** is a row of page tabs: **1 Today**, **2 Still open**, **3 This week**, and at the end of the row, **All pages**. One page shows at a time.

**Why it is laid out this way.** A home page that shows everything at once is a wall of lists, and a wall of lists is hard to start a writing day from. Page 1 holds only what you need to begin. The other two wait until you want them.

**How to use them:**

1. Click a tab to turn to that page. The tab you are on is joined to the page below it.
2. Look at the numbers. A page number turns ink blue when that page has something on it. **1 Today** is always blue. **2 Still open** turns blue when any of its lists has something in it. **3 This week** turns blue once this week has new words, a scene worked on or a decision made.
3. To turn a page without going back up, use the links at the bottom of each page. They name the page before and after, such as **Page 2: Still open**.
4. Click **All pages** to see all three, one after another, each with its number and name as a heading.

Story Canon remembers which page you were on for each novel while the program is open. Close the program and open it again, and Where was I starts on **1 Today**.

**The common mistake** is thinking your questions and decisions have gone, because the page opens on Today. They are on page 2. A blue **2** tells you something is waiting there.

**Example.** You open *The Keeper of Gull Point* in the morning. Page 1 shows your progress and the last lines of the storm scene. The **2** is blue, so you glance at **2 Still open** and see that yesterday you decided Eli drowned in October. You click **Page 1: Today** at the bottom and then **Write**.

## Where was I: the top of the page

At the very top, the heading **Where was I**, then the line *This is (your title)'s home page.*, then a short paragraph:

*Story Canon opens here every time you come back to this book. It picks up where you left off: the next thing to write, how your words are going, notes you left for later, questions still open, and what you decided most recently. Whenever you lose your place, come back here.*

That last sentence is the whole idea. If you get lost anywhere in the program, click **Where was I** in the list on the right.

## Next and Write: the one step that stands out

**What it is.** Under the small word **Next** is a single sentence in large type, such as *Keep writing "The empty boat"*. Below it is the orange **Write** button. Beside the button, in small gray type, there is sometimes a detail line: the scene's status and word count (for example *Drafting, 640 words*), or where a planned scene sits in your plan (for example *Your next planned scene, at Catalyst*).

A **planned scene** here is a scene card (a plan for a scene) that has no written page yet, or whose page is still empty.

**Why it helps.** A writing day needs one clear first move, not a menu. Next is that move. It is the one thing on the page meant to stand out.

### Where was I: how Story Canon chooses Next

Unless you have written your own next step (below), Story Canon works it out from where you left off, in this order:

1. **You were in a scene.** Next says *Keep writing "(the scene's name)"*, and the detail line shows its status and words.
2. **You have planned scenes with no page yet**, and either the book has no words at all or you have no scene open. Next says *Write "(the card's name)"*, the first planned scene in story order, and the detail line names the beat it sits on, if it has one.
3. **A scene is still at a not-started status** (Idea, Planned or Ready to draft, in the usual status steps). Next says *Draft "(that scene)" in (its chapter)*, such as *Draft "The harbor" in Chapter 3*.
4. **The book has no chapters yet.** Next says *Write the first line of your first scene*.
5. **Every scene has been started.** Next says *Start the next scene after "(your last scene)"*.

There is one exception to the first rule. If the scene you were in is still empty and was never planned, and a planned scene is waiting, Next points you at the planned scene instead. An empty, unplanned page is rarely the best place to start.

### Where was I: what the Write button does

**Write** always lands you on a page you can type on, never on a form:

- If Next names a scene that has a page, **Write** opens it in **The Writing Room**, with the cursor where you left it.
- If Next names a planned scene that has no page yet, **Write** makes its page, puts it in the right place among your chapters, and opens it.
- If there is nothing to open, **Write** makes a new empty scene at the end of your last chapter (and a first chapter, if you have none) and opens it.

Planning is never required first. You can always click **Write** and start.

### Where was I: writing your own next step

Sometimes you know better than the program what comes next: "Draft the scene where Ana sees the boat", or "Fix the tide times in chapter 6 before anything else". You can say so.

1. Click **Settings** in the list on the right.
2. On page **1 This novel**, find **Your next writing action**, under the novel's title and length. (Its small print says *Leave empty and the program suggests one from where you left off.*)
3. Type your next step in your own words.
4. Click **Save settings** at the bottom of the window.

Your words now appear under **Next**. **Write** opens the scene you were last in, or makes a new scene if you were in none. To go back to the program's suggestion, empty the box and save again.

**The common mistake** is writing a whole to-do list in that box. Next works because it is one step. Write the one you will do first, and change it when it is done.

**Example.** *The Keeper of Gull Point* has three half-finished scenes. The writer types *Finish the scene where Marisol finds Eli's knife in the boat* and saves. Every morning that week, Where was I opens on that sentence, and **Write** opens the scene she was in. On Friday the scene is done, so she empties the box, and Next goes back to suggesting the next scene on its own.

## Where was I: unsaved typing was kept

**What it is.** If Story Canon closed while you were typing (a power cut, a crash, a laptop lid shut at the wrong moment), your typing is not lost. The next time you open the book, Where was I shows a notice just under **Next** and **Write**, above the page tabs, so you cannot miss it: *Unsaved typing was kept for "(the scene's name)". Open the scene to choose which version to keep.* Under it is a button, **Open "(the scene's name)"**, for each scene that has kept typing.

**What to do:**

1. Click **Open "(the scene's name)"**. The scene opens in the Writing Room with a box over it titled *Typing from (the date and time) was kept*.
2. Read the **Kept typing** shown in the box. It is the end of what you typed before the program closed.
3. Click **Use the kept typing** to make it the scene's text, or **Keep the saved version** to keep what was saved before.

Either way, nothing is thrown away: the version you did not choose stays in the scene's earlier versions. Until you choose, the page will not take typing, so nothing can overwrite the kept words by accident. The full detail is in [The Writing Room](#the-writing-room).

**The common mistake** is ignoring the notice and opening the scene some other way, then wondering why the page won't let you type. The page is waiting for your choice. Make it, and the page opens.

## Where was I: the stage row

At the top of page **1 Today** is a row with three things in it, and a gray line under them: *The stage is what you are doing with the book now. The draft is which full pass of the manuscript you are on.*

- **Stage** is a menu. It says what you are doing with the book now: *Plotting and planning*, *First draft*, *Revising*, *Querying*, *Resting* and so on. Choose a new one whenever the book's life changes. A new novel says *Choose a stage*.
- **Draft:** followed by a name in bold, such as **First draft**, says which full pass of the manuscript you are on. It is a label, not a button.
- **Drafts** is a link to the Drafts page, where a finished draft is kept whole so you can compare against it later.

The two can look like the same thing, and they are not: a book can be at the stage *Revising* while you work on its *Second draft*. Both are taught fully in [Library: stages and drafts](#library-stages-and-drafts), and the Drafts page in [The Writing Room](#the-writing-room).

## Where was I: the Plant the seed card

On a novel with no characters yet, a card headed **Plant the seed** appears here. It asks six short questions (who the story is about, who they are, what they want, what stands in the way, what they stand to lose, and how it might end). Every answer is optional and goes in as **Developing**. **Plant it** makes your lead's record and opens the Story Brain; **Not now** puts the card away for good in this novel. The card is taught in full in [Plant the seed: six questions for a new novel](#plant-the-seed-six-questions-for-a-new-novel).

## Where was I: drafting progress

Below the stage row is your drafting progress: a heading (the month's name, or your writing push's name, such as **November**), a sentence about today, a row of thin columns (one for each day), and a few figures underneath.

**What it answers.** *How are my writing days going, and am I on track?*

Until you set a goal, it shows how many words you wrote each day this month, with a line inviting you to set a daily goal or a writing push, and a **Settings** link. Once you set a daily goal, or a writing push (a stretch of days with a word target, such as November and 50,000 words), it measures each day against that goal and tells you what each remaining day needs.

How to read the columns, in short:

| What you see | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| A solid dark column | You met that day's goal (or wrote anything at all, when there is no goal) |
| A gray striped column | You wrote that day, but less than its goal |
| A flat line and no column | No words that day |
| A dotted line | A day still ahead |
| A thick pink line under a day, with a pink number | Today |

Point at any column to see that day's exact count. The sentence above the columns says how today is going, such as *Today, 0 words of your 1,000.* Under the columns are figures such as *(month) so far*, **To finish on time**, **Against the plan**, **Days in a row** and **The whole book** (some appear only once you have a goal or a push). Everything about goals, pushes, catching up, lighter days and rest days is in [Goals, sprints and November](#goals-sprints-and-november).

**Looks wrong, but isn't.** Words you type today can take a little while to appear in the sentence about today, because a day's words are counted from saved versions of your scenes, and a version is saved after a short pause in typing or when you leave the scene. Come back from the Writing Room, and the count catches up. How versions are saved is taught in [The Writing Room](#the-writing-room).

## Where was I: the Set up this novel note

Until you make your first choice in Novel setup, a one-line note appears here: *Whenever you like: tell the program what kind of story this is and which methods you use, and it will offer the right tools.* The **Set up this novel** link at its end opens Novel setup.

The note goes away as soon as you change anything in Novel setup: one tick is enough. A novel that started from your starting point (with methods already ticked) never shows it. Novel setup is taught in [Novel setup: telling Story Canon what kind of book this is](#novel-setup-telling-story-canon-what-kind-of-book-this-is).

## Where was I: where you stopped

**What it is.** On page **1 Today**, under the heading **Where you stopped**, is a small gray line naming the chapter and scene you were last in (for example *Chapter 2, The empty boat*), and below it the last few lines of that scene, set off by a line down the left side. If the scene is long, the lines begin with "…" because only the end is shown. If the scene has no words yet, it says *This scene is still blank.*

**Why it helps.** Rereading your last paragraph is the oldest trick for getting back into a scene. Ernest Hemingway made a habit of stopping each day while he still knew what would happen next, so the next morning he could pick up the thread at once. Where you stopped shows you the thread before you even open the page.

**How to use it.** Read the lines. If they bring the scene back, click **Write** at the top of the page (or **The Writing Room** in the list on the right), and you land at your cursor, in that scene.

**Example.** Where you stopped shows *Chapter 2, The empty boat*, and then: *…The oars were shipped, neat as a sailor's bed. Whoever had rowed this boat had not been in a hurry to leave it.* Marisol's unease comes back at once, and so does the next line.

## Where was I: left for later

**What it is.** A **note for later** is a reminder you type inside your manuscript between double square brackets, like `[[how many steps does the tower have?]]`, so you can keep writing instead of stopping to look something up. **Left for later**, the last section on page **1 Today**, gathers every such note in the book, in the order of the book. The heading shows how many there are, for example **Left for later (3)**.

Each note shows:

- **The note itself**, as a link in slanted type.
- Under it, in small gray type, **where it is**: the chapter number, the scene's name, and the few words just before the note, in quotation marks, so you remember what you were writing when you left it.

The list shows the first eight notes. If there are more, a link **Show all** (with the number) shows the rest, and **Show fewer** folds them again.

**How to use it:**

1. Click a note. Its scene opens in the Writing Room with the note selected on the page.
2. Type the answer. Typing replaces the selected note, brackets and all.
3. The note leaves the list as soon as its brackets leave the page.

When the list is empty, it says *Nothing waiting.* and reminds you how to leave a note: type it inside double brackets, or press **Ctrl+Shift+L** while you write. Notes are never counted as story words. The full teaching is in [The Writing Room](#the-writing-room).

**Example.** The storm scene is moving fast, and the writer can't remember how many steps the lighthouse has. She types `[[how many steps?]]` and keeps going. A week later, on a slow morning, Left for later shows that note with *Chapter 4, The storm* and the words just before it. She clicks it, types *one hundred and twelve*, and the note is gone from the list.

## Where was I: questions still open

**What it is.** The first list on page **2 Still open**: the eight newest questions in your book that are still open. A question is a record in the Story Brain's **Questions I have**: something you need to find out or decide, such as *Did Eli take the boat out alone that night?* Each one has a short line at its left edge, the mark for **Still open**. When there are none, the list says *None open.*

**Where they come from.** Every question you save with **Capture** (choosing **A question I have**), every question you add in the Story Brain, and questions that arrived when you brought in your planning.

**How to use it:**

1. Point at a question to see more of it, if it has more than its title.
2. Click it. The Story Brain opens on that question's own record.
3. Answer it there, or mark it answered, and it leaves this list. Questions are taught in [The Story Brain](#the-story-brain).

**Why it helps.** An open question is a decision you are carrying around in your head. Seeing the newest few each morning lets you notice which ones are now blocking the scene you are about to write, and settle those first.

**The common mistake** is treating the list as a chore to clear before writing. Most open questions can stay open for weeks. Answer the one that stands between you and today's scene, and leave the rest.

## Where was I: latest decisions

**What it is.** The second list on page **2 Still open**: the five things most recently decided, newest first. They are written in **Decided** ink: blue, with a small blue square at the left edge. Decisions that arrived with planning you brought in count too, so right after bringing in a book this list shows the last decisions from your planning. When there are none, it says *No decisions recorded yet.*

Each entry has three lines:

1. **The record's name**, as a link, for example **Marisol Vega** or **The empty boat**.
2. **The field**, in small type, for example *Goal* or *Where it pays off*.
3. **What was decided**.

So an entry that reads **Cyrus Hale**, then *Role*, then *Antagonist* means: on the record *Cyrus Hale*, in the field *Role*, you decided *Antagonist*.

**How to use it.** Click the record's name to open its record in the Story Brain, where you can see the decision in place, change it, or see where it came from.

**Why it helps.** Decisions are easy to forget between sessions, and a forgotten decision gets made twice, differently. A glance at this list before you write keeps today's pages in line with what you settled yesterday.

**Example.** Yesterday the writer decided, on the record *Eli Vega*, that he drowned in October. This morning Latest decisions shows it at the top, so the flashback she writes today has autumn light, not summer.

## Where was I: thoughts waiting to be sorted

**What it is.** The third list on page **2 Still open**: the eight newest thoughts and ideas you saved that have not found a home yet. A **thought** is anything you jot down with **Capture** as **Just a thought**: unsorted, waiting for you to decide what it is. An **idea** is something you might use, saved as **An idea I am thinking about**, that you have not adopted or set aside yet. Both wait here until you sort them.

When the list is empty, it says *Nothing waiting. Every thought you saved has a home.* Under the list, **All questions and ideas** opens the Story Brain, where every question, idea and thought lives.

**How to use it.** Click a thought to open it in the Story Brain. There you can turn it into a question or an idea, file it, or connect it to a character or scene. Sorting is taught in [The Story Brain](#the-story-brain).

**Why it helps.** Capture works because it asks nothing of you in the moment. The price is a pile of unsorted notes. This list keeps the pile small and visible, so a good idea from Tuesday doesn't vanish under Wednesday's.

**The common mistake** is sorting thoughts instead of writing. Sort two or three on a slow day, not every day before you start.

## Where was I: detours still open

**What it is.** A **detour** is a time you left the page in the middle of writing: you were in a scene in the Writing Room and went to another view (the Story Brain to check a name, Narrative Zoom to look at a beat, or Where was I). Story Canon notes each one, with where you were and where you went, so stepping away never costs you your place. **Detours still open** lists up to five detours you have not come back from yet, each named for where you went, such as *Went to Story Brain*. A gray line under the heading says: *A detour is a time you left the page. It closes when you return to writing, or when you mark it resolved in the Writing Trail.*

Under the list is a link, **Writing Trail**, to the full record of every detour. The section sits last on page **2 Still open**, and only appears when there is at least one open detour.

**How a detour closes.** Return to writing with the pink **Return to Writing** ribbon at the top left of the window, or with **Ctrl+Shift+W**. Both put you back in the scene you left, with your cursor where it was, and close every open detour at once. You can also click **Mark resolved** on a detour in the Writing Trail. The Writing Trail is taught in [The Writing Room](#the-writing-room).

**Looks wrong, but isn't.** Clicking **The Writing Room** in the list, or a scene, does not close a detour. Only Return to Writing does. If you went back to the page another way, the detour stays listed until your next Return to Writing. Nothing is wrong with your work; the list is only a record.

**Why it helps.** "Where was I?" is hardest to answer after a detour that turned into an hour. The list shows you that you left, and the ribbon takes you straight back.

## Where was I: this week

**What it is.** Page **3 This week**: a short list of what you did in the **last seven days** (counted back from now, not from Monday):

| Line | What it counts |
| --- | --- |
| **new manuscript words** | Words you added to your scenes while writing, minus words you cut |
| **scenes worked on** | Scenes you typed in |
| **decisions made** | Things you marked **Decided** yourself |
| **thoughts, questions or ideas saved, sorted or not** | Everything you captured or added as a thought, question or idea, whether you have sorted it yet or not |
| **connections made between records** | Links between two records, such as a character connected to a scene they appear in. Point at the line for a reminder |
| **questions answered** | Questions you marked answered (this line only appears when there is at least one) |

**Why it helps.** Writing a book is more than typing. A week with few new words may still hold ten decisions and twenty connections, and those are the work that makes next week's words come easily. This list counts the thinking as well as the typing, in plain numbers, with no judgment.

**Looks wrong, but isn't.** Right after you bring in a planned novel, This week may say something like *51 thoughts, questions or ideas saved, sorted or not* while **Thoughts waiting to be sorted** says *Nothing waiting*. Both are true. The first counts everything created this week, including what arrived with your planning, sorted or not. The second shows only thoughts that still need sorting.

## Using Where was I to start each writing day

Here is a routine that takes about two minutes. Do it in this order.

1. **Open the book.** Where was I is the first page you see, on **1 Today** (or the page you last looked at, if the program has stayed open).
2. **Read Next.** If it is the right step, keep it in mind. If it isn't, you can still choose any scene in the Writing Room.
3. **Read Where you stopped.** Let the last lines bring the scene back.
4. **Glance at your progress.** Read the sentence about today and, during a writing push, **To finish on time**. That is today's number. Don't study the columns.
5. **Turn to 2 Still open and scan Latest decisions and Questions still open.** Look only for anything that touches today's scene. If one question blocks you, answer it now; leave the rest.
6. **Check Detours still open**, on the same page. If yesterday ended in a detour, the **Return to Writing** ribbon takes you back to exactly where you were.
7. **Click Write**, which stays at the top whatever page you are on.

On a slow day, or at the end of a week, add two more steps: clear a few notes from **Left for later**, and sort two or three **Thoughts waiting to be sorted**.

**Why this order.** Next and Where you stopped get you into the scene. Progress gives you a number to aim for. Decisions and questions keep the scene from contradicting itself. Everything else can wait for a day when the words won't come.

**The common mistake** is reading the whole page every morning, opening every question and sorting every thought, and running out of time to write. Where was I is a doorway, not a room. Walk through it.

**Example.** The writer opens *The Keeper of Gull Point* on a Tuesday. Next says *Keep writing "Ana at the door"*. Where you stopped shows Marisol with her hand on the bolt, the doctor's knock still in the air. The progress line says *Today, 0 words of your 1,000.* Latest decisions reminds her that yesterday she decided Ana has never seen the sea from the lighthouse, and that matters in this scene. She ignores the six open questions, none of which touch the door, and clicks **Write**. Forty minutes later she has 1,100 words, and Marisol has let Ana in as far as the kitchen.

## When Where was I looks wrong but is working

| What you see | Why |
| --- | --- |
| The sentence about today still says 0 words just after you typed | A day's words are counted from saved versions. A version is saved after a short pause in typing, or when you leave the scene. Wait a moment, or come back from the Writing Room, and the count catches up |
| Next ignores your planned scenes | You have a scene open with words in it, so Next keeps you there. Planned scenes come next once nothing is under way, and they are always listed in the Writing Room under **Planned, not written yet** |
| A question you never typed is in Questions still open | It arrived with planning you brought in, or from a conversation. Click it to see its record and where it came from |
| Detours still open keeps listing a detour you came back from | You came back with the list on the right or a scene link, not with **Return to Writing**. Click the ribbon, or press **Ctrl+Shift+W**, once |
| The page can't be typed on after you open a scene from the *Unsaved typing was kept* notice | The scene is waiting for you to choose **Use the kept typing** or **Keep the saved version** |
| The *Set up this novel* note won't go away | You have not changed anything in Novel setup yet. One tick there is enough |
| The columns don't measure anything | You have not set a goal. See [Goals, sprints and November](#goals-sprints-and-november) |
| Your questions and decisions seem to be gone | They are on page **2 Still open**. Click its tab, or **All pages** to see everything at once |
| Where was I opens on page 2 or 3 instead of Today | Story Canon remembers the page you were on while the program is open. Click **1 Today**. After you close and reopen the program, it starts on Today again |
