# Settings and your other computers

**Settings** is the view where you tell Story Canon how you want one novel to work: the name it calls you, how long you expect the book to be, your goals, how much the collaborator speaks up, the words it uses for each status, and where your backups go. This chapter walks through Settings page by page, every section in the order you meet it on screen. Then it covers three things you reach from Settings or near it: sharing your novels between two computers (including setting up a second computer from the Library), **My methods** (making a beat sheet of your own), and **Drafts** (keeping a whole version of the book).

Read this chapter when you want to change how Story Canon behaves, when you buy a second computer, or when something on another screen sends you here. You do not need to read it before you start writing. Nothing in Settings is required. Every choice already has a sensible starting value, and a new writer can leave the whole page alone for weeks.

## Settings: finding your way around the page

**Where:** click **Settings** in the list of views on the right. It sits in the lower group of the list, below the small word *and*, between **Import and export** and **Help**.

The page is headed **Settings for this novel**. That heading matters: most of what you change here belongs only to the novel you have open. If you write two novels, each keeps its own title, goal and status steps. A few pages belong to you across every novel (**About you** and **All my novels**), and some settings belong to this computer (**Appearance** and **Your other computers**).

### Settings: the parts of the page, from the top

1. **Novel setup**, in a shaded box, with the link **Open novel setup**. Novel setup is a separate page of questions about what kind of book this is: its genres, its story types, how the story is told, and your storytelling toolkit (the methods you build with). Your answers there decide which fields and tools Story Canon shows you. It is taught in [Your first hour: the Library and a new novel](#your-first-hour-the-library-and-a-new-novel).
2. A line explaining how saving works on this page (see [Settings: Save settings, and sections that take effect right away](#settings-save-settings-and-sections-that-take-effect-right-away)).
3. **The page tabs**: eight numbered pages, then **All pages** (next section).
4. The page you are on.
5. A small line with the program's version number. You may be asked for it if you ever report a problem.
6. At the very bottom of the window, the bar with **Save settings**.

### Settings: the eight pages

Settings holds a lot, so it is laid out like a notebook with numbered pages, the same way a record is (see [Record pages: the numbered tabs](#record-pages-the-numbered-tabs)). One page shows at a time, with its number and name at the top.

| Page | What is on it |
| --- | --- |
| **1 This novel** | **Title**, **Your name**, **Author name on this book**, **Target length in words**, **Words per page for page estimates**, and **Your next writing action** |
| **2 Goals and focused efforts** | **Guidance and goals** (the guidance levels and your everyday goal), **A writing push**, and **Door closed** |
| **3 Writing** | **Scene status steps** and **Words for decisions** |
| **4 The collaborator** | Your API key, sharing, and which Claude answers |
| **5 About you** | The questions about you as a writer |
| **6 All my novels** | **For all my novels**: My craft shelf, My methods, and the stages a book can be in |
| **7 Appearance** | Light, dark, or matching Windows |
| **8 Other computers and backups** | **Your other computers** (sharing) and **Backups** |

**How to move around:**

1. Click a tab to turn to that page.
2. Or use the links at the bottom of each page, which name the page before and after, such as **Page 3: Writing**.
3. Click **All pages**, at the end of the row, to see every page one after another, as one long page.

Story Canon remembers which page of Settings you were on, for each novel, while the program is open. On Settings, the page numbers do not turn blue the way they do on a record: every page here always has settings on it.

**Save settings works across pages.** You can change something on page 1, turn to page 3, change something there, and press **Save settings** once. Both changes are kept. Turning a page never throws away what you typed.

When this guide or the program points you to a part of Settings, it names the page. For example, the line under **Your name** says *About you (page 5)*. Two links elsewhere open Settings straight at the right page: **Turn it on in Settings**, in a conversation while the collaborator is off, opens page **4 The collaborator**, and the **Settings** link in the drafting progress on Where was I? opens page **2 Goals and focused efforts**.

**Example.** You want to rename **Developing** to "Pencil" in *The Keeper of Gull Point*. You click **Settings**, then the tab **3 Writing**, and find **Words for decisions** there.

### Settings: Save settings, and sections that take effect right away

Almost everywhere else in Story Canon, your work saves itself. Settings is the one exception, and it is the most common surprise for new users, so it is worth learning first.

Most of this page is kept only when you press **Save settings**. That button sits in a bar along the bottom of the window, so it is always in view however far you scroll. Beside it, a short line tells you where things stand:

| The line says | It means |
| --- | --- |
| **Everything here is saved.** | Nothing to do. |
| **You have changes not saved yet.** | You changed something. Press **Save settings** before you leave this page, or the change is lost. |
| **Saved at 3:25 PM.** | Your last press worked, at that time. |
| **Not saved.** followed by a reason, in red | Something on the page can't be kept as it is, such as a push whose last day comes before its first. The reason says what and where. Nothing on the page was saved: fix that one thing and press **Save settings** again. |

A few sections save the moment you change them, and their headings carry a small tag reading **Takes effect right away**: **The collaborator**, **Appearance** and **Backups**. Two more act at once through their own buttons, with no need for **Save settings**: **Door closed** and **Your other computers**.

**Why it is built this way.** Settings changes how the whole novel behaves. Waiting for one deliberate press means a stray keystroke in a box you scrolled past cannot quietly rename your statuses or change your goal.

**The common mistake** is changing your goal, clicking another view, and finding the old goal next time. Before you leave Settings, glance at the line beside **Save settings**. If it says you have changes not saved yet, press the button.

**Example.** You are writing *The Keeper of Gull Point*. You change the **Target length** to 80000 on page **1 This novel**, then click the tab **3 Writing** and rename **Developing** to "Pencil." The line says **You have changes not saved yet.** You press **Save settings** once, and both changes are kept: **Saved at 9:12 PM.**

## Settings: This novel

Page **1 This novel** starts with five boxes in two columns, then **Your next writing action**. Everything on it belongs to this novel only.

### Settings: Title

The novel's title. It shows on the book's cover in the Library, at the top of every page, and on everything you export. Change it whenever you like; nothing else changes with it. A working title is fine. Many writers find the real title halfway through the draft.

### Settings: Your name

The name Story Canon uses for you in this novel. A gray line under the box tells you which name it actually calls you by:

- If you gave a name in **About you** (page 5), it calls you that, and the line says so, for example: "Story Canon calls you Dana, the name in About you (page 5)."
- If not, it uses the first word of this box, and the line says: "Story Canon calls you by your first name here, unless you give it another in About you (page 5)."

This box is used for three things:

- **The name on your messages** in conversations with the collaborator. Messages you send are signed with this box exactly as you typed it.
- **The Kept for you marker**, through your About you name or your first name (see [Settings: the Kept for you marker](#settings-the-kept-for-you-marker)).
- **The author name on exports**, unless you set a pen name in the next box.

**This looks wrong but is fixable in one step.** A novel that came to you from someone else, such as an archive a friend sent, arrives with that person's name in this box. Your messages in its conversations will be signed with their name until you change it. Type your own name here and press **Save settings**.

### Settings: Author name on this book

The full label reads **Author name on this book (your pen name, if it has one)**. Whatever you type here is printed on the title page when you export. Leave it empty and your name from the box above is used; the empty box shows that name in gray as a hint. A gray line under it says: "Printed on the title page when you export. Leave it empty to use your name."

**When to use it:** you publish cozy mysteries under one name and literary fiction under another. Each novel can carry its own. Most writers leave this empty.

### Settings: Target length in words

The full label reads **Target length in words (optional)**. It is how long you expect the finished book to be, up to 2,000,000 words.

**Why it matters.** A **beat** is a turning point in the story, and a beat sheet says where each one falls as a share of the book: the middle turning point at about halfway, for example. To turn "about halfway" into a word count, Story Canon needs to know how long the book will be. Without a target, it can only measure against what you have drafted so far, which makes every beat's estimate tiny when you have written three chapters. With a target of 80,000 words, it can tell you the midpoint falls around word 40,000. The **Where beats fall** tab in Narrative Zoom (the view for the book's structure) and the plotting sheet you can export both use this number.

**Do most writers need it?** Yes, if you plan with beats at all. It takes ten seconds.

**Example.** *The Keeper of Gull Point* is a mystery, and mysteries often run 70,000 to 90,000 words. Type 80000 and press **Save settings**. Every beat's word and page estimate now reflects an 80,000-word book, so you can see that Marisol should find the empty boat (the Catalyst) somewhere around word 8,000.

**The common mistake** is typing a number with a comma or the word "words" in it. Type the digits only.

### Settings: Words per page for page estimates

How many words make one printed page, used wherever Story Canon estimates pages. It starts as **250, 300**: two estimates, because pages differ. About 250 words fit on a double-spaced manuscript page, and about 300 on a page of a printed book. Type one number, or several separated by commas, each between 100 and 600. Most writers never change it.

## Settings: Your next writing action

On page **1 This novel**, under its own heading below the five boxes, **Your next writing action** is one line for what you mean to write next, such as "Draft the opening scene, where Marisol finds the empty boat." The empty box shows a gray example of its own.

What you type here shows as **Next** at the top of **Where was I?**, your novel's home page (see [Where was I? Your novel's home page](#where-was-i-your-novels-home-page)), and under the book's cover in the Library. So the first thing you see when you open the book tomorrow is what to do.

Leave it empty and Story Canon suggests one from where you left off, usually the next scene you planned. The gray line under the heading says so: "Leave empty and the program suggests one from where you left off."

**Why it helps.** The hardest minute of a writing day is the first one, when you have to decide what to do. Writing tonight's decision down means tomorrow's you only has to start.

**Example.** At the end of a session you type "Marisol rows out to the empty boat and finds the name scratched off the stern," and press **Save settings**. Tomorrow, Where was I? opens with that line under **Next**.

## Settings: Guidance and goals

Page **2 Goals and focused efforts** starts here. The line under the heading reads: "How much the collaborator speaks up about concerns, while you draft and while you plan." This section holds two kinds of setting: the guidance levels, and your everyday goal.

### Settings: the guidance levels

**Guidance** is how much the collaborator volunteers about problems it notices, beyond what you asked. Two dropdowns set it: **Guidance while drafting** and **Guidance while planning**. Each offers three choices:

- **Quiet: note concerns, never interrupt**
- **Advisory: small notices you can ignore**
- **Deep: full detail on everything it notices**

Drafting starts at **Quiet** and planning at **Advisory**. "Drafting" applies when you ask the collaborator from inside a scene; "planning" applies everywhere else.

**Why two settings.** While you draft, a long list of concerns pulls you out of the story. While you plan, those same concerns are exactly what you want to hear. Most writers keep the starting values. What each level does in a conversation, with examples, is in [Conversations and the collaborator](#conversations-and-the-collaborator).

### Settings: the everyday goal

**Everyday goal** is the goal you work to on ordinary days. Under the dropdown, a gray line explains whichever choice is showing.

| Choice | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **No goals** | No daily number. Where was I? still shows what you wrote. This is where every novel starts. |
| **Words per day** | A number of words each day. Where was I? tracks it every day you write. |
| **Scenes per week** | A number of scenes each week. It is kept as your goal, but for now Where was I? counts words. |
| **Minutes per day** | Minutes of writing each day. It is kept as your goal, but for now Where was I? counts words. |
| **Milestones** | No daily number. A box called **My milestones** opens, for the milestones you are working toward, one per line, such as "Act One drafted by March." They are yours to tick off. |

For **Words per day**, **Scenes per week** and **Minutes per day**, a box called **Goal amount** appears beside the dropdown for the number.

**How to set one:**

1. Choose a goal type in **Everyday goal**.
2. Type a number in **Goal amount** (or your milestones in **My milestones**).
3. Press **Save settings**.

How the goal shows on Where was I?, day by day, is in [Goals, sprints and November](#goals-sprints-and-november).

**The common mistake** is setting the goal you would hit on a perfect day. Set the number you can hit on a tired Tuesday. Meeting a small goal every day builds a book; missing a big one builds guilt.

**Example.** You work full time and write after dinner. You choose **Words per day**, type 300, and save. Three hundred words is about one page. At that pace, the first draft of *The Keeper of Gull Point* is done in under a year.

## Settings: planning a writing push

A **writing push** is a stretch of days with a word goal of its own, such as November and 50,000 words, the challenge many writers know as NaNoWriMo (National Novel Writing Month). While a push runs, its daily share replaces your everyday goal. **Where was I?** shows how you are tracking against it, starting two weeks before the first day so you can see it coming.

**Do most writers need it?** Only if you are doing a challenge or a deadline sprint. Leave it alone otherwise.

**How to set one up:**

1. On page **2 Goals and focused efforts**, under the heading **A writing push**, choose where to start: **30 days from today** (30,000 words), **November, 50,000 words**, or **My own dates**.
2. Four boxes appear, filled in from your choice: **Name it**, **First day**, **Last day** and **Words to write in it**. Change any of them. Under them, tick **This push has a real deadline** for a contest, a due date or November; a pause then keeps its last day unless you say otherwise. The November choice ticks it for you.
3. A line works out the daily share, for example: "That is about 1,667 words a day for 30 days." It goes on to say that this is your daily goal on those days only (your everyday goal applies the rest of the time), that if a day comes up short the difference is shared by the days after it, and that on Where was I? you can set a lighter day or a rest day ahead of time.
4. Leave **Coach me through the push** ticked if you want the push coach on Where was I? A note for each day, the milestones as you reach them, and plain help when you are behind or stuck. Untick it to work without the coach. Under it, **Your own notes for the coach** takes notes of your own, one per line; click **Save my notes**. See [Goals: the push coach](#goals-the-push-coach).
5. Press **Save settings**.

While a push is running, this section shows it under *The push you're in*. You can change it (it stays the same push), **Pause this push**, **End this push early**, or **Plan your next push** to begin after it ends, but you can't start another push on top of it. A push that hasn't started yet can be taken away with **Don't plan this push**. See [Goals: changing, pausing or ending a push](#goals-changing-pausing-or-ending-a-push).

A push can run up to a year. The last day cannot come before the first; if it does, Settings tells you so when you save, beside the **Save settings** button, and nothing is saved until you fix it.

Lighter days, rest days and the catch-up math are set from **Where was I?**, not here. See [Goals, sprints and November](#goals-sprints-and-november).

**Example.** You want to finish Act One of *The Keeper of Gull Point* over spring break. Name it "Spring break," set the first and last days, and type 15000. The line says "That is about 1,667 words a day for 9 days." Save.

## Settings: Door closed

Page **2 Goals and focused efforts** ends with **Door closed**, which switches off everything that judges your pages until a day you choose. It acts through its own buttons, with no need for **Save settings**. What it does and how to use it are taught in [Goals: door closed](#goals-door-closed).

## Settings: scene status steps

A **status step** is a stage a scene moves through, from first idea to finished. You choose a scene's step from the **Status** dropdown on page **2 About it** of the Writing Room's scene plan (the panel beside a scene that holds its notes and settings; see [The Writing Room](#the-writing-room)). This section, on page **3 Writing**, is where you decide what the steps are.

**Do not confuse these with the six statuses.** The statuses (Decided, Developing, Still open, Example, Suggestion, Set aside) say how settled a *fact* is. Status steps say how far along a *scene's writing* is. They are separate lists that happen to share the word "status."

The starting steps are **Idea**, **Planned**, **Ready to draft**, **Drafting**, **First draft complete**, **Revising**, **Revised** and **Finalized**.

Each step is a row:

| On the row | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| The name box | Rename the step. Type over it. |
| **Counts as drafted** | Tick it for steps where the scene has prose on the page. |
| **↑** and **↓** | Move the step earlier or later. |
| **Remove** | Takes the step out. (You cannot remove the last one.) |

Under the list, **Add a step** adds one named "New step" at the end, already ticked **Counts as drafted**. Rename it and move it where it belongs. Press **Save settings** when you are done.

**Do most writers need it?** No. The starting steps fit most ways of working. Change them if your own process has a step they lack.

### Settings: Counts as drafted, and the circles in the binder

The **binder** is the list of chapters and scenes down the side of the Writing Room. Each scene in it has a small circle before its name. The circle follows the scene's status step, not its word count:

| Circle | Meaning | For the starting steps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Empty | Not started | Idea, Planned, Ready to draft |
| Half filled | Being written | Drafting |
| Full | Written | First draft complete, Revising, Revised, Finalized |

The rule: steps before the first one ticked **Counts as drafted** show an empty circle. The first ticked step shows a half circle. Every step after it shows a full circle. The gray line in this section of Settings says the same thing, and the scene plan in the Writing Room shows the key under the **Status** dropdown (page **2 About it**), with your own step names.

**The common mistake** is ticking **Counts as drafted** on a planning step such as **Ready to draft**. The binder would then show scenes with no prose as half written.

**Example.** You like to read every finished scene aloud before calling it done. Click **Add a step**, rename "New step" to "Read aloud," and use **↑** to move it just after **Revised**. It is already ticked **Counts as drafted**. Save. Scenes at "Read aloud" show a full circle.

## Settings: words for decisions

**Words for decisions** is also on page **3 Writing**, under the status steps. The line under the heading says: "The words shown everywhere for how settled something is. Change any of them to your own."

### Settings: renaming the six statuses

Every fact in Story Canon carries a status. Six boxes let you rename the words for them. Each box is labeled with the word it replaces:

| Box | The starting word means |
| --- | --- |
| **Shown instead of "Decided"** | You have made up your mind. |
| **Shown instead of "Developing"** | You are still working on it. |
| **Shown instead of "Still open"** | A question not answered yet. |
| **Shown instead of "Example"** | An illustration of what an answer could look like. Never a decision. |
| **Shown instead of "Suggestion"** | Someone else's idea, waiting for you. |
| **Shown instead of "Set aside"** | Retired, but kept. |

**When to use it:** your own notes already say "Locked" instead of "Decided," or you think in "Ink" and "Pencil." Use your words. After you press **Save settings**, they appear everywhere: on records, in the legend under a record's name, in search, in exports, and in what the collaborator is told.

Renaming changes only the word on screen. It never changes what the status means or how it looks. A fact you call "Locked" is still written in blue ink with a small blue square, because it is still Decided underneath. (Each status's look is taught in [The words Story Canon uses, and getting help](#the-words-story-canon-uses-and-getting-help).)

**The common mistake** is renaming two statuses to the same word. Story Canon lets you, but you will no longer be able to tell them apart by name, only by their ink.

### Settings: the Kept for you marker

**Kept for you** is a mark you can put on any record or scene to say "this one is mine." You set it from the **Kept for you** dropdown near the top of a record, or the **Kept for you to write or decide** box in a scene's plan. The collaborator never drafts anything carrying it, and the Story Brain lists everything marked this way under **Kept for you**.

The box **The marker for what is yours to write or decide** holds the words of the main mark. It starts as your name followed by "it's on you now," for example "Dana, it's on you now." The name is filled in for you and follows your name if it changes. A gray line says where the name came from: "Your name, Dana, comes from About you and follows it if it changes" (or "from Your name above").

**If the marker shows the wrong name, or none:** fix the name in **About you** (page 5) or in **Your name** (page 1), and press **Save settings**. If neither has a name, the marker simply reads "It's on you now," and the gray line says "Add your name in About you and it appears here."

### Settings: other markers for work you keep

**Other markers for work you keep (one per line)** lists more specific "mine" marks, which say what kind of work waits for you. The gray line under the label explains: "Different kinds of work you keep for yourself, such as Mine to build or Mine to choreograph." The starting list is **Mine to write**, **Mine to build**, **Mine to design**, **Mine to choreograph**, **Mine to time**, **Mine to escalate** and **Mine to settle**; what each one means is in [Kept for you: work only you will do](#kept-for-you-work-only-you-will-do).

Add your own, one per line, such as "Mine to research." Delete a line to remove a marker. Press **Save settings**. Every marker on this list, plus the main one, appears in the **Kept for you** dropdown on records and scenes.

## Settings: the collaborator

This section, page **4 The collaborator**, turns the collaborator on for this novel, holds your API key, says whether a scene's prose may be shared, and picks which Claude model answers. It is tagged **Takes effect right away**.

An **API key** is a long code from Anthropic, the company that makes Claude, that lets Story Canon ask Claude questions on your behalf. Each question is billed to your own key.

In order, the section holds:

- A box, **Claude API key**, and **Use this key**. Once a key is set, the box is replaced by "Your key is set." and a **Remove key** button. A faint line says where the key is kept: "The key is kept in Windows secure storage."
- **Use the collaborator for** (your novel's title)**. Story records you ask about are sent to Claude when you ask.** Nothing is sent until this is ticked.
- **Also share the prose of a scene when I ask about that scene.** This can only be ticked once the collaborator is on.
- **Which Claude answers**: **Opus** (the most careful, slower, costs the most per question), **Sonnet** (a good everyday choice at a middle cost), **Haiku** (the fastest and least costly) or **Fable** (another model your key may reach; check its price with Anthropic first).

If Windows cannot store the key securely, a message says the key works for now but you will need to enter it again next time.

**Do most writers need it?** No. Every other part of Story Canon works without the collaborator. Each choice here is taught step by step in [Conversations and the collaborator](#conversations-and-the-collaborator).

## Settings: About you

**About you**, page **5**, is the same set of questions Story Canon asks the first time you open it, in the Library. Every answer is optional. The gray line explains: it stays on this computer, goes to the collaborator only when you talk with it, and shapes nothing but how the collaborator speaks with you.

| Question | What it is used for |
| --- | --- |
| **What should I call you?** | The name Story Canon and the collaborator call you. It also goes into the Kept for you marker. |
| **Your pronouns** | So the collaborator never assumes. Click a suggestion (she/her, he/him, they/them, she/they, he/they, ze/zir, any pronouns, just my name) or type your own. |
| **How do you find your story?** | **I plot first**, **I find the story as I write**, **Somewhere in between**, or your own words. The collaborator fits its help to how you work. |
| **How long have you been writing?** | Years, books finished, or "this is my first." |
| **How do you like to be helped and taught?** | For example, "teach the idea fully before you show me an example." |
| **Anything that drains you, or that the collaborator should stay away from?** | Topics, kinds of feedback, anything. |
| **Anything else about you as a writer** | Whatever else you want it to know. |

Here in Settings, these answers are kept with **Save settings**. They belong to you, not to one novel, so every book uses them.

## Settings: for all my novels

Page **6 All my novels** holds the section headed **For all my novels**. Everything in it belongs to you across every novel, and is kept with **Save settings**.

### Settings: My craft shelf

**My craft shelf** lists the craft books and teachers you learn from. When you ask the collaborator where to learn more about something, it starts with your shelf, naming books by title and author only.

The shelf is a small table with three columns:

| Column | What goes in it |
| --- | --- |
| **Title** | The book's title. |
| **Author** | Who wrote it. |
| **What I use it for** | Your own note, such as "structure for mysteries" or "when a character feels flat." This is what tells the collaborator which book fits which problem. |

Each row ends with **Remove**. **Add a book** adds an empty row. A row with no title is dropped when you save.

**Example.** Add *Save the Cat! Writes a Novel*, Jessica Brody, "where the beats fall." Next time you ask where to learn more about a sagging middle, that book comes first.

### Settings: the link to My methods

A line, "Methods from any teacher or book, with their own beats, questions and fields," with the link **Open my methods**. See [My methods: making your own beat sheet](#my-methods-making-your-own-beat-sheet), later in this chapter.

### Settings: Stages a book can be in

A **stage** is where a whole book stands in its life. You choose it from a dropdown on the book's cover in the Library, and from the **Stage** menu on Where was I?. The box **Stages a book can be in** lists the choices, one per line: Idea, Plotting and planning, First draft, Second draft, Third draft, Revising, Editing, With beta readers (trusted early readers), With an editor, Querying (sending to literary agents), On submission (an agent is offering it to publishers), Published, Resting.

Add, rename, reorder or delete lines to make your own list, then press **Save settings**. Stages are labels only. They never lock, hide or change anything. How a stage differs from a draft is explained in [Drafts: keeping a whole version of the book](#drafts-keeping-a-whole-version-of-the-book).

## Settings: Appearance

Page **7 Appearance** has three choices, tagged **Takes effect right away**:

- **Match Windows** follows your Windows setting: when Windows is dark, Story Canon is too. This is the starting choice.
- **Light**: the purple cover and cream page, always.
- **Dark**: a deep eggplant cover and a dark page, always.

The choice belongs to this computer and applies to every novel. The status inks keep their meaning in both: Decided is still blue, Developing is still pencil gray.

## Your other computers: writing on two machines

You can write on two computers, such as a laptop at home and another at work, and keep your novels in step between them. Story Canon calls this **sharing**. It is set up in a section called **Your other computers**, which appears in two places: in **Settings** inside any novel, and in the **Library** (the screen listing all your novels), below **Your novels**. The two are the same switch for the whole computer; turning sharing on in one turns it on in the other.

**Do most writers need it?** Only if you write on more than one computer. If you use one computer, skip to [Settings: backups and archives](#settings-backups-and-archives).

### Sharing: how it works

Each computer keeps its own full copy of your novels, so each one works with no internet at all. Both use one folder that both computers can see, such as a folder in your OneDrive (Microsoft's online storage, which keeps a folder the same on every computer you sign in to).

- **Sending.** When a novel has changed, Story Canon puts a sealed copy of it in the shared folder, inside a folder called **Story Canon shared**. It does this about every five minutes while there are changes, and again when you close the program.
- **Receiving.** Each computer looks in the shared folder every minute while Story Canon is open, and when it starts.
- **Safety.** Neither computer ever writes into a file the other one made, and each copy is checked when it is read. A copy still arriving through OneDrive is simply tried again next time.

**Why it is built this way.** Programs that keep one shared file in OneDrive can end up with two computers changing the same file at once, and the file breaks. Story Canon never shares one file. It shares sealed copies and keeps track of which computer has seen which.

### Sharing: turning it on on your first computer

1. Open your novel, click **Settings**, and turn to page **8 Other computers and backups**, where **Your other computers** is (or use the Library's section of the same name).
2. In **Call this computer**, type a short name, such as *Laptop*. This is the name your other computer will show in its notices ("Your Laptop shared newer work…").
3. Click **Choose the shared folder** and pick a folder in your OneDrive, or any folder both computers can see.
4. A message says "Sharing is on. Your novels are in the shared folder now; install Story Canon on your other computer and choose the same folder there."

All your novels are copied into the shared folder at once.

### Sharing: setting up your second computer from the Library

A brand-new second computer has no novels yet, so you cannot open Settings inside one. That is why the Library has its own **Your other computers** section, and you set up the second computer there, with no novel open.

1. Install Story Canon on the second computer and open it. It opens on the Library.
2. Wait for OneDrive to finish bringing the shared folder onto this computer.
3. Scroll to **Your other computers**, below **Your novels**.
4. In **Call this computer**, type a different name, such as *Office PC*.
5. Click **Choose the shared folder** and pick **the same folder** you chose on the first computer (the folder you chose, not the **Story Canon shared** folder inside it).
6. A message says "Sharing is on. Novels your other computer has shared appear in this Library under On your other computers. Bring each one here once, and from then on it stays one book on both computers."
7. Just above **Your other computers**, a list headed **On your other computers** appears, with the line "Shared from another computer and not on this one yet. Bring one here and the two stay one book." Each novel shows its title, which computer shared it and when ("from Laptop, Tue 3:25 PM"), and **Bring to this computer**.
8. Click **Bring to this computer** beside a novel. It says *Bringing it…*, then the novel opens. Do this once for each novel.

From then on, the two computers keep each novel as one book.

**The common mistake** is getting the book onto the second computer some other way, such as starting it fresh or bringing in an archive. Each of those makes a second, separate book that never stays in step with the first. Turn on sharing in the Library first, and use **Bring to this computer**, which brings over the same book.

**Example.** You planned *The Keeper of Gull Point* on your home laptop. On the computer at work, you install Story Canon, name it *Office PC* in the Library, choose the same OneDrive folder, and click **Bring to this computer** beside *The Keeper of Gull Point*. It opens on its home page, with every chapter and record.

### Sharing: what the section shows once it is on

- "This computer is **Laptop**, sharing through" the folder.
- Inside a novel's Settings only: whether this novel has changes not shared yet ("They go out within a few minutes, and when you close Story Canon"), or when it was last shared, and when work last came in from another computer.
- **Also sharing:** each other computer and when it was last seen. Or, before the second computer joins: "No other computer has joined yet. Install Story Canon there and choose this same folder."
- The tick box **Ask me before bringing in work from my other computer** (next section).
- Inside a novel's Settings only: **Notes from the road** (see below).
- **Share now** sends this computer's changes at once. The message says "Shared." or "Nothing new to share; your other computer already has everything."
- **Change folder** picks a different shared folder.
- **Stop sharing** turns sharing off on this computer, after asking "Stop sharing from this computer? Nothing is deleted here or in the shared folder."

### Sharing: bringing in work from your other computer

When you open a novel and your other computer has shared newer work on it, a notice appears above the page: "Your Office PC shared newer work on this novel (Tue 1:10 PM). Bring it in before you write." with **Bring it in**. Click it. Story Canon makes a backup first, then brings the newer work in and shows it. If you were typing at that very moment, it says "You were typing, so nothing was changed. Try again in a moment."

The tick box **Ask me before bringing in work from my other computer** decides how much waits for you:

- **Ticked** (the starting choice): nothing changes until you click **Bring it in**.
- **Unticked**: newer work comes in on its own, after a backup, whenever there is nothing to choose. This happens when Story Canon starts, and in the background for novels you have not opened since starting it. A novel you have already opened in this session is never replaced under you; the notice still asks.

Either way, when both computers changed a novel, you always choose (next section).

If the novel is open on your other computer with changes it has not shared yet, a different notice says so: "This novel is open on your Office PC with changes it has not shared yet." If you write here too, Story Canon will ask you later which version to keep as the main one, and nothing is lost. Click **Write here anyway** to hide the notice and go on.

**Good habit:** close Story Canon on one computer before you start on the other. It is not required, but you will almost never be asked to choose.

### Sharing: when both computers changed a novel

Say you wrote at lunch on the work computer, and the laptop at home also had changes it had not shared. Story Canon will not pick for you. On the laptop, a notice says "This computer and your Office PC both changed this novel since they last shared," with two buttons:

- **Keep this computer's as the main version**
- **Make the Office PC's the main version**

Whichever you choose becomes the main novel on both computers. The other is not thrown away. It is kept in your Library beside the main one, on both computers, named for where it came from, such as "The Keeper of Gull Point (from Office PC, Oct 21, 1:10 PM)". A message tells you its exact name.

Afterward, open the kept version, copy anything you want from it into the main one, then remove the kept version from the shelf if you like.

If you already chose on the other computer, Story Canon says "You already made this choice on your other computer, so it stands." If something newer arrives while you are deciding, it says "Something newer just arrived from your other computer. Look again before choosing."

### Sharing: notes from the road

Once sharing is on, each novel gets its own notes file in your shared folder, inside a folder called **Story Canon notes**, named for the novel (for example, `Story Canon notes\The Keeper of Gull Point.docx`). In a novel's Settings, under **Your other computers**, the part headed **Notes from the road** shows exactly where the file is, with **Copy where it is** to copy that location.

**Why it helps.** Ideas arrive in the grocery line, not at the desk. This lets you dictate a thought into your phone and find it waiting in the novel.

**How to use it from your phone:**

1. Open the file from the OneDrive app in Word.
2. Tap the microphone and dictate. Start each note on a new line.
3. When Story Canon has that novel open on one of your computers, each new paragraph arrives as a captured thought, waiting under **Thoughts waiting to be sorted** on Where was I? (page **2 Still open**). A message says "A note from the road came in."

A few things to know:

- **Your file is never changed.** Leave old notes in it or delete them; either is fine. Each note comes in only once.
- **A note comes in about a minute after you stop,** once the file has sat still for a minute and synced. A note still being dictated arrives whole, not in pieces. Just after Story Canon starts, or after the computer wakes from sleep, it waits about three more minutes first, so newer work from your other computer can arrive before the notes.
- **The novel must be open** in Story Canon, on a computer that shares through that folder.
- **Only one computer brings each note in,** even with the novel open on both.
- If you change an old note in the file, the changed version comes in as a new thought.
- No Word on your phone? A plain text file with the same name (`The Keeper of Gull Point.txt`) works too.

**Example.** Walking past a harbor, you dictate: "Cyrus keeps the logbook in his coat, not the office." That evening you open the novel at home, and the line is waiting under **Thoughts waiting to be sorted**, ready to file on Cyrus Hale's record.

## Settings: backups and archives

A **backup** is a complete spare copy of all your novels, made so that a broken computer cannot cost you your book. An **archive** is one file holding a single novel and its whole history, made so you can store it or move it.

**Where:** **Settings**, page **8 Other computers and backups**, the section **Backups**, under **Your other computers**. Its heading is tagged **Takes effect right away**: its buttons work the moment you click them. The gray line under the heading says: "Backups are also made automatically while you work. An archive is one file with this whole novel and its history, which you can keep anywhere and bring back in from the Library."

### Backups: the automatic ones

Story Canon makes a backup by itself every thirty minutes while you are making changes, and again when you close the program. Each one is checked for damage as it is made; a copy that fails the check is not kept. The most recent twenty automatic backups are kept, and older ones are removed as new ones arrive. A backup is also made before work from your other computer is brought in.

The line at the top of the section says where they go, for example "Backups go to C:\Users\Dana\Documents\Story Canon\Backups, the usual place." Each backup is a single file named with the date and time it was made.

### Backups: Make a backup now

**Make a backup now** makes an extra copy this minute and checks it. A message says "Backup made and checked" with its size. Up to fifty of these are kept, apart from the automatic ones.

**When to use it:** before anything big, like bringing in a long document, deleting a part of the book, or starting a revision.

### Backups: choosing where they go

The one thing worth doing in this section: put your backups somewhere other than this computer. A backup on the same computer is lost along with it.

1. In **Settings**, on page **8 Other computers and backups**, under **Backups**, click **Choose a folder**.
2. Pick a folder on a second drive, a USB stick, or a synced folder such as OneDrive. (The faint line under the button says the same: "A second drive, a USB stick or a synced folder such as OneDrive keeps a copy safe even if this computer is not.")
3. Story Canon makes the first backup there at once and checks it. A message says "Backups now go to" the folder, then "The first one is already there, checked."

To go back to the starting folder, click **Back to the usual place**.

If the folder cannot be written to (a USB stick that was unplugged, say), you see "That folder cannot be written to. Choose another, or check that the drive is connected." Nothing changes until you pick one that works.

**The common mistake** is choosing a USB stick and then leaving it unplugged. Automatic backups need the folder to be there. A synced folder such as OneDrive is the easiest choice, because it is always connected.

### Backups: Save an archive of this novel

**Save an archive of this novel** saves just this one novel, with all its history, as a single file named with the title and today's date, such as `The Keeper of Gull Point archive 2026-10-21.json`. You choose where to save it. Keep it anywhere: email it to yourself, put it on a USB stick, move it to a new computer.

**When to use it:** before you lend your laptop to someone, when you buy a new computer and do not want sharing, or as a keepsake of a finished draft.

### Backups: getting a novel back from an archive

1. Click **Library** at the top left of any page.
2. Under **Bring in an archive**, choose the archive file.
3. Before anything is added, a box shows what the archive holds: the title, the counts of chapters, scenes and records, and any warnings.
4. Click **Import** when the novel is not already here. If a version of it is already on your shelf, the button is **Import as a separate copy**: the archive comes in beside your novel, with "(copy)" after its title, and your novel is not changed. If the very same novel is already on your shelf, the box says "This exact novel is already in your library. Nothing will be imported unless you import it as a copy." **Cancel** closes the box with nothing added.

Nothing already in your Library is ever overwritten by an archive.

**Example.** You are moving to a new laptop and do not want sharing. On the old one, you open *The Keeper of Gull Point*, go to **Settings**, page **8 Other computers and backups**, click **Save an archive of this novel**, and save it to a USB stick. On the new laptop, you open the Library, choose the file under **Bring in an archive**, check the counts, and click **Import**. The novel arrives with every scene, record and undo in its history.

**About the backup files.** The backup files themselves cannot be opened from inside Story Canon. They are the last line of defense if a computer fails completely, and they are best restored with help. For moving a novel, or keeping a copy you can open yourself, use an archive.

## My methods: making your own beat sheet

A **method** is a way of building a story taught by a particular writer, teacher or course. A **beat sheet** is a method's list of turning points (beats), in order, with roughly where each falls in the book. Story Canon comes with well-known ones, such as Save the Cat, The Hero's Journey, Story Circle, Seven-Point Structure and Three-Act Structure. **My methods** is where you add your own.

A method can be a full beat sheet, a lens with its own questions and fields, or both. Methods belong to you, not to one novel, so every novel can use them; each novel chooses its own in Novel setup.

**Do most writers need it?** No. Most writers use a built-in beat sheet. Use My methods when you learned a structure in a class or from a book that is not built in, or you adapted a known one to the way you actually work.

**Where:** **Settings**, page **6 All my novels**, under **For all my novels**, the link **Open my methods**. Or, in Novel setup, on page **5 My storytelling toolkit**, the button **Make a method of my own**. The **Settings** view stays lit in the list while you are there.

The **My methods** page lists your methods (or "None yet."). Each row shows the method's name (click it to edit), where it comes from, how many beats and fields it has (or "no beat sheet"), and "in this novel" if the open novel uses it. The **Novel setup** link at the top goes to Novel setup.

### My methods: making a method, step by step

1. Click **Make a method of my own**. A page headed **A method of my own** opens.
2. Fill in **Name**. It is the one box you must fill; the empty box says "Needed to save," and **Save this method** stays grayed out until it has a name. A line beside the button says "Give the method a name at the top to save it."
3. Fill in **Where it comes from (teacher, book, course)**, such as "Ms. Alvarez's plotting workshop."
4. Fill in **What it does for me**, in your own words.
5. Add beats, questions and fields if the method has them (the next sections).
6. Click **Save this method**. A message confirms it, such as "Five Walls is saved. Turn it on for any novel in Novel setup."

**Cancel** at the bottom, or **All my methods** at the top, leaves without saving.

**The common mistake** is copying a book's text into a method word for word. Describe it in your own words. It is more useful to you, and it respects the author.

### My methods: its beats, row by row

Under **Its beats**, two gray notes explain the section. Leave beats empty for a method that is a lens or a habit rather than a beat sheet. With beats, the method can drive the Board (where you place scene cards on beats in Narrative Zoom), placement and Narrative Zoom just like Save the Cat.

To start, click one of the **Start from** links: **A blank page**, **Save the Cat**, **The Hero's Journey**, **Story Circle**, **Seven-Point Structure**, **Three-Act Structure** or **McKee's Five Parts**. Starting from a known sheet copies its beats in for you to change. **A blank page** gives you one empty beat. **Where it comes from** is filled in for you if it was empty.

Each beat is a numbered row:

| Part | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Beat name** | What you call the beat, such as "The Lie Exposed." |
| **at** __ **%** **to** __ **%** | Where it falls in the book, as a percentage of the length. 50 means halfway. For a beat that is a single moment, put the same number in both. |
| The act dropdown | **Act 1**, **Act 2, first half**, **Act 2, second half** or **Act 3**. Decides which row of the Board it sits on. |
| **Lines up with… (optional)** | The shared moment this beat matches. See below. |
| **↑**, **↓**, **Remove** | Reorder or delete the beat. |
| **What this beat is and the job it does** | Your teaching for the beat. It shows on the beat's record when you plan. |
| **A test it must pass** (optional) | A question to check the beat against, such as "Has the hero chosen, or only reacted?" |

**Add a beat** adds an empty row at the end. A beat with no name is dropped when you save.

**Lines up with** names a moment that many beat sheets share under different names. The choices are:

- **The opening** (Opening Image, Ordinary World)
- **The inciting event** (Catalyst, Call to Adventure)
- **The hesitation** (Debate, Refusal)
- **Into Act Two** (Break into Two, First Threshold, Plot Point 1)
- **The new world** (Fun and Games, Tests and Allies)
- **The midpoint** (Midpoint, Ordeal, Find)
- **The lowest point** (All Is Lost, Pinch 2, Take)
- **Into Act Three** (Break into Three, Road Back, Plot Turn 2)
- **The climax** (Finale, Resurrection)
- **The ending** (Final Image, Return with the Elixir)

**Why it matters.** If you switch a novel from Save the Cat to your method, a scene placed on Save the Cat's Midpoint can find your method's midpoint beat, but only if that beat lines up with **The midpoint**. A beat with nothing chosen simply is not carried across, which is fine for a beat no other sheet has.

### My methods: questions it asks and fields it adds

**Questions it asks of a story**: one question per line, such as "What does the hero believe on page one that is false?" The collaborator and Novel setup ask them of any novel using this method.

**Fields it adds**: places to write what the method needs you to know. For each, choose which kind of record it goes on (**Each character**, **Each character arc**, **Each scene card**, **Each beat**, **The story world**, **Each relationship**, **Each place**, **Each storyline** or **Each set piece**), type a **Field name**, and if you like, **A prompt** that appears as the hint under the field. **Add a field** adds a row; **Remove** deletes one. In a novel using the method, these fields appear grouped under the method's name, on the record's pages.

### My methods: turning your method on for a novel

A method belongs to you, so every novel can use it, but each novel chooses its own.

1. Open the novel, then **Settings**, then **Open novel setup**.
2. On page **5 My storytelling toolkit**, tick your method. It carries a **yours** label and an **Edit** link that brings you back to My methods.
3. If the method has beats and the novel now has more than one beat sheet ticked, choose which one drives the Board under **The beat sheet that drives the board**. Tick **Offer my placed scenes on the matching beats** to carry scene placements across using **Lines up with**. They arrive as "might go here," waiting for you to decide.

Novel setup saves each choice as you make it. Novel setup itself is taught in [Your first hour: the Library and a new novel](#your-first-hour-the-library-and-a-new-novel).

**Changing a method later.** Edit it in **My methods** and save. If the open novel uses it, the message adds: "This novel has been updated to match; anything placed on its beats stays where it is."

**Example.** Your workshop teacher uses a five-beat shape called Five Walls: Wound, Want, Wall, Wreck, Win. Start from **A blank page** and add five beats at 0, 25, 50, 75 and 95 percent. Line Wall up with **The midpoint** and Wreck with **The lowest point**. Add a field on **Each character** called "The wound" with the prompt "What happened before page one?" Save, then tick Five Walls in Novel setup for *The Keeper of Gull Point*. Marisol's record now has a place to write about the night Eli drowned, and the Board shows your five beats.

## Drafts: keeping a whole version of the book

A **draft** is one complete pass through your book. Your first draft is the first full version; revising it from start to finish makes a second. When you finish one, Story Canon can keep an exact copy of the whole book as it stood, so any scene can be compared with it later.

**Where:** on **Where was I?**, next to the **Stage** menu, a line says **Draft:** and the current draft's name, with the link **Drafts**. Click it. (**The Writing Room** stays lit in the list of views while you are on the Drafts page.)

**Why it helps.** Revision is braver when the old version is one click away. You can cut a scene to the bone without saving a copy "just in case."

### Drafts: draft and stage are different

A line on the Drafts page explains it: a draft is a version of the pages; the stage on the book's cover is where the book as a whole stands. The two move separately. A book can be in "Plotting and planning" while its first draft is open, and every new book starts in its first draft. That is working as designed: it means you are planning, and when you write, it will be your first draft.

They help each other in two ways:

- **A book that arrives part way along.** If you set the stage to *Second draft* or *Third draft* before you have kept any draft here, the draft you are in takes that name too.
- **Finishing a draft.** When you keep a finished draft, a book whose stage is a draft name (or has no stage) moves to the next draft's name. A book whose stage is something else, such as *Revising*, keeps its stage.

### Drafts: keeping a draft, step by step

1. Open **Drafts**. Under **Now:** and the draft's name, a line says when it began.
2. Click **This draft is done**.
3. If you like, write **A note about this draft (optional)**, such as what this draft did and what the next one is for.
4. If you like, fill in **Name for the next draft (optional)**, such as "Second draft" or "The voice pass."
5. Click **Keep first draft and start the next** (the button names your current draft). **Not yet** cancels.

A message confirms it, for example: "First draft is kept, 82,400 words. You are now in the second draft." You keep writing in the same place. Nothing in your manuscript changes.

Under **Kept drafts**, each kept draft shows its name, when it was finished, its word count and number of scenes, your note, and **Compare with now**.

**The common mistake** is waiting for the draft to feel good enough to keep. Keep it when you reach the end, however rough. The point is to have it to compare with.

### Drafts: comparing a draft with now

Click **Compare with now** beside a kept draft. A section opens with a summary, such as "82,400 words then, 79,150 now. 31 scenes changed, 12 untouched, 2 new, 3 no longer in the book." Then up to three lists:

- **Changed**: each scene with its chapter and its word count then and now. Click a scene's name to see it side by side.
- **New since then**: click a scene to open it.
- **In that draft, not in the book now**: scenes you cut, still kept in that draft.

On a single scene's comparison, choose which kept draft to compare with under **Compare now with**. Struck text was in the older draft; highlighted text is new since. **Back to the comparison** returns.

To send out a kept draft instead of the current book, choose it when you export (see [Bringing things in and sending things out](#bringing-things-in-and-sending-things-out)).

**Example.** In your second draft of *The Keeper of Gull Point*, you rewrote the scene where Ana Ruiz first examines Marisol's cut hand. Compare with now shows it under **Changed**, 1,900 to 1,400 words. Click it, and you see the lines you cut struck through and Ana's new, shorter dialogue highlighted.

### Drafts: I finished the draft too soon

Under the kept drafts is a link, **I finished first draft too soon** (it names your latest kept draft). Click it. A notice asks whether to reopen it: its kept copy is released and you carry on in it, and your writing is not touched. Click **Reopen it**, or **Keep it finished** to cancel.
